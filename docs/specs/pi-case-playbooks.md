A. PLAYBOOKS
A1. MVC — Standard Auto

1. Challenge / watch-out

"I accept responsibility" admissions are NOT negligence as a matter of law — don't build liability on the admission alone (Douglas v. Aguilar, 599 S.W.3d 105 (Tex. App.—Houston [14th Dist.] 2020)). Prompt: corroborating liability evidence checklist.
Sudden mechanical failure (tire blowout) is not itself evidence of negligence (Lozada v. Posada, 2025 WL 1717009 (Tex. 2025)) — if the defense story is component failure, auto-suggest the Products Overlay (A3) against the maintenance chain instead.
Cell-phone/distraction discovery requires a pleaded predicate: allege or show some evidence of use at a time it could have contributed, or expanded phone/telematics data will be denied (In re Kuraray Am., 656 S.W.3d 137 (Tex. 2022); In re Padilla (Austin 2018)). Prompt at pleading stage: "distraction pleaded? company-policy allegations pleaded?"
Defendant facing parallel criminal charges may take the Fifth as to pre-crash bars/drinking (In re Peters, 699 S.W.3d 307 (Tex. 2024)) — dram-shop leads must be developed through third parties (receipts, bar witnesses) while the 2-year dram-shop clock runs; Texas juries may draw the negative inference (Wilz v. Flournoy).
Dram-shop screen: viability now requires objective evidence of intoxication apparent at the time of service (Raoger Corp. v. Myers, 711 S.W.3d 206 (Tex. 2025)); high BAC hours later is speculation.
Pre-existing conditions: expect defense discovery of 5 years pre / 5 years post medical records as the customary reasonable scope (In re Liberty Cnty. Mut., 679 S.W.3d 170 (Tex. 2023); In re Austin Maintenance (Houston [1st] 2024) — courts CAN compel signed tailored authorizations).
Screens that reroute the file: government-owned vehicle → A6 (TTCA notice clocks start immediately); commercial vehicle → A2; death → A10; low policy limits / hit-and-run / carrier insolvency → A9 (UM/UIM) and Spotter D (Stowers).
Mental-anguish intake counseling: garden-variety anguish keeps mental-health records privileged; a DSM diagnosis, psych treatment, or designating a treater as an expert opens records and a Rule 204 exam (In re Barnes; In re Richardson Motorsports, 690 S.W.3d 42 (Tex. 2024)). See Spotter C.

2. Discovery to demand

All policies and indemnity agreements at the outset (TRCP 194.2(g); Rule 192.3(f) limits to existence/contents — In re Dana Corp.).
Specific RFP for surveillance/photos/video of plaintiff — the impeachment carve-out does not defeat a specific Rule 196 request (Rule 192.5(c)(4)); withholding is sanctionable and sanctions motions are TCPA-proof (Ferchichi v. Whataburger, 2025 WL 1350005 (Tex. 2025)).
EDR/ACM data from all vehicles (spoliation-safe download by qualified tech — Spotter A); scene photos; 911/dispatch; FOIA/PIA packet to every responding agency (reports, field notes, photos, dash video) — TPIA, Tex. Gov't Code ch. 552 (any person may request, no purpose statement).
Defendant's driving history, criminal history, phone records (post-predicate), prior-collision history.
UIM angle: corporate-rep deposition of the UM/UIM carrier on its liability/damages contentions is allowed even pre-entitlement (In re USAA Gen. Indem., 624 S.W.3d 782 (Tex. 2021)).

3. Deadlines to calendar

2-year tort SOL — CPRC § 16.003(a). Intake auto-calculates; 6-months-out alarm triggers forum/discovery-rule review (Slack pre-suit framework).
Notice letters to all carriers: firm-standard 24-hour target (lifecycle phase gate, not statutory).
Initial disclosures: 30 days after first answer — TRCP 194.2(a) (incl. (b)(10) all medical records and bills or authorization).
Expert designations: 90 days before end of discovery period (party seeking affirmative relief) / 60 days (all others) — TRCP 195.2, unless DCO overrides. ⚠ NOTE: the course book (ch. 13) states these inverted (60/90); the rule text is 90/60 — verified against TRCP 195.2; keep Part III row E1's conflict flag.
§ 18.001 billing-affidavit service and counter-affidavit clocks per current statute/DCO [CONFIRM current § 18.001(d)–(e) service periods; SB 30 (which would have rewritten § 18.001) FAILED in 2025 but returns in 2027 — build as a toggle]. Watch item: Ortiz v. Nelapatla [23-0953] (SCOTX pending, partially controverted affidavits). Anticipate nurse-reviewer counter-affidavits (In re Allstate Indem., 622 S.W.3d 870 (Tex. 2021)).
MSJ clocks (new): hearing within 45 days after response filed; written ruling within 90 days after submission — SB 293 (eff. Sept. 1, 2025). Calendar from "MSJ response filed."
E-filing rejection protection: capture rejection + immediate refile timestamps (Whitelock v. Stewart (El Paso 2023)).

4. Proof / damages theories + defenses to anticipate

Liability: negligence; negligence per se; negligent entrustment against owner.
Damages proof runs Spotter C (Chohan). Evidence-tethered per diem argument affirmed post-Chohan (Garza v. Escamilla, 2025 WL 677013 (Tex. App.—Houston [14th Dist.] 2025) — $1/waking hour). Lost earning capacity in NET after-tax form — CPRC § 18.091. Future medicals: reasonable-probability standard; no expert prerequisite (Strahan v. Davis).
Anticipated defenses: comparative responsibility (Ch. 33) and RTP designations (objection due by the 15th day after service of the designation motion [CONFIRM — CPRC § 33.004(f)]); sudden emergency; unavoidable accident; pre-existing condition; failure to mitigate; seatbelt nonuse; LOP/referral-source attack themes (SB 30's disclosure agenda survives as defense discovery strategy; attorney-referral testimony featured in In re SpaceX (Tex. 2025)).

5. Data-model fields/flags

Vehicle records (per vehicle): make/model/year, VIN, EDR present/downloaded (by whom/date/protocol), custodian/location, salvage-yard contact date, preservation-letter sent date.
Flags: commercial_vehicle, government_vehicle, product_suspected, intoxication_suspected (auto-runs dram-shop sub-checklist + Spotter B felony auto-flag), parallel_criminal_case, hit_and_run, distraction_pleaded, um_uim_open.
Policy register: every policy canvassed (defendant liability layers, plaintiff UM/UIM, PIP, MedPay per household) with limits, written-UM-rejection-on-file flag.
Damages module: per-element segregated fields (Spotter C); § 18.001 affidavit tracker (served/controverted/reviewer identity).
A2. MVC — Commercial / Trucking Overlay (loads on top of A1)

1. Challenge / watch-out

CPRC ch. 72 bifurcation regime (HB 19, 2021). On any CMV defendant's motion the court SHALL bifurcate (§ 72.052(a)); phase 1 = liability + compensatory only; phase 2 = liability for AND amount of exemplaries (§ 72.052(c)–(d)) — note this phase split DIFFERS from § 41.009 (where exemplary liability sits in phase 1); the charge/workflow must branch. Employer's timely course-and-scope stipulation (§ 72.054(a)) confines phase 1 to respondeat superior and bars derivative ordinary-negligence claims (entrustment/hiring) in phase 1 (§ 72.054(b)), EXCEPT: (i) § 72.053(b) regulatory-violation evidence (specific regulation governing the duty + proximate-cause link); (ii) the § 72.054(c)–(d) statutory evidence list for negligent entrustment (CDL status, disqualification, out-of-service orders, medical cert, HOS violations, texting/handheld at time of collision, employment application if crash ≤1 year of hire, drug-test refusals within 2 years, employer § 391.23 background checks if ≤1 year, drug-testing compliance if driver impaired and crash ≤180 days of hire); (iii) § 72.054(f) carve-out — independent claims like negligent maintenance stay in phase 1, and Ch. 41 exemplary claims for the employer's own conduct survive (Patterson/Estate of Arrington rule). Driver gross negligence also goes to phase 2 (In re Southwest Motor Transport (Houston [1st] Dec. 19, 2024)). SB 39 (would have repealed § 72.054(c)–(e)) FAILED in 2025 — regime unchanged; watch 2027.
Werner v. Blake decided (2025 WL 1774169, Tex. June 27, 2025): proximate cause requires but-for AND substantial-factor; trucker lawfully in his lane when a pickup crossed the median was "too attenuated" — judgment rendered for the carrier. Court declined to reach the "Admission Rule" (held it "need not consider" it), but Justice Young's concurrence signals it's coming — treat derivative-negligence pleading strategy as unstable; keep watch-item alert.
Federal forum flips the analysis: § 72.052 is procedural and yields to FRCP 42(b) (Trejo-Munoz, 615 F. Supp. 3d 558); in federal court expect stipulation + MSJ to kill derivative claims — plead independent direct-negligence theories (maintenance — Bedford v. Moore) and a prima facie exemplary case.
Broker-liability screen. Map the load chain (shipper → broker → carrier → driver). Evaluate: (i) "hidden motor carrier" facts (broker holds itself out / controls transport — 49 U.S.C. § 13102(2); 49 C.F.R. § 371.2(a)); (ii) negligent selection/hiring against the broker; (iii) FAAAA preemption forum analysis (49 U.S.C. § 14501(c)(1)): all three circuits to reach it find negligent-hiring claims within the preemption clause; the § 14501(c)(2)(A) safety exception saves them in the 9th Cir. (Miller v. C.H. Robinson) but not the 7th (Ye) or 11th (Aspen); the 5th Cir. has NOT addressed it and Texas district courts split (Lopez v. Amazon — saved; Gillum, Popal, Hamby, Patterson v. Hunt Perfection (S.D. Tex. 2025) — preempted); SCOTUS denied cert (Gauthier, Jan. 13, 2025). Forum selection is outcome-determinative; anticipate late FAAAA removal gambits — plead to avoid a removal hook.
Casteel trap: don't submit the employer twice (vicarious + direct) in one broad-form question.
Nuclear-verdict awareness (defense mirrors this): anchoring discipline per Spotter C; wealth references and abandoned-exemplary-claim argument reversed a nine-figure verdict (Team Indus. Servs. v. Most (Houston [1st] 2024)); expect reverse-reptile framing, counter-anchoring from voir dire, daily corporate-rep presence.
Driver-side note: FAA § 1 exempts transportation workers from employment arbitration clauses (MVT Servs. v. Robles) — relevant when the client is a trucker suing a motor-carrier employer (cross-ref A5).

2. Discovery to demand

Telematics/ELD/video set (day-one preservation, Spotter A; then discovery): ELD/HOS data (carrier may not alter or erase — 49 C.F.R. § 395.30(f)); ECM/black box (records ~5 seconds pre/post hard-braking events and can be OVERWRITTEN by later events — download immediately); dash-cam (inward/outward; cloud vs. local); GPS; AI video-telematics harsh-event logs, driver safety scores, coaching records (real-time-notice capability = negligent-supervision gold; five prior Lytx events survived SJ on supervision/entrustment, Lewis v. Medford (N.D. Ga. 2025)).
The carrier's service contract with its telematics provider — identifies the plan in force, what data exists, and whether the carrier has portal credentials; then SUBPOENA the provider directly (Samsara, Motive, Lytx, Verizon Connect, Bendix, Geotab, PeopleNet, Qualcomm…) — the provider will have more than the defense produces. Authentication path: records-custodian certification from the PROVIDER, or route through the reconstructionist (Magee v. Noe — carrier table with disclaimer not self-authenticating; expert reliance survives).
Temporal-scope fight: defense offers ~3 minutes of video; demand ~30 days of driving-behavior data to show a pattern the carrier failed to correct.
Driver qualification file, drug/alcohol testing, HOS logs + supporting documents, maintenance records (§ 72.054(f) negligent-maintenance lane), broker-carrier agreement, carrier-vetting file and safety scores (broker cases).
Company-wide OSI discovery requires company-wide policy/practice pleading (In re Reyna/Reyes (Corpus Christi 2024)); untailored nationwide claims requests get cut (In re Contract Freighters, 646 S.W.3d 810 (Tex. 2022); In re UPS Ground Freight, 646 S.W.3d 828 (Tex. 2022)).
Infotainment/BERLA iVe examination of both vehicles (paired-phone data, speed/event logs) — an onboard factory telematics device is "part of the vehicle" subject to inspection (Rule 196.7; Mollaei v. Otonomo (9th Cir. 2025) analog).

3. Deadlines to calendar

§ 72.052(b) bifurcation/stipulation clock, per defendant: motion due on or before the LATER of (a) the 120th day after that defendant's original answer, or (b) the 30th day after a claimant files a pleading adding a claim against that defendant. Engine tracks per-defendant; an amended pleading restarts the 30-day limb. Missed deadline = defense loses the election (plaintiff-side monitoring value: know when the window closes).
Provider data-retention windows (per telematics vendor plan) [CONFIRM per-provider retention; ELD supporting-document retention is 6 months under 49 C.F.R. § 395.8(k) [CONFIRM]] — preservation letter must beat these.
All A1 deadlines; Spotter B deadlines if exemplaries pleaded (§ 41.009 vs. § 72.052 branch).

4. Proof / damages theories + defenses to anticipate

Theories: respondeat superior (post-stipulation); negligent entrustment via the § 72.054(c)–(d) evidence list; negligent maintenance (independent, phase 1); gross negligence vs. carrier for its own acts (phase 2; vice-principal analysis per Spotter B); broker negligent selection ± hidden-motor-carrier; HOS-violation/fatigue (also supports objective gross-negligence prong — Rayner v. Dillon, 501 S.W.3d 143).
Defenses: course-and-scope stipulation strategy; § 1445(c) doesn't apply (that's nonsubscriber); removal on diversity; FAAAA preemption (brokers); sudden emergency; Werner-style attenuated-causation attack; comparative fault of third drivers; independent-contractor characterization (but "employee" includes FMCSR statutory employees, 49 C.F.R. § 390.5 — § 72.051; telematics monitoring itself evidences employee status, Brant v. Schneider).

5. Data-model fields/flags

Per-defendant: answer date → auto-computed § 72.052(b) deadline; stipulation_filed (date), bifurcation_motion_filed/granted, trial-phase structure (unified / § 41.009 / § 72.052).
CMV compliance checklist keyed to § 72.054(c)–(d) items (CDL, OOS orders, medical cert, HOS, texting, hire date vs. crash date computations for the 1-year/180-day/2-year windows).
Telematics registry: provider(s), plan, service-contract obtained, portal credentials, subpoena status, black-box download status (overwrite-risk tickler), video inventory, harsh-event/safety-score log.
Broker module: load-chain party map, broker-carrier contract, vetting file, FAAAA motion pending, forum/circuit field, hidden-motor-carrier evidence list.
Watch-item subscriptions: Admission Rule adoption; SB 39 revival (2027); FAAAA cert grants.
A3. Products-Liability Overlay for Auto Cases ("Is your crash a products case?")

1. Challenge / watch-out

Run the screen on every serious-injury/death MVC intake (Bright 8-step protocol). Trigger heuristics the engine should surface: occupant ejected (full/partial) → glazing/latch/curtain/retractor; injuries disproportionate to crash severity → aggressive airbag / seatback; rollover → SSF/handling + roof crush + containment systems (and verify pretensioners/side-curtains actually fired — shared rollover sensors fail together); fire → fuel-fed vs. engine origin; "unbelted" per police report → belt-failure analysis FIRST (false latching, inertial unlatching, spool-out leave physical signatures; do not decline for "no seatbelt" without inspecting the restraints, medical records, witness marks); tread on roadway / banging in wheel well → tire delamination; parked-vehicle movement → rollaway family (park-to-reverse, BSI, keyless ignition, e-shifter); child injured → car seat / power window; loud ADAS-equipped vehicle → both "system failed" and "failed to equip available tech" theories. Full 13-category defect catalog stored as checkbox taxonomy.
Attorney-liability trap: agreeing to investigate a potential product claim creates a duty to take preservation measures; failure = spoliation exposure against the LAWYER even if the case is later declined. Day-one tasks fire on product_suspected, before sign-up decisions.
Severity/cost gate: stand-alone product litigation costs $200,000–$500,000+; injuries must generally be catastrophic. Required workflow step: written client cost disclosure BEFORE filing; expert-packet review before declining.
Post-Milburn § 82.008 reality: compliance with a mandatory federal standard gives a rebuttable nonliability presumption that now goes to the JURY, with adequacy decided by preponderance (Am. Honda v. Milburn, 696 S.W.3d 612 (Tex. 2024)). Applicability remains a question of law; the standard must govern the narrowly-defined "product risk" (Kim v. Honda, 86 F.4th 150 (5th Cir. 2023); Kia v. Ruiz).
Non-manufacturing sellers shielded by § 82.003; marketplace platforms that never take title are not "sellers" (Amazon v. McMillan, 625 S.W.3d 101 (Tex. 2021)) — party-screening rule.
Personal jurisdiction is fragile for foreign component makers: "a stream, not a dribble" (BRP-Rotax v. Shaik, 2025 WL 1727903 (Tex. June 20, 2025), reversing the COA; Hyundam v. Swacina) — pre-suit jurisdictional workup per Slack; jurisdictional discovery may overlap merits (In re Christianson).

2. Discovery to demand

Preservation first (Spotter A): subject vehicle + ALL vehicles (bullet vehicle needed for Delta-V), tires and every tread piece, indoor climate-controlled storage (tarps insufficient — corrosion destroys metallurgical proof), TRO if adjuster won't cooperate (enjoin insurers, tow lots, salvage buyers, automakers; reserve inspection/purchase/relocation rights).
NHTSA complaints/recalls/TSBs by make-model-year and VIN; Safety Research & Strategies database; VIN history; tire DOT numbers (plant/week/year → age).
§ 82.008 rebuttal set (Milburn's 13 topics) once the presumption is asserted: NHTSA's failure to consider the failure mode, cost-only reasoning, ignored red flags, subsequent developments/technology changes, other incidents involving the same component type — including complaints/lawsuits/injuries for OTHER products using the same component (a major expansion past "substantially similar"); plus whether the defendant reported the CLIENT'S OWN claim to the government (§ 82.008(b)(2) withholding hook).
Safer-alternative-design discovery reaches other models sharing the technology (In re Deere; Nissan v. Armstrong), limited by substantial similarity (In re Michelin).
OSI evidence: "reasonably similar circumstances" (Uniroyal; Kia v. Ruiz).
Belt-use evidence 5-source checklist (witnesses; medical records/autopsy/photos; physical restraint marks — load marks, web-grabber indentions, transfer marks; witness marks; police reports). Initial medical packet incl. EMS/flight records for ALL occupants; autopsy set for all decedents.

3. Deadlines to calendar

15-year statute of repose, per defendant, from THAT defendant's date of sale — CPRC § 16.012(b). "Sale" completes at physical delivery (manufacturer → dealer title transfer), typically well before consumer purchase; defendant need not prove the exact date if the sale necessarily fell outside the window (Ford v. Parks, 691 S.W.3d 475 (Tex. 2024)). Intake auto-computes vehicle age from model year and flags repose risk; separate clocks per component defendant.
2-year SOL (§ 16.003) unchanged; scene-evidence and salvage-yard tasks are hour-scale, not deadline-scale (Spotter A).
Multi-state screen: other states' repose statutes vary (trigger, period, exceptions) — choice-of-law task when product or sale is out of state.

4. Proof / damages theories + defenses to anticipate

Theories: design defect (§ 82.005 — safer alternative design + producing cause; prototype not required, math/physics suffice — Casey v. Toyota; GM v. Sanchez); manufacturing defect (Ledesma; PJC 71.3); marketing defect (5 Sims elements); negligence; breach of warranty vs. retailer (also the remand tool — plead retailer warranty claims fully in the state petition; leave to amend post-removal, Palmquist v. Hain Celestial, 103 F.4th 294 (5th Cir. 2024)); civil RICO where fraud-tinged product marketing caused business/property losses (treble damages — Medical Marijuana v. Horn, 145 S. Ct. ___, 2025 WL 978102 (U.S. Apr. 2, 2025)).
Expert architecture: defect expert must identify a SPECIFIC defect and rule out other plausible causes (Guijarro v. Enter. Holdings, 39 F.4th 309 (5th Cir. 2022)); tire cases are chemistry-heavy and expert-only; post-Milburn add a regulatory expert (or prep the defect expert) for the NHTSA-adequacy rebuttal topics; formal risk-utility analysis not required (Kim).
Jury-charge preservation script (post-Milburn): object that § 82.008 adequacy is a question of law; object to the preponderance instruction / tender "any evidence" standard; tender alternative instructions and get written rulings — expect overruling; preserves appeal.
Defenses: § 82.008 presumption; repose; § 82.003 seller protections; misuse/alteration; crash-too-severe (rebut via reconstruction); "diving" defense in roof-crush (Malibu I/II testing rebuts); preemption (FMVSS compliance does not preempt most auto design claims — savings clause, 49 U.S.C. § 30103(e); Williamson v. Mazda).

5. Data-model fields/flags

Products screen sub-form on every MVC: 13-category defect checkboxes; ejection status; rollover parameters (SSF, roll count, roof-crush measurements); airbag generation; ADAS equipment list with standard/optional status by model year; shifter type; keyless ignition; tire records per tire (DOT number → age, tread inventory, storage location, X-ray done, installer identity).
Per-defendant repose clock (sale date, source of proof); seller-chain entities with title-holding status (marketplace_no_title flag).
§ 82.008 module: standard(s) invoked, narrow product-risk statement (pleading field), 13-topic discovery tracker, government-reporting-of-client-claim status.
Cost module: litigation budget estimate vs. damages; client cost-disclosure document (gate before filing).
Preservation: letter recipients/dates, storage facility (climate-controlled flag), TRO status, EDR download protocol record.
A4. Premises Liability

1. Challenge / watch-out

Theory election is reversible error territory: negligent activity (contemporaneous conduct) vs. premises condition (nonfeasance) require different submissions; trying the wrong theory or omitting premises elements = reversal (Clayton W. Williams v. Olivo, 952 S.W.2d 523; Keetch v. Kroger; Timberwalk; Del Lago). The playbook forces an early theory-election field (can plead both — Donaldson v. Pro Craft footnote — but the charge must be right).
Entrant-status framework drives duty: invitee (reasonable care re conditions owner knew or should have known), licensee (no willful/wanton/gross negligence + warn of known dangers), trespasser. Status field required at intake.
Chapter 95 screen (contractor-injury cases): if the claimant was constructing/repairing/renovating/modifying an improvement and the injury arises from the condition/use of THAT improvement, plaintiff must prove BOTH retained/exercised control AND the owner's ACTUAL knowledge of the danger (CPRC §§ 95.002–.003) — covering owner's own negligent activity too (Abutahoun); "same improvement" analysis (Energen); conditions in close proximity count (Los Compadres); your own pleadings can establish applicability as judicial admissions (Weekley Homes v. Paniagua, 691 S.W.3d 911 (Tex. 2024)). Intake prompts: what was the client hired to work on; who controlled means/methods; what did the owner actually know.
Open-and-obvious kills the warning duty (SandRidge v. Barfield) — but screen for the necessary-use exception (invitee must use the premises despite awareness; Austin v. Kroger; applied in JMI Contractors v. Medellin), and concealment (box hidden between displays not open-and-obvious, Bergevin v. O'Reilly).
Common/ordinary conditions need aggravating evidence: prior accidents, complaints, reports, regulatory noncompliance making the condition "measurably more likely" to injure (Pay & Save v. Canales, 691 S.W.3d 499 (Tex. 2024) — $6M wiped).
Seward public-safety-officer rule (intake bar): premises owners owe NO duty to responding police/firefighters injured by the risk that occasioned the response (Seward v. Santander, 2025 WL 1350133 (Tex. May 9, 2025)); auto-flag when client is a responding officer (residual duties — warn of known hidden dangers — survive).
Governmental premises → TTCA variant (A6): premises defects impose licensee-standard duty on the government; recreational use statute drops it to gross negligence (watch City of San Antonio v. Realme [24-0864], pending: is an organized 5K "recreation"?) [CONFIRM TTCA premises-duty details — sourced from statute, not notes].
Recreational-facility releases: arbitration + delegation clauses send even "does the old release cover this visit?" scope fights to the arbitrator (Cerna v. Pearland Urban Air (Tex. May 23, 2025)) — capture ALL releases ever signed at the facility. Pre-injury releases of gross negligence are void in most districts but enforceable in Dallas with express fair notice (Quiroz v. Jumpstreet8; contra Van Voris) — see Spotter B.

2. Discovery to demand

Actual/constructive knowledge evidence: sweep/inspection logs, prior incident reports, complaints, maintenance/repair history (roof-leak pattern satisfied constructive knowledge, Peterson v. HEB), employee statements showing pre-knowledge of the specific condition (Daniels v. Allsup's).
Surveillance: what cameras existed, whether recording, when footage stops, retention policies, operator deposition — all discoverable when pleaded (In re Reyes (Corpus Christi 2024)); specific RFP for footage of the incident and preservation demand day one (Spotter A).
Company-wide policy discovery requires company-wide pleading (In re Reyna/Reyes; In re HEB Grocery motorized-cart line).
Ch. 95 / construction: contracts across the party chain (owner–GC–sub–sub-of-sub), retained-control evidence (site supervision, safety personnel, instructions — JMI), safety meetings, OSHA citations (no negligence per se, but awareness evidence — JMI), preconstruction meeting records.
Negligent-security cases: crime-grid/Timberwalk foreseeability data, prior crimes on/near premises, security staffing and policy-vs-practice gap (policy not enforced = breach, Flashdancer v. Fulcher).

3. Deadlines to calendar

2-year SOL — CPRC § 16.003. TTCA notice clocks if governmental (A6). Standard litigation clocks per A1 (disclosures, experts, MSJ 45/90).
Surveillance-retention window: many systems overwrite in days — preservation letter is an hour-scale task, not a deadline (Spotter A).

4. Proof / damages theories + defenses to anticipate

Theories: premises condition (invitee elements: condition posing unreasonable risk; actual/constructive knowledge — proximity/conspicuity/longevity, Wal-Mart v. Reece; failure to make safe/warn; causation); negligent activity; negligent undertaking (Restatement § 324A — parent/affiliate defendants need SPECIFIC control over the injury-causing safety function, Lippert Components v. Williams); negligent security (Timberwalk/Del Lago); gross negligence where owner created/knew (Spotter B).
Defenses: no duty (open-and-obvious; no knowledge; Hillis v. McCall actual-notice rule for natural hazards); Ch. 95; comparative fault; independent-contractor/no-control (JLB Builders); "not unreasonably dangerous as a matter of law" (Canales); Seward bar; arbitration via release.

5. Data-model fields/flags

Entrant status (invitee/licensee/trespasser); theory election (condition/activity/both); condition description + longevity evidence log.
chapter_95 flag with sub-fields: improvement identity, work hired to perform, control evidence, actual-knowledge evidence.

A5. Non-Subscriber Workplace Injury

1. Challenge / watch-out

Verify subscriber status FIRST, and search the RIGHT entities. TDI coverage searches must run all parent/affiliate names; administrative defects (unregistered carrier, unpaid franchise tax) do NOT defeat the exclusive-remedy bar, and receipt of comp benefits proves coverage (Lane v. Odle (Fort Worth 2024) — plaintiff received $92k benefits, suit dead). Intake fields: benefits received? amounts? dates?
Election-of-remedies trap: accepting comp benefits WHILE REPRESENTED BY COUNSEL can be an "informed election" barring later suit (Medrano v. Kerry Ingredients, applying Medina/Bocanegra). Mere receipt alone isn't automatic election — but the system should hard-flag "client receiving benefits + engagement letter signed."
Staffing-agency screen: borrowed-employee doctrine can make the client company the comp-covered employer despite contrary contract language (Waste Mgmt. v. Stevenson (Tex. 2021)); TES + alternate-employer endorsement protects the client company (Waeli v. BWFS; Lab. Code §§ 93.004(b), 406.034, 408.001).
§ 406.033 framework (the case's engine): employer loses contributory negligence, assumption of risk, and fellow-servant defenses (§ 406.033(a); Kroger v. Keng — comparative responsibility CANNOT be submitted); remaining defenses: employee intent to injure and employee intoxication (§ 406.033(c)); plaintiff must still prove employer negligence (§ 406.033(d)) — 1% employer fault = 100% damages.
BUT the duty element is where the defense fights: no duty to warn of commonly known/appreciated dangers (Elwood; Skiles; Goss); same-character-of-work doctrine. The counter is the necessary-instrumentalities theory — nondelegable, continuous duty to furnish safe and necessary equipment survives even known/obvious hazards (Austin v. Kroger, 465 S.W.3d 193; Zeferino; Yoakum v. Tyson (N.D. Tex. May 21, 2025)); Allsup's/Warren factors (employee requested the instrumentality; complained; prior safe performance; industry-standard equipment; prudent-employer; medical evidence) — build these into the intake interview.
Arbitration screening at intake: injury-benefit-plan existence, plan tier elected, signed agreement, delegation clause (validity goes to the arbitrator — In re Builders FirstSource), covered parties ("clients of the company" binds non-signatories like Amazon — De La Victoria; Keele), judicial-admission-of-signing authenticates, illiteracy/Spanish-version arguments fail (Brackenridge v. Camero), waiver bar is high (6.5 months + written discovery insufficient). Escape routes: non-signatory defendants keep a court forum (Vibra Rehab); FAA § 1 transportation-worker exemption for truck drivers (MVT v. Robles); cost-based unconscionability needs detailed comparative cost evidence (Shattenkirk).
RTP designation per In re ETMC Athens (712 S.W.3d 88, Tex. Apr. 25, 2025): nonsubscriber employers CAN designate non-fellow-employee third parties as RTPs (Ch. 33 applies — these are "damages" claims, not benefits claims); the defendant needs only sufficient evidence, not a Ch. 74 expert report; erroneously striking a designation = mandamus. Plaintiff-side workflow: preserve claims against third parties yourself (mind Ch. 74 expert-report clocks when the third party is a healthcare provider — the ETMC plaintiff's dismissed EMT claims are the cautionary tale), and calendar RTP objection windows.
Removal: district courts split on whether § 406.033 claims "arise under" workers' comp laws for 28 U.S.C. § 1445(c) nonremovability (Wagnon v. Kroger (N.D. Tex. 2025) — remand; Canine v. Sam's East (S.D. Tex. 2024) — removal OK). Plead § 1445(c) nonremovability; know the district.

2. Discovery to demand

Employment-status set: TWC 20-factor and Limestone 5-factor evidence (control, tools, payment method, right to discharge); staffing contracts + alternate-employer endorsements; comp policy documents and TDI filings.
Instrumentalities set: equipment inventory/availability, maintenance, purchase records for the safety equipment NOT provided; industry-standard practice; the client's requests/complaints (emails, safety-meeting minutes); comparable equipment at other sites/trucks (Flores — other trucks had steps).
Gross-negligence set (Spotter B): org chart, hire/fire authority, department heads (vice-principal identification — Waste Mgmt. v. Dodd: title not dispositive), safety-policy vs. practice gaps tied to a vice principal's actual awareness, prior similar injuries known to supervisors (Yoakum — co-worker/supervisor depositions carried the subjective prong).

Injury-benefit-plan terms (fee-shifting provisions — In re Murillo's $17k fee award warning).

2-year SOL — CPRC § 16.003.
Removal/remand: motion to remand for § 1445(c) defect within 30 days of removal — 28 U.S.C. § 1447(c).

Ch. 74 expert-report clock (120 days after each healthcare defendant's answer — § 74.351(a)) when any third-party target is a provider [CONFIRM period; the notes flag the trap without the number]; PLPA/Ch. 148 pandemic claims carry the same report mechanics + one 30-day cure window (Gonzalez v. C.S. Auto).
Arbitration-motion response windows per court order; Spotter B clocks if exemplaries pleaded.

4. Proof / damages theories + defenses to anticipate

Theories (plead in parallel): failure to provide necessary instrumentalities/safe equipment (strongest post-Austin); failure to provide adequate assistance (must have REQUESTED help for the specific task — Yoakum; unusually-precarious situations, Mejia v. Home Depot); failure to train (weak where dangers are common knowledge); premises theory where employer controls the site; negligent hiring/supervision; gross negligence for exemplaries (employer created the danger — Flashdancer; § 408.001 death cases: exemplary-only claim against SUBSCRIBER employers, which is fully TAXABLE — route to settlement-tax planning before release).

No comparative-responsibility submission against the employee (Keng) — charge-drafting note.

A6. Texas Tort Claims Act (Governmental Defendants)

1. Challenge / watch-out

Everything is jurisdictional. Notice, limitations, AND service diligence: in suits against governmental entities the SOL — including timely, diligent SERVICE — is a statutory prerequisite to suit and jurisdictional (Tex. Gov't Code § 311.034; Tex. State Univ. v. Tanner, 689 S.W.3d 292 (Tex. 2024) — service 3.5 years post-limitations killed the case). The engine must track service date, not just filing date, on every governmental defendant, with escalating diligence prompts.
Emergency-responder counter-workflow (Sauls/Wagner/Powell line): official-immunity good faith is an OBJECTIVE need-vs-risk test; an officer's (or employer's) affidavit describing the emergency establishes good faith unless CONTROVERTED with evidence — argument is not enough (City of Houston v. Sauls, 690 S.W.3d 60 (Tex. 2024) — 22 mph over, no lights/siren, still immune; City of Mesquite v. Wagner, 712 S.W.3d 609 (Tex. 2025) — uncontroverted affidavit carried the plea). Under the TTCA emergency exception the court FIRST asks statutory/ordinance compliance, and only then recklessness — which requires conscious disregard of a high risk; inattention/following too closely is ordinary negligence and immunity holds (City of Austin v. Powell, 704 S.W.3d 437 (Tex. 2024); Gomez; Terry — also flags the separate 9-1-1 exception). Auto-task on every emergency-response case: retain a police-practices expert and build the controverting need/risk affidavit BEFORE the plea to the jurisdiction / SJ stage. The employer bears the burden to prove official immunity (City of Houston v. Rodriguez, 704 S.W.3d 462 (Tex. 2024)) but plaintiffs lose by silence.
Negligence per se claims fit within the § 101.021(1) waiver where the statute refines the common-law duty (City of Houston v. Manning, 2025 WL 1478506 (Tex. 2025)).
Election of remedies (§ 101.106) can dismiss claims via the employee/entity election — Seward shows the dismissal mechanics; also screens the public-safety-officer rule when the CLIENT is the responder.
Expect delay: pleas to the jurisdiction and immunity MSJs remain interlocutorily appealable with automatic stays (SB 2516, which would have eliminated those appeals, FAILED in 2025) — build a delay expectation and appellate budget into every TTCA matter.
Notice can be satisfied through the entity's designated agent/TPA; document any direction about where to send notice (Reyes v. Jefferson County, 601 S.W.2d 795 (Tex. 2020)); actual notice requires the entity's subjective awareness of fault [CONFIRM formulation — Cathey v. Booth line, not in notes].

Vehicle maintenance and telematics on governmental vehicles; TCOLE records [CONFIRM availability].

Intake must capture the entity type day one and compute the shortest applicable notice clock; the 24-hour notice-letter phase target applies with special urgency here.
Actual-notice fallback (§ 101.101(c)) tracked as a fact issue, never relied on as a plan.
2-year SOL (§ 16.003) + jurisdictional service-diligence tracking (Tanner): service-completion field with tickler escalation.

A7. Dangerous Animal

1. Challenge / watch-out

Two liability tracks with different knowledge elements: strict liability (animal with known dangerous/vicious propensities; propensities must be a PRODUCING cause) vs. negligent handling (ordinary care; proximate cause) — plus premises theory where relevant (Sandles v. Laskoskie rules digest; Bolton v. Fisher; Trujillo v. Carrasco). Plead in the alternative; the charge differs.
Expert-causation trap in death/unwitnessed cases: circumstantial evidence (blood on the dog, prior bite, victim's fear) without an ADMISSIBLE expert causation opinion = no-evidence summary judgment (Sandles — the report was excluded as speculative). Auto-task: qualified causation expert early where the victim can't testify.
Livestock-on-roadway variant: where the county has adopted a stock law, Tex. Agric. Code § 143.102 supplies the ONLY standard for highway collisions ("knowingly permit" element) (Pruski v. Garcia, 594 S.W.3d 322 (Tex. 2020)). Intake prompt: stock-law county? which statute governs?
Claims dressed as professional negligence stay ordinary claims: a physician's service-animal letters didn't make a dog attack a health care liability claim — no Ch. 74 expert report needed (Leibman v. Waldroup (Tex. 2025)); screen mischaracterization attempts.
Coverage: homeowner's/renter's policies drive recovery; check animal-breed exclusions and prior-claim surcharges [CONFIRM policy-form specifics].

Knowledge/propensity set: prior bites/attacks (with dates), animal-control and 911 complaint records, quarantine records, vet records (behavioral notes, vaccination), neighbor witnesses, owner's containment/control practices, warning signs, breed and training history, leash-law citations.
Scene/injury set: photos of wounds (disfigurement element), EMS records, rabies-protocol records.
Stock-law cases: fencing condition/maintenance, prior escapes, county stock-law adoption records.

4. Proof / damages theories + defenses to anticipate

Lee (Fort Worth 2024)) — disfigurement is a distinct element (Hopkins County Hosp. Dist. v. Allen) and dog-attack cases should always plead it; run Spotter C.

A8. Assault (Civil)

1. Challenge / watch-out

The perpetrator is rarely the recovery source. Intentional-injury exclusions defeat liability coverage for the assailant; the playbook's center of gravity is the NEGLIGENT co-defendant: premises owner (negligent security — Timberwalk foreseeability factors; Del Lago), employer (negligent hiring/supervision; policy-not-enforced breach theory, Flashdancer v. Fulcher), bar (dram shop), franchisor (uphill: no duty absent contractual/actual control over the franchisee's hiring — Massage Heights v. Hagman, 712 S.W.3d 615 (Tex. 2025)).
Dram-shop lane: TABC "provider… obviously intoxicated" standard now requires objective evidence of intoxication apparent AT the time of service (Raoger Corp. v. Myers, 711 S.W.3d 206 (Tex. 2025)); and the assailant/drunk driver can lawfully refuse to identify the bars on Fifth Amendment grounds (In re Peters) — so the bar-identification hunt (receipts, cards, phone location, companions) is a day-one third-party task while the clock runs.
Parallel criminal proceedings: track them; Texas does not abate the civil case for the criminal one; invocation of the Fifth permits a negative inference civilly (Wilz v. Flournoy); statements to police may not waive the privilege if involuntary (Peters).
Exemplary damages: assaultive conduct is the heartland of the § 41.008(c) cap-busters (aggravated assault § 22.02, sexual assault § 22.011, aggravated sexual assault § 22.021, trafficking ch. 20A, murder) — Spotter B runs automatically; pre-injury releases don't reach intentional torts.
SOL nuance: assault claims accrue on the assault; sexual-abuse claims may carry extended limitations (CPRC § 16.0045 — 30 years for certain sex crimes against children; 5 years certain adult sexual assaults) [CONFIRM periods — statute-based, not in notes].
Settlement-tax flag: recoveries without physical injury (some abuse/emotional-distress theories) are taxable; the Survivor Justice Tax Prevention Act (pending 2025) would change this for sexual-abuse recoveries — settlement-planner gate before release (Ch. 19 series).

2. Discovery to demand

Employer set: personnel file, background check actually run vs. policy, prior complaints about the assailant, training records.
Dram-shop set: POS receipts, surveillance, server depositions, TABC-mandated training records, credit-card records via third-party subpoena (Fifth Amendment workaround).
Criminal-case mirror: offense reports, 911 audio, bodycam, plea papers (a conviction is potent civil evidence) [CONFIRM collateral-estoppel use].

Crime Victims' Compensation application windows [CONFIRM — administrative deadlines not covered in course book].

Theories: civil assault/battery vs. perpetrator (collectability screen; cap-buster exemplaries); negligent security/premises (foreseeability via prior-crime evidence); negligent hiring/supervision/retention; dram shop; intervening-criminal-act causation answered by foreseeability (failure to enforce weapon-search policy made discharge foreseeable — Flashdancer).

A9. UM/UIM First-Party

1. Challenge / watch-out

The consent-to-settle gate is the defining malpractice trap: written UM/UIM-carrier consent BEFORE releasing any tortfeasor. The carrier holds statutory and contractual subrogation rights (Tex. Ins. Code § 1952.108); settlement without consent risks forfeiting coverage — the prejudice fight is unwinnable territory (Hernandez v. Gulf Group Lloyds (prejudice required) vs. Gonzales v. Phila. Indem. (5th Cir. 2016) (burden on insured; release of driver and parents without asset investigation fatal)). The lifecycle engine should HARD-BLOCK the release task on any case with um_uim_open until carrier_consent_written = Y — even for policy-limits tenders.
Brainard prerequisite: the carrier has no contractual duty to pay until the insured obtains a judgment establishing the tortfeasor's liability, damages, and under/uninsured status; demand, suit, settlement, or admission does not trigger it (Brainard v. Trinity Universal, 216 S.W.3d 809 (Tex. 2006)). Strategy fork the playbook must present: (a) tort-judgment-first route; (b) UDJA route — sue the carrier directly for a declaration of entitlement, ± the tortfeasor in the same suit, with discretionary fees under CPRC § 37.009 (Allstate v. Irwin, 627 S.W.3d 263 (Tex. 2021); Villegas; ripeness on unsuccessful benefits claim, Stelly). Watch item: Justice Sullivan's 2025 Dessart concurrence signals Brainard's fee holding may be ripe for overruling.
Nicastro waiver trap: on jury demand, the AMOUNT of UDJA fees is a jury fact question and the PLAINTIFF bears the burden to request the jury question — failure waives fees (State Farm v. Nicastro (Dallas 2025, pet. denied)). Charge-checklist item, auto-inserted in UDJA cases.
Severance/abatement is the norm: extracontractual/bad-faith claims (and their discovery) are severed and ABATED until entitlement is established; entity depositions on claims handling get quashed (In re State Farm, 629 S.W.3d 866 (Tex. 2021); In re State Farm/Dessart, 712 S.W.3d 100 (Tex. Apr. 25, 2025)). Assume two phases; plan discovery accordingly — but the insured IS entitled to a limited corporate-rep deposition on the carrier's liability/damages CONTENTIONS in phase one (In re USAA Gen. Indem., 624 S.W.3d 782 (Tex. 2021)).
Coverage screens: UM/UIM must be rejected IN WRITING or it exists (§ 1952.101 — oral/recorded rejection ineffective, Sloan); canvass ALL household/parental policies (multiple policies each pay to their limits up to damages, Am. Motorists v. Briggs); hit-and-run unknown driver = uninsured (§ 1952.102); "underinsured" per § 1952.103/Stracener. Carrier bears the burden on insured status of the other vehicle (§ 1952.109).
Offsets: credit for the FULL liability limits of the at-fault driver even if the insured settled for less; PIP/MedPay offsets against double recovery only. No health-plan subrogation against claimant-purchased UM/UIM or PIP (CPRC § 140.008) — lien-module rule.
Removal trend: carriers remove to federal court because the federal DJ Act lacks the § 37.009 fee hook — forum consideration at filing.

2. Discovery to demand

Policy set: certified policy, declarations, the written UM/UIM rejection (or its absence), underwriting file [phase-2], all household policies.
Carrier's file on the ACCIDENT (not claims handling): investigation results, liability/damages contentions and their evidentiary basis (USAA-scope corporate-rep deposition).
Tortfeasor set: liability limits (Rule 194.2(g)), asset investigation (documents the no-prejudice case for consent disputes and the underinsured math).
Phase-2 (post-entitlement) set: claims-handling file, reserves, adjuster depositions — calendar-triggered on entitlement judgment.

3. Deadlines to calendar

4-year contract SOL on the UM/UIM claim (Franco v. Allstate, 505 S.W.2d 789 (Tex. 1974)) — separate clock from the 2-year tort SOL; expiration of the tort SOL does not bar the UIM claim (State Farm v. Bowen), but the offset still requires proving the tortfeasor's liability — practical rule: run both clocks.
Prompt-payment clocks (Ch. 542): 15 days from notice of claim to acknowledge, commence investigation, and request items — § 542.055(a); 60 days after receiving all requested items to pay — § 542.058; violation = statutory interest plus attorney's fees — § 542.060 (18% per the course book's UM/UIM chapter; the 2017 amendments set judgment-rate-plus-5% for weather/542A claims — [CONFIRM the applicable § 542.060 rate for UM/UIM claims post-2017]). WRITTEN notice triggers the penalties (§ 542.055) — template the written notice at claim opening.
Consent-to-settle request/response tracking (no statutory period; workflow SLA with escalation).
Nicastro jury-question checklist item at charge conference; MSJ 45/90 clocks (SB 293).

4. Proof / damages theories + defenses to anticipate

Phase 1 (UDJA/contract): prove the tort case within the first-party suit — liability, damages (Spotter C), and the tortfeasor's limits/exhaustion; Dessart posture is the template ($50k tort limits + documented past/future medicals far above the $100k UIM limits).
Phase 2 (extracontractual): Ch. 541 unfair settlement practices (§ 541.060(a) — liability reasonably clear; treble on knowing, § 541.152); Ch. 542 penalties; split authority on whether the carrier can simply await judgment — newer cases allow bad faith where liability was reasonably clear despite no judgment (State Farm v. Cook (San Antonio 2019); Burgess v. Allstate (Austin 2021)) vs. the older no-bad-faith line (Mid-Century v. Daniel; In re Allstate County Mut.).
Defenses: Brainard no-duty-yet; consent-clause breach; offsets; failure to exhaust; "not legally entitled to recover" elements; abatement of everything extracontractual.

5. Data-model fields/flags

um_uim_open master flag → activates the consent-to-settle release gate, dual SOL clocks, two-phase litigation structure fields (phase = contract/UDJA vs. extracontractual; abatement_status).
Policy canvass checklist (every household/vehicle policy: carrier, limits, written-rejection status, PIP/MedPay).
Consent tracker: request date, carrier response, written consent document.

Charge checklist: UDJA fee jury question requested (Nicastro), fee evidence (lodestar, Rohrmoos).
A10. Wrongful Death / Survival Designation (playbook-level summary; estate/probate workflow detail is in Part II — cross-reference throughout)

1. Challenge / watch-out

Two distinct claims, two clients, two agreements. Wrongful death (CPRC § 71.004 — surviving spouse, children, parents, individually) vs. survival (§ 71.021 — the decedent's own claim, prosecuted by the personal representative; an "estate" cannot sue). The playbook enforces separation end-to-end: separate FEE agreements (WD signed individually now; survival agreement only after PR appointment, signed in representative capacity — a contingency over 1/3 without advance probate-court approval is VOID, Tex. Est. Code § 351.152(b)) and separate SETTLEMENT agreements (probate court has jurisdiction over the survival settlement only; sloppy commingled drafting hands it the whole settlement) → Part II for mechanics.
Beneficiary determination: spouse/children/parents only; legally adopted children in, equitably adopted OUT of WD (Goss v. Franz; Amos v. Central Freight — though estoppel children still take an estate/survival share, a settlement-allocation friction point); paternity by clear and convincing evidence for children born outside marriage (Brown v. Edwards Transfer); common-law-spouse determinations in probate don't automatically bind the WD court (collateral-estoppel elements required). Remarriage: fact admissible, effects and "marital prospects" excluded (CPRC § 71.005). Exemplary damages in death cases: surviving spouse and heirs of the body only — parents excluded (Tex. Const. art. 16, § 26).
3-month PR rule: if no WD beneficiary has commenced the action within 3 calendar months of death, the executor/administrator SHALL bring and prosecute it unless all beneficiaries request otherwise — CPRC § 71.004(c) — a control-of-the-case risk the engine should surface at day one of any death case (align the family, or the PR you don't control litigates the WD claim).
WD-vs-survival allocation logic (the settlement brain): liens and estate creditors reach the SURVIVAL share only — WD recoveries are not subject to the decedent's debts (§ 71.011) and hospital liens don't attach to WD proceeds (Tarrant Cnty. Hosp. Dist. v. Jones); Ch. 140 health-plan subrogation applies only against claimants who can recover past medicals (WD beneficiaries/children typically cannot — § 140.005). So allocation among WD/survival and among claimants directly changes net recovery — but probate courts almost never approve a $0/nominal survival allocation, and the split should track an anticipated verdict (conscious pain? decedent's earnings? beneficiaries' ages). Allocation modeling tool + probate-counsel-consult gate BEFORE any release is signed (Part II).
Subscriber-employer death cases: only an exemplary claim survives against the employer (Lab. Code § 408.001) — fully TAXABLE recovery (Ch. 41 exemplaries; the Ch. 19 "double tax"); auto-route to settlement-tax planning before mediation/release.

2. Discovery to demand

The 13-item WD anguish evidence-collection checklist (letters/cards, photos incl. old photos (Russell v. Ramirez), home video, activity documentation, funeral program/sympathy cards, social media, family counseling records/bills, children's academic decline, replaced-services invoices, manner-of-death and how the family learned, headstone, closeness evidence, treating/EMT/counselor/co-worker witnesses — NAMED in discovery responses).
Survival-claim set: conscious pain and suffering evidence — medical examiner testimony can support an award for seconds of consciousness (United Rentals v. Evans — sub-15-second interval); EMS records, eyewitness affidavits (Slack: interview officers/EMTs pre-suit), autopsy set.
Loss-of-inheritance proof (decedent's probable lifetime earnings/expenditures — Yowell v. Piper); pecuniary-loss monetization (replacement-cost evidence for household services and parental guidance — also grows the exemplary cap, Spotter B / Goodyear v. Rogers).
Beneficiary-vetting: the 15-item plaintiff/family vetting checklist (prior injuries/claims, criminal history, CPS, relationship harmony, living arrangements, remarriage status) — anticipates the standard defense themes.

3. Deadlines to calendar

WD: 2 years from death — CPRC § 16.003(b); NOT tolled by the death itself (Sowell v. Dresser).
Survival: 2 years, tolled up to 12 months after death — CPRC § 16.062(a) — but tolling ENDS EARLY when a PR qualifies (§ 16.062(b)); the engine needs death date + PR-qualification date as first-class inputs (Part II feeds these).
3-month § 71.004(c) checkpoint (beneficiary alignment task).
Probate application outer limit: 4 years from death — Tex. Est. Code § 301.002 (Part II owns the probate cascade: temporary administration 180-day terms, heirship, creditor-notice clocks).
All underlying-liability playbook deadlines still run (the death overlay composes with A1–A8).

4. Proof / damages theories + defenses to anticipate

WD elements per claimant: pecuniary loss (care, maintenance, support, services, advice/counsel — monetized), mental anguish (WD is the one context with a charge definition — Moore v. Lillebo), loss of companionship/society (Moore's five relationship factors), loss of inheritance, exemplaries (eligible beneficiaries only). Survival elements: decedent's conscious pain/anguish, medical expenses, funeral/burial, property damage, exemplaries.
Chohan applies at full strength — it WAS a wrongful-death case; run Spotter C with the wrongful-death enhancements (grief teaching expert; workplace/community witnesses; marked demonstratives; segregation per element AND per claimant, since the jury divides the award — § 71.010).
Defense themes to anticipate: remarriage/cohabitation, decedent fault, decedent bad character, avoided consumption/taxes, annuity-cost testimony; inoculation strategy (introduce bad facts first — but note Ohler waiver tradeoff).
**§71.005 trial guardrail (CPRC § 71.005, official text read 2026-07-26 — UNVERIFIED, no registry entry opened).** Evidence of the **actual ceremonial remarriage** of a surviving spouse is **admissible if true**, but the defense is **prohibited from directly or indirectly mentioning or alluding to** (a) a **common-law marriage**, (b) an **extramarital relationship**, or (c) the **marital prospects** of the surviving spouse. This narrows the "remarriage/cohabitation" defense theme above to ceremonial remarriage only — the kind of line that gets crossed mid-trial, so it belongs in the pre-trial motions-in-limine set, not only in the file. Note the interaction with the beneficiary set: common-law marriage can determine **who the §71.004 beneficiaries are**, so it may be litigable on that question while barred as a damages-minimizing theme.
Tax posture: compensatory physical-injury damages excludable (IRC § 104(a)(2)); exemplaries and interest taxable; allocation language locks tax treatment at signing — planner-before-release gate (Ch. 19).

Spotter B — Exemplary Damages

Trigger: exemplary_pleaded or facts suggesting gross negligence/malice/fraud; intoxication_suspected auto-runs the cap-buster branch.

Elements/burden: clear and convincing evidence + UNANIMOUS finding of fraud, malice, or gross negligence — CPRC § 41.003(a), (d); gross negligence = (A) objectively extreme risk (probability × magnitude; likelihood of SERIOUS injury) and (B) actual subjective awareness + conscious indifference — § 41.001(11); ordinary-negligence evidence satisfies neither prong (§ 41.003(b)). Evidence tracker links proof to each prong (objective: U-Haul v. Waldrip; HOS violations Rayner v. Dillon; subjective: known-defect continued use, Press Energy; no hindsight).
Pleading checklist: express exemplary allegations (Marin v. IESI); cap-busting exception must be PLED (Marin); defendant need not plead the cap — it applies automatically (Zorrilla v. Aypco, 469 S.W.3d 143 (Tex. 2015)).
§ 41.008(b) cap calculator — inputs: economic damages (only monetized, fact-based amounts count — Goodyear v. Rogers, 538 S.W.3d 637: monetize household services/advice via replacement cost to grow the cap) and noneconomic damages (enters formula capped at $750,000). Output: greater of (i) 2 × economic + noneconomic (≤ $750k), or (ii) $200,000. Charge must find economic and noneconomic SEPARATELY (§ 41.008(a)). Constitutional overlay: ratio warning at >4:1 (Tony Gullo v. Chapa); recent remittiturs to 4:1 (Xna, Amarillo 2024) and 2:1 (Swanson v. Clack, Houston [1st] Feb. 2025) — display a ratio gauge, not just the statutory cap.
Cap-busting felony list (§ 41.008(c), 17 items): murder (§ 19.02), capital murder (§ 19.03), aggravated kidnapping (§ 20.04), aggravated assault (§ 22.02), sexual assault (§ 22.011), aggravated sexual assault (§ 22.021), injury to child/elderly/disabled (§ 22.04, except health-care provision), forgery (§ 32.21), commercial bribery (§ 32.43), misapplication of fiduciary property (§ 32.45), securing execution of document by deception (§ 32.46), fraudulent destruction of writing (§ 32.47), felony theft (ch. 31, 3rd-degree+), intoxication assault (§ 49.07), intoxication manslaughter (§ 49.08), continuous sexual abuse of young child (§ 21.02), trafficking (ch. 20A). Requires knowing/intentional conduct via a SEPARATE jury question — EXCEPT §§ 49.07/49.08, which carry no culpable-mental-state element (Penal Code § 49.11) and bust the cap regardless of intent → the engine AUTO-FLAGS cap-busting on any intoxication_suspected crash. Open questions to surface: burden on the cap-busting question (PJC uses preponderance; unresolved) and unanimity (NOT required for the § 41.008(c) finding — § 41.003(d) doesn't reach it; 10-of-12 default).
§ 41.0115 net-worth gate workflow (state machine): motion → notice → hearing → WRITTEN order finding "substantial likelihood of success on the merits" (prima facie standard, less than preponderance — In re Bella; two-step: likelihood on merits AND that harm results from fraud/malice/gross negligence, In re Kimco (Dec. 31, 2024)) → least-burdensome-method scoping → production; mandamus-reviewable on the motion record only. TRAP the system must warn on: filing the motion triggers the presumption that adequate time for discovery has elapsed — be ready to defeat a no-evidence MSJ on the exemplary claim BEFORE filing. Absent the written finding, net-worth discovery is an abuse (In re Boone).
§ 41.009 bifurcation: on any defendant's motion — made BEFORE VOIR DIRE (or per pretrial order) — the court SHALL bifurcate: phase 1 = liability (compensatory AND exemplary) + compensatory amount; phase 2 = exemplary amount; same jury (In re Bradle); unanimity instruction mandatory (§ 41.003(e)). Branch: CMV cases under ch. 72 move exemplary LIABILITY to phase 2 (§ 72.052(c)–(d)) — different charge architecture (A2).
Corporate imputation worksheet: four Fisher/Hammerly routes (authorized, reckless employment of unfit agent, managerial scope, ratification) + vice-principal classes (officers; hire/fire authority; nondelegable duties; department managers — title not dispositive, Waste Mgmt. v. Dodd); LLC manager = officer (BOC mapping).
Ancillary flags: insurability analysis (generally insurable absent exclusion, Fairfield; public policy barred coverage for an employee drunk driver's exemplaries, Frederking) — feeds Stowers (Spotter D); pre-injury releases of gross negligence void in most districts, enforceable with express fair notice in Dallas (Quiroz); exemplary recoveries are TAXABLE (Ch. 19 double-tax planning gate); mediation readiness checklist (Ch. 41 pleaded, net-worth finding obtained, cap pled/computed).
Spotter C — Chohan Noneconomic-Damages Proof

Trigger: any case claiming noneconomic damages (i.e., all of them); enhanced mode for death cases. Frame: Gregory v. Chohan, 670 S.W.3d 546 (Tex. 2023) is a plurality (limited precedential force, Kelly Custom Homes) but courts apply it as if binding — litigate to its standards: legally sufficient evidence of nature, duration, and severity supporting existence AND amount, plus a rational connection between the evidence and the number; no "picking a number."

Per-element segregation (hard rule): separate amounts for past/future pain, mental anguish, impairment, disfigurement, companionship — an unsegregated lump award = remand (Garza v. Rogers); in WD cases segregate per claimant too (§ 71.010). The damages module refuses lump-sum entries.
Nature/duration/severity evidence inventory (per element): disruption of daily routine, work performance, relationships; treating providers and counselors; contemporaneous records; photographs; activity-loss specifics (walking, hobbies, grandchildren — Garza v. Escamilla); grief-process "teaching expert" and peer-reviewed literature (death cases); financial traces of distress (therapy costs, lost productivity) as amount evidence.
Non-family lay witnesses: deliberately cultivate NON-relative witnesses with quality contact — co-workers, managers ("disenfranchised grief"), clergy, educators, business partners, neighbors — less impeachable than family "moaner/groaners"; NAME them in discovery responses; the witness tracker tags family vs. non-family sources and warns if the inventory is family-only.
Anchor-free argument discipline: no unrelated-object values (fighter jets, paintings), no unrelated math (cents-per-mile), no wealth/"send a message"/punishment themes in a compensatory case (reversals: Chohan; Team Industrial — which also teaches: request the "not a penalty" instruction and bifurcate so exemplary themes don't permeate). Ratios to economic damages are neither required nor sufficient. WHAT SURVIVES: evidence-tethered per diem ($1/waking hour × life expectancy affirmed, Garza v. Escamilla); "reasons" for the amount articulated from the record; verdict comparisons on appeal (not to the jury). Closing-argument checklist artifact generated per case; mark demonstratives for the record.
Defensive edges: expect interrogatories demanding noneconomic calculations — objectionable "without qualification" (In re Scherer); garden-variety anguish keeps mental-health records closed (In re Barnes six factors), but a DSM diagnosis, psych treatment, treater-expert designation, or defense alternative-cause pleading opens records and a Rule 204 exam (In re Richardson Motorsports; In re Williams Bros.) — intake counseling field records the election; zero-award challenges rarely succeed (Cannon v. Fenton line) — don't over-rely on reverse-Chohan.
Watch items: SB 30's nature/duration/severity codification and noneconomic caps (HB 939/1419) failed in 2025 but return in 2027 — build cap-calculator toggles dormant.
Spotter D — Stowers / Settlement Pressure

Trigger: liability reasonably clear + damages plausibly ≥ any defendant's limits; also fires when any settlement demand is drafted.

Demand checklist (all elements captured as fields; a missing element voids the pressure): (1) in WRITING; (2) within policy limits — a demand exceeding actual limits, however reasonable, triggers NO Stowers duty (Yorkshire v. Seger) → the system blocks "Stowers" labeling until limits are verified (Rule 194.2(g) disclosures/carrier confirmation); (3) claims within the SCOPE OF COVERAGE; (4) offering a COMPLETE release of the insured — record WHICH insureds are released (releasing only one of several can still be valid, OneBeacon; insurer may settle for one insured or one claimant even if it exhausts limits — Soriano; Travelers v. Citgo); (5) terms an ordinarily prudent insurer would accept given the likelihood of an excess judgment (Am. Physicians v. Garcia, 876 S.W.2d 842) — attach the liability/damages package proving exposure ≥ limits; reasonable deadline; funding/lien logistics stated.
Accrual/ownership notes for evaluator display: the Stowers cause of action accrues when the underlying judgment becomes FINAL (Street v. Second Court of Appeals); no Stowers claim without an excess judgment or excess settlement — an insured who funds a within-limits gap has only a contract claim (In re Farmers, 621 S.W.3d 261 (Tex. 2021)); the claim belongs to the insured (assignment logistics post-verdict).
UIM-consent interaction (composition rule with A9): on any case with um_uim_open, the Stowers demand to the tortfeasor's carrier and the UIM workflow interlock — the demand may be sent, but the RELEASE cannot be executed until written UM/UIM-carrier consent is obtained (§ 1952.108 subrogation); sequence: (1) confirm limits; (2) send Stowers demand; (3) on acceptance, request UIM-carrier consent to the settlement/release IN WRITING; (4) only then release; (5) preserve the underinsured math (full-limits credit applies regardless of the actual settlement figure). The engine hard-blocks step (4) until step (3) completes.
Exemplary/coverage complication: a pending gross-negligence claim may be partly uninsurable (Fairfield/Frederking) — flag how non-covered exposure distorts the carrier's acceptance calculus and the insured's personal exposure; consider element-specific release/allocation language.
Mediation interface: most "Stowers demands" fail the elements (course-book mediator's estimate: fewer than half qualify) — pre-mediation checklist requires policy identification, coverage confirmation, and within-limits verification before any demand is styled "Stowers"; never open mediation with a demand above limits.
Data model: Stowers event record (date sent, expiration, amount, limits verified source, insureds released, coverage-confirmed flag, acceptance/rejection, excess-judgment tracking); links to policy registry and UIM consent tracker.

0. HEADLINE CHANGES TO THE CURRENT DESIGN
The "~100-day window" on hospital liens is wrong as modeled. There is no 100-day filing window. The 100 days is a coverage limit — the lien secures charges for the first 100 days of hospitalization (Prop. Code §55.004(b); days need not be consecutive, and it is 100 days of hospitalization, not 100 days from the accident — Bullard, Garner, Heights Hosp. v. Patterson). Perfection turns on filing with the county clerk before payment is made (§55.003) plus a 72-hour admission requirement (§55.002). Replace the window field with the checklist in §1.1.
"ERISA-governed" is not the operative split — "self-funded vs. insured" is. An insured ERISA plan is regulated like any Texas health insurer via the savings clause (29 U.S.C. §1144(b)(2)(A); FMC Corp. v. Holliday, 498 U.S. 52 (1990); Wurtz v. Rawlings, 761 F.3d 232 (2d Cir. 2014)) and falls under CPRC Ch. 140. Only a self-funded ERISA plan escapes state regulation (deemer clause, §1144(b)(2)(B)). The plan-type picker should make funding status the master switch, with a stop-loss sub-analysis (§1.3).
Every lien type needs a fee/reduction calculator, not just a "negotiated reduction" field. Ch. 140 lesser-of, Medicare §411.37 procurement reduction, Medicaid 15%/Ahlborn pro-rata, WC net-recovery/§417.003 — each is deterministic math the system should compute (§§1.1–1.13).
Add a fund-location status axis (Montanile) to every private-plan lien: segregated-in-trust / disbursed-to-client / dissipated-nontraceable / traceable-assets. It is outcome-determinative for ERISA recovery actions (Montanile, 136 S. Ct. 651 (2016)) and drives the written-risk-disclosure workflow (§2.4).
Two new top-level workflows: a taxable-damages gate before any release is signed (§3.1) and an estate/probate gate on any death or incapacity case (§4). Both are hard gates, not advisory checklists — Kostura, Babener, and Price Johnson each frame the underlying failures as malpractice-grade.

1.1 Hospital lien (Tex. Prop. Code Ch. 55) — includes physician and EMS sub-liens
Governing law: Prop. Code §§55.001–.008; HB 2929 (eff. June 10, 2019 — 50% cap, §55.0015 "admission" definition, Ch. 146 linkage); HB 2064 (eff. June 16, 2021 — §55.004(b)(3) pro-rata fee reduction on fact-finder awards); CPRC Ch. 146 (balance billing); ACA §501(r) (nonprofit-hospital billing limits); No Surprises Act (eff. 1/1/2022).
Validity/perfection checklist (auto-validated fields):
Admission within 72 hours of accident (§55.002). Post-6/10/2019, ER-only treatment counts — "allowed access to any department" (§55.0015; Featherly, Ft. Worth 2022). Transfer/subsequent hospitalizations inherit attachment if first admission was within 72 hours (Baylor v. Travelers, 587 S.W.2d 501).
Filed with county clerk (county where services provided) before payment made (§55.003). Constructive notice on receipt by clerk even if unindexed (Memorial Hermann v. Progressive, Houston [1st] 2011).
§55.005(b) four required contents: (i) injured individual's name/address; (ii) accident date; (iii) hospital/EMS name and location; (iv) name of person alleged liable if known. FATAL-DEFECT FLAG: lien naming the plaintiff as the liable party (or wrong accident date/amount) is not valid notice — Methodist Hospitals of Dallas v. Mid-Century, 259 S.W.3d 358 (Dallas 2008). System should prompt a contents audit on every filed lien and surface the invalidity argument.
5th-business-day mailed notice to the injured individual after clerk's recording notice (§55.005(d); lien survives if mailed but unreceived, §55.005(g)).
Scope exclusions (auto-flag): does NOT reach UM/UIM or PIP (not "public liability insurance," §55.003(b)(3); Members Mut. v. Hermann Hosp., 664 S.W.2d 325 (Tex. 1984)); does NOT reach wrongful-death recovery (Tarrant Cty. Hosp. Dist. v. Jones, 664 S.W.2d 191) but DOES reach survival recovery (Ohio Medical v. Suber).
Amount caps / reduction levers (calculator):
HB 2929 lesser-of cap (§55.004(b)(2)): lien = lesser of (a) reasonable charges for the first 100 days of hospitalization or (b) 50% of all amounts recovered by the injured individual. Physician sub-lien: emergency care during first 7 days of hospitalization (§55.004(c)); EMS sub-lien: county population ≤800,000, capped at $1,000 (§55.004(f)).
"Reasonable and regular rate" audit (§55.004(d)(1)): chargemaster is not the measure — discovery of negotiated payer rates allowed (In re North Cypress, Tex. 2018; In re K&L Auto Crushers, Tex. 2021); hospital bears burden of proving reasonableness (Perrin). For indigent/financial-assistance-eligible patients, ACA §501(r)(5) caps at amounts generally billed to insured (best or avg-of-3-best commercial rates, or Medicare rates).
Ch. 146 / EOB balance-billing check (hard rule): if provider was required to timely bill the patient's health benefit plan and didn't, it may not bill the patient the amounts the plan would have paid (§146.003(a); protection extends to estate/parents/family, §146.003(b); §55.004(d)(5)). Ledger rule: pull the EOB — the patient-responsibility figure on the EOB is the maximum lienable amount. Worked pattern: $30,000 billed − $20,000 insurer payment − $5,000 contractual adjustment = $5,000 co-pay/deductible → lien valid for $5,000. Same math with $10,000 adjustment = $0 patient responsibility → no lien. This maps directly onto the existing Type 2 medical-bill ledger — connect the ledger's "remaining balance" cell to the hospital-lien record.
Medicare-patient sub-rules: once Medicare is billed, charges limited to Medicare-approved amount (Haygood; 42 U.S.C. §1395cc). Provider "promptly" period = 120 days (42 C.F.R. §411.50) to bill liability insurance (filing a lien counts); after that, provider may bill Medicare (withdrawing lien except non-covered/deductible/coinsurance) or keep the lien — but lien limited to lesser of settlement and lien charges, minus procurement costs, no interest/filing/admin fees (MSP Manual Ch. 40; MLN7748519, Nov. 2023). 1-year timely-filing deadline for providers to bill Medicare (ACA §6404; 42 C.F.R. §424.44(a)(1)); untimely = deductible/coinsurance only. Build Kostura's 4-step escalation sequence as tasks: demand Medicare billing → calendar 120 days → forward liability denial → warn provider of being left out → UDJA action.
HB 2064 (§55.004(b)(3)): when a fact-finder awards a specific amount of hospital bills, judge subtracts pro-rata attorney's fees/expenses (Bashara inapplicable there).
Deadlines: filing race vs. payment date (no fixed window — model as a risk, not a clock); 5-business-day notice; provider 120-day promptly period per provider; provider 1-year Medicare billing per date of service; hospital suit on lien: apparently 4 years from plaintiff's receipt of settlement funds (Baylor v. Borders, Dallas 1979 — doubted in McAllen Hosps. v. State Farm, Tex. 2014) [CONFIRM current vitality].
UM/UIM reachability: NO. WD reachability: NO (survival YES).
Status-flow additions: suspected → clerk-verified-none (verification letter, indexed + received-but-unindexed) → filed → contents-validated / defective-notice → rate-disputed (North Cypress discovery / Ch. 146 / ultra vires vs. public-hospital officials, Lopez v. Sosa (Dallas 2023)) → negotiated → paid/withdrawn/declared-invalid. Release gate per §55.007 in §2.1.
Documents to demand: clerk's lien notice; clerk verification letter; itemized bill + EOB; hospital Financial Assistance Policy + application; chargemaster/negotiated-rate discovery set; Medicare billing status.

Plan gets $68,333.33. Stop-loss analysis: existence, attachment point, and amounts already reimbursed by the stop-loss carrier (discovery target). Levers: Hegar v. HCSC (Tex. 2022) — stop-loss sold to self-funded plans is regulable health insurance; low attachment point → argue plan effectively insured above that point (contra Brown v. Granatelli, 5th Cir. 1990); windfall math (plan recouped from stop-loss AND wants the settlement) as negotiating leverage.
ERISA status is a fact question the plan must prove (Gahn; Hansen two-step; DOL safe harbor 29 C.F.R. §2510.3-1(j) four factors; preemption is an affirmative defense, waived if not pleaded — Phillips Petroleum v. Brantley).
Plan-language audit flags (per §IX–X of the paper): SPD discloses subrogation/reimbursement (29 C.F.R. §2520.102-3(l) — if not, argue unenforceable)? SPD vs. plan conflict (CIGNA v. Amara; 5th Cir.: more liberal SPD reading controls, Hansen/Rhorer/Koehler; SPD as of date of injury controls, Gorman)? Popowski-compliant lien language (identified fund + portion) vs. bare "reimburse in full" (insufficient)? "You/participant" scope — injured dependents may escape (Hamilton v. Pilgrim's Pride)? First-party coverages (UM/UIM/PIP/MedPay) expressly reached (silence = not reached)? "Responsible/liable party" trigger — defeat with "doubtful and disputed claim, no admission" release language (Reinhart v. Vial)? Made-whole disclaimed? Common-fund disclaimed (if silent, common fund applies — McCutchen remand)? Condition-precedent signature form actually obtained before payment (Mills; Burgett — requirement not in SPD is unenforceable)?
Montanile fund-location status (required field): segregated_in_trust / registry_deposit / disbursed_to_client / dissipated_nontraceable / traceable_assets (van/CD purchases are traceable, Goodspeed; commingling alone isn't dissipation, Zirbel). Warn: firm's own fee is reachable while in firm's possession (GC America v. Hood; McKesson v. Dillow; Bombardier), and the plan bears the burden of proving non-dissipation (Verizon v. Rogers, D.R.I. 2024). Special-needs trusts do NOT shield (ACS v. Griffin, 5th Cir. en banc 2013); WD probate-court custody CAN (AEP v. Fitch, 6th Cir. 2022).
Counter-attack menu (task templates): allocate to family members/consortium claims (Gamboa; Griffin wife's award); WD-only recovery (plan can't reach WD; Rhodia v. Bollinger); no-identifiable-fund; probate creditor-discharge route (§4.5 — open question for ERISA, flag as such); Press v. Food Employers WARNING — refusing to sign the plan's reimbursement agreement can get the family's benefits terminated; counsel, don't reflexively refuse.
UM/UIM reachability: only if plan + SPD expressly say so. WD reachability: NO.
1.4 Medicare Parts A/B (traditional) — MSP
Governing law: 42 U.S.C. §1395y(b); 42 C.F.R. Part 411; SMART Act (2012); Section 111 MMSEA reporting; not technically a lien (Zinman) but recovery right against beneficiary, primary payers, and attorneys (42 C.F.R. §411.24(g); U.S. v. Harris). Reaches PIP/MedPay (USAA v. Perry, 5th Cir. 1996) and UM/UIM (Warmbrod). Counsel is on constructive notice — lack of actual notice is no defense.
File-opening workflow (at intake, not settlement): verify beneficiary status or expected eligibility within 30 months; send BCRC Proof of Representation + Medicare consent/privacy authorization (medical authorization alone doesn't authorize negotiation); open MSPRP portal file. Contacts table (BCRC OKC; CRC Lathrop CA since Mar. 2023) as maintained reference data.
Deadline objects (each with cite):
CPN 30-day response — if BCRC learns of the claim/settlement late or from the defense, it issues a Conditional Payment Notice instead of Letter: 30 days to respond, or the demand issues for the FULL amount with NO procurement-cost reduction. Highest-severity clock in the module.
Final-CP process: notice "once and only once" within 120 days of expected settlement (42 U.S.C. §1395y(b)(2)(B); regs 5/17/2016); portal updated within 65 days; re-check within 3 days of settlement and download — figure is then the final conditional amount; discrepancy determinations within 11 business days.
60-day payment of final demand (interest accrues from demand date; if funds arrive after demand, document receipt date and request interest waiver).
Section 111 / CMP exposure: RRE reporting within 1 year of settlement; reporting clock live since Oct. 11, 2024; CMS random audits begin April 1, 2026 (250 records/quarter); penalties $250/$500/$1,000 per day years 1/2/3 (2024-adjusted $357/$714/$1,428), capped $521,220 per record; 5-year CMP SOL (28 U.S.C. §2462). Plaintiffs/attorneys must furnish SSN/Medicare ID and notify BCRC before negotiating settlement.
SMART Act 3-year SOL for government recovery suits from notice of settlement; CPRC §41.014 — no post-judgment interest against defendant on Medicare portion if defendant pays within 30 days of receiving the CMS demand letter.
Reduction calculator — 42 C.F.R. §411.37 procurement reduction: Medicare's demand is reduced by its pro-rata share of attorney's fees + costs (ratio of procurement costs to total recovery). If settlement < Medicare's claim, Medicare generally takes total settlement minus procurement costs. No Ahlborn-style proration (Hadden v. U.S., 6th Cir. 2011 — allocation follows the scope of the claim you pled; pleading-consistency warning: don't plead 100% then argue 10%). Streamlined options as calculator modes: $750/$1,000 no-pursuit thresholds; Fixed 25% option (trauma settlements ≤$5,000); Self-Calculated CP (trauma ≤$25,000, incident ≥6 months old, treatment complete). Unrelated-charge disputes: diagnosis-code scrub of the payment summary; burden shifts to CMS after prima facie showing (Estate of Urso).
Waiver track: SSA-632-BK to BCRC → CMS regional office; hardship/equity grounds (§1870(c); 20 C.F.R. 404.506–.512); waivers >$100,000 require DOJ approval.
Other checks: Part C/D separate records (§1.5); dual Medicare+Medicaid check; defendant cannot force Medicare as co-payee (Zaleppa; Tomlinson); "settled but not funded" letter to avoid starting the 60-day clock prematurely; "NOTICE OF SETTLEMENT" cover-letter flag; confidentiality clauses must carve out disclosures to resolve government subrogation.
UM/UIM reachability: YES. WD reachability: NO on WD-only recovery (MSP Manual §10.9 — Texas WD damages exclude decedent's medicals); survival YES. Documentation set for WD-only position: fee agreement limited to WD, no survival pleadings/discovery, release does not release survival claims (§3.4).
1.5 Medicare Advantage (Part C) and Part D
Same recovery rights as traditional Medicare (42 C.F.R. §422.108(f)); private cause of action with DOUBLE DAMAGES in the 3d/4th/5th/11th Circuits (In re Avandia; Humana v. Farmers (W.D. Tex.); Humana v. Western Heritage, 11th Cir. 2016 — double damages mandatory even where funds held in trust; MA plan may sue the attorney, Humana v. Paris Blank). Separate lien record per part — Parts A/B, C, D, and supplement each have separate amounts; repaying one does not satisfy another. Medicare-supplement (Medigap) rights are contractual → route to Ch. 140 (§1.2). Parra v. PacifiCare (9th Cir.): MA recovery vs. WD-only settlement governed by state law → no MA claim on Texas WD-only recoveries — encode as WD-reachability NO.
1.6 Medicaid / Medicaid HMOs / MERP
Governing law: 42 U.S.C. §§1396a(a)(25), 1396k (assignment), 1396p (anti-lien); Hum. Res. Code §32.033 (application = assignment); 1 TAC §§354.2301–.2356; Ahlborn, 547 U.S. 268 (2006) (pro-rata limit to the medical share — alive after BBA 2018 §53102 repealed the repeal retroactive to 9/30/2017); Wos v. E.M.A. (arbitrary fixed formulas preempted); Gallardo v. Marstiller (U.S. 2022) (state may reach portions allocated to past AND future medicals; Texas has no allocation statute — [CONFIRM current TX HHSC post-Gallardo practice]).
THE 45-DAY CLOCK (criminal exposure): attorney must notify Medicaid (TMHP) within 45 days of being hired or of identifying the third party, with recipient identity, third-party identity, and treating providers (book: "the attorney is obligated to notify Medicaid within 45 days"); the statute makes the applicant's intentional failure to give notice a Class C misdemeanor (Hum. Res. Code §32.033 [CONFIRM subsection and whether it reaches counsel]); counsel is on constructive notice regardless. Build as an intake-triggered task with hard due date.
Reduction calculators:
Ahlborn pro-rata: Medicaid limited to the ratio (settlement ÷ full case value) × Medicaid's payments — e.g., $550k settlement stipulated as 1/6 of $3M value → Medicaid gets 1/6 of its claim. Inputs: full-value estimate, settlement, Medicaid paid; supports allocation-hearing evidence. Kostura: "giving Medicaid a disproportionate share of the recovery is malpractice."
Statutory fee reduction: attorney's fee = 15% of the recovered Medicaid interest + pro-rata expenses capped at 10% of total recovery (1 TAC §354.2332); Medicaid's recovery is deducted from the gross BEFORE the contingent fee is computed (§354.2332(e)); no fee on any charitably waived portion (§354.2333). Ahlborn reductions stack on top.
Hardship waiver request (§32.033(f)); state cannot waive the ~60% federal share (42 C.F.R. §433.139) unless recovery costs exceed recovery.
Balance-billing engine (1 TAC §354.2322): provider must bill the third party before Medicaid when TPL established; may keep above-Medicaid third-party money ONLY if all four §354.2322(d) conditions met; otherwise no balance billing; sanctions up to 3× the excess. Filing a hospital lien = billing the patient (Daughters of Charity v. Linnstaedter, 226 S.W.3d 409 (Tex. 2007)) — cross-fire this check against §1.1.
Scope levers: UM/UIM not listed in §32.033 "other sources" — argue unreachable (UM/UIM reachability: DISPUTED — default NO with warning); minors — Medicaid assignment arguably reaches only the parents' medical-expense claim, not the child's own damages (Manning; Torres v. Giacona, Houston [1st] June 10, 2025); WD reachability NO (no past medicals in WD).
Dual-eligibility check: Medicare AND Medicaid interests can coexist; also screen Medicaid managed-care HMOs (separate collectors; TMHP leads) and MERP (estate recovery, 1 TAC Ch. 373) on death cases — exemptions (surviving spouse, child <21, disabled child, hardship).
Eligibility preservation gate at settlement: SNT / §142.001 trust / Arc of Texas Master Pooled Trust / structured annuity before disbursement (ties into §3.3).
1.7 VA (FMCRA / 38 U.S.C. §1729)
Governing law: 42 U.S.C. §§2651–2653; 38 U.S.C. §1729; rates per 38 C.F.R. §17.101 (Federal-Register-published, judicially noticed, not challengeable as unreasonable).
Deadlines: U.S. gets an independent right to sue the tortfeasor if the injured person doesn't act within 6 months of first treatment (§2651) (§1729 framing: 180 days of care + 60 days after notice); government SOL: 6 years from the last day care was furnished. Track both.
Mechanics: VA typically asserts through the veteran's suit — required petition paragraph (claim "for the sole use and benefit of the United States … under 42 U.S.C. §2651 et seq., with its express consent") must be inserted — template it. VA gives free certified records but won't share procurement costs absent hardship; waiver/reduction per 32 C.F.R. §§757.18–.19; arguably subrogation-only, no reimbursement right vs. the veteran (Aldous logic — open in 5th Cir.).
UM/UIM reachability: YES (U.S. v. USAA, W.D. La. 2011; Warmbrod) — but seek equitable waiver/pro-rata (Commercial Union, D.C. Cir.: "equity is equality"). VA cannot waive TRICARE's interest and vice versa — separate records, separate payoffs.
1.8 TRICARE
Governing law: FMCRA; 10 U.S.C. §§1095, 1095b; 32 C.F.R. §199.12. Same 6-month intervene-or-sue window and 6-year SOL family as VA.
Perfection/condition-precedent: DD Form 2527 (third-party-payer info) is a condition precedent to TRICARE claims processing — claims suspend, then deny without it. Track form status.
Scope: "third-party payers" include liability, no-fault, PIP/MedPay carriers — but not the beneficiary or the beneficiary's attorney (§1095); no express reimbursement right vs. beneficiary (but attorney immunity does NOT cover federal violations, Taylor v. Tolbert, 644 S.W.3d 637 (Tex. 2022)); government claim must include future medicals — no settlement without considering them (32 C.F.R. §199.12). Payment to the beneficiary does not discharge the third-party payer's obligation to the U.S.
1.9 FEHBA (federal-employee plans)
Governing law: 5 U.S.C. §8901 et seq.; OPM rule 5 C.F.R. §890.106 (eff. June 22, 2015); Coventry v. Nevils, 137 S. Ct. 1190 (2017) (preempts state anti-subrogation law); Empire v. McVeigh (carrier reimbursement actions belong in state court).
UM/UIM reachability: YES (by rule). Levers: read the actual contract; subrogation-vs-reimbursement distinction (Freitas — demand complete plan documents); preemption weaker than ERISA's.
1.10 FECA (federal workers' comp)
Governing law: 5 U.S.C. §§8131–8132; 20 C.F.R. §§10.705–.719. Form EN-1108 calculator: injured worker keeps guaranteed 20% of recovery + pro-rata share of attorney's fee; carve-outs if actually pleaded — WD allocation up to 65% (survival ≥35%), uninjured spouse up to 25%, children 5% each (15% max for 3+). 1.11 Texas workers' compensation (Lab. Code Ch. 417)
Governing law: §§417.001–.004; first-money rule, no made-whole (Ledbetter, 251 S.W.3d 31); excluded from Ch. 140.
Validity/audit checklist: lien covers only benefits paid "to the employee or the legal beneficiary" — audit out TPA/case-management/audit/utilization-review fees (§417.001(b); In re Old Republic — $5.35M claimed vs. <half actually paid; RFA templates); carrier need not prove reasonableness/necessity of benefits (Serrano); deductible reimbursable in full (Argonaut v. Baker); waiver-of-subrogation endorsement check — waiver counts only if in the policy itself (Wausau v. Wedel, Tex. 2018 — TDI endorsement waives BOTH subrogation and reimbursement; Exxon v. ICSOP, Tex. 2019); discovery task: policies, endorsements, construction contracts between carrier/employer/third party.
Reduction calculator ("net amount recovered," §417.002): carrier reimbursed from recovery net of attorney's fees + costs (worked example: $25,000 − $10,000 fee − $579.08 expenses = $14,420.92 to carrier). WATCH ITEM: Hartford v. Francois (Dallas 2023, pet. pending) equated gross with net (worker took $0) — build both computations and flag the split. Fee to claimant's counsel for creating carrier's recovery: up to 1/3 of the cash restored to the carrier, paid in advance of reimbursement (§417.003; Alcorta), plus pro-rata expenses; no fee on the future-benefit "holiday" value (Allison) but pro-rata costs on it are recoverable; fee computed on FULL lien even if lien sold at a discount (Erivas — $2,500 purchase of $22,349 lien; fee follows the assignee, Sentry v. Bravin, Dallas June 21, 2024); tort defendant buying the lien gets credit for FULL face amount (Bennett v. Thomason — verdict-wipeout risk warning).
Employer-negligence reduction (§417.001(b)): lien reduced by employer's comparative percentage ONLY on a trier-of-fact finding — settling everything without a finding forfeits the reduction (Russell v. METRO; Morales). Encode as a pre-settlement warning on WC cases with employer-fault evidence.
Post-settlement "holiday" tracker: net recovery is treated as an advance of future benefits; benefits resume when exhausted (§417.002(b)); computed on collective recovery (Carty) but a beneficiary who takes $0 owes nothing and keeps benefits (Norsworthy); benefits continue until the settlement is FUNDED (Garcia); Starkey close-out drafting choice — "full and final satisfaction" language can waive the carrier's future credit (decide deliberately; template both versions).
Dual-representation compliance gate (§417.003(b)): representing both claimant and carrier requires advance disclosure, written client consent, and filing with DWC before judgment — noncompliance = denial of ALL fees (Gray Law, 5th Cir. 2009). One-fee ethics rule (Ethics Op. 549 (2003), limited to WC by Op. 660 (2016)): don't take a full fee off the top AND a carrier fee; any carrier fee agreement must be disclosed to the client and captured in final settlement docs (Criaco).
Deadlines: 2-year SOL vs. third party (shared; carrier intervention relates back, Guillot); 2-year SOL for carrier's suit vs. worker from distribution (Autry v. Dearman).
UM/UIM reachability: turns on WHO PAID PREMIUMS — employer-purchased: YES (Erivas, Knight, Aetna, Dyess); employee/family-purchased: NO (Gomez, Demock, Kinser). PIP: NO. Guaranty Association funds: NO (Ins. Code §462.207). Gross-negligence exemplary recovery vs. employer: NOT subject to the lien (Medina v. Herrera). Governmental self-insured plans: fee-sharing contested (sovereign immunity line, §504.053(e), Manbeck; contra Knapp) — flag on gov-employer cases.
Comp-substitute (non-subscriber occupational) plans are NOT Ch. 417 — route to Ch. 140 (§1.2) with plan-purpose analysis (employer-protection plan = offset/credit affirmative defense; fringe benefit = collateral source, Rentech v. Teel).

1.15 PIP / MedPay / UM-UIM carrier (first-party records)
PIP: NO subrogation (Ins. Code §1952.155). Strategy task: submit lost wages/household services and co-pays/deductibles to PIP first so Medicare/ERISA can't attach the PIP dollars. Medicare can attach if Medicare-paid bills were submitted to PIP.
MedPay: contractual subrogation; common fund applies (Seals) unless carrier protected its own interest (Valle, Edminster); made-whole applies where not expressly repudiated (Oss; Osborne). 2. ATTORNEY-LIABILITY GUARDRAILS (SYSTEM CHECKS)

Model these as a pre-disbursement lien-clearance gate: the disbursement step of the Settlement workflow stays locked until every check is affirmatively cleared, overridden with a documented reason, or routed to holdback/registry.

2.1 Pre-disbursement checklist (blocking)
Hospital-lien clerk verification. County-clerk verification letter covering both indexed and received-but-unindexed liens, obtained at/immediately before funding (liens filed after the check arrives but before distribution still attach — Methodist v. Mid-Century (2006); Richards). Support the two-check simultaneous-exchange closing (insurer rep at clerk's office; checks exchanged against verification letter + release) as a templated task with the sample-letter text from the paper.
§55.007 release-validity check. If a hospital lien exists, a release is invalid unless (a) charges paid in full before execution/delivery, (b) paid to the extent of full consideration paid to the injured individual, or (c) hospital is a party to the release (Trinity Universal v. Bleeker, 966 S.W.2d 489). Corollary: a Stowers demand is probably defective if it does not resolve hospital-lien/Medicare/WC statutory interests — surface at demand-drafting.
Child-support lien search on every client receiving funds (and every adult WD beneficiary), plus the Power v. Kilgore priority warning whenever LOPs coexist with a child-support lien.
Government payoffs current: Medicare final demand (re-checked within 3 days of settlement); Medicaid TMHP final; Part C/D and supplement separately; VA and TRICARE separately; WC lien resolved with drafted holiday language.
Co-payee check handling: a co-payee hospital cannot alone discharge the instrument (McAllen Hosps., Tex. 2014) — flag any multi-payee check for endorsement workflow.
Estate cases: survival proceeds go to the PR only, never directly to heirs while administration is pending (personal-liability exposure; Price Johnson §V.D); probate approval order on file for dependent administrations/guardianships (§§351.051(a)(4), 1151.102(b)(4)).
Eligibility preservation: Medicaid/SSI recipient? → SNT/§142 trust/pooled-trust decision documented before funds hit the client (client may later sue the attorney for benefits lost).
2.2 Never-do rules (hard warnings at document generation)
Never sign — and never let the client sign — a plan reimbursement agreement broader than the plan's actual right; annotate any repayment form "in accordance with Ch. 140 CPRC" where applicable. Forward subrogation questionnaires unsigned; counsel modifies/objects but never becomes a party.
Never personally indemnify defendants/insurers against liens — Ethics Op. 694 (July 2022) (also unethical for defense counsel to induce it; Regional Specialty Clinic v. Randle — attorney who agreed lost SJ). Release-drafting checklist: indemnity limited to claims "by, through, or under" plaintiff; capped at settlement amount (Knutson); valid medical/governmental liens only; releasees must tender lien defense to plaintiff first; deductible carve-out (Roadmasters).
Never promise to "protect the plan" and never make independent commitments — mere notice ≠ implied contract (Hotel Employees v. Gentner; Drury v. Goding), but a signature or promise creates exposure, and Texas attorney immunity does not shield federal-law violations (Medicare, arguably ERISA — Taylor v. Tolbert).
Never contract away made-whole in settlement documents (Rosa's Café v. Wilkerson).
Never take two fees on the same funds (Op. 549/660; the 20%-then-33% military-lien reprimand); Medicaid: no fee on the waived portion (§354.2333).
2.3 Personal-liability warning bank (surface contextually)
Garriga v. Ace American (Eastland 2010): attorney + client jointly/severally liable for the entire settlement ($12,600) over a stiffed WC lien though the fee was only $2,706.83 — liability is NOT capped at the fee. Also Watson v. Glens Falls (joint & several), AIG v. Federated (conversion + exemplary damages for distributing over a known subrogation interest).
U.S. v. Harris (attorney judgment for unreimbursed Medicare from a $25k settlement); 42 C.F.R. §411.24(g); Humana v. Paris Blank (MA plan, double damages, vs. law firm).
McKesson v. Dillow / Bombardier / GC America v. Hood: the firm and the fee are §502(a)(3) targets while funds/fee remain in the firm's possession.
Rule 1.14 / Op. 681 matrix: "matured legal or equitable interests" requiring protection = statutory lien; adjudicating judgment; court order; written assignment; subrogation right in the funds; signed LOP. Op. 606: no duty to hunt for unasserted claims. Encode as the trigger list for whether a known interest blocks disbursement.
Two-check practice + written client instructions: where the client insists on distribution over an unresolved contractual claim with no matured interest: (a) detailed disclaimer in the settlement statement (no notice received / client instructed no investigation / obligation is client's), (b) client's written instructions + written risk disclosure (Montanile strategy, plan retaliation risk incl. benefit termination and Press), (c) two checks (fees/expenses to firm; remainder to client), (d) disputed-portion options: joint-payee check, trust holdback, or court-registry tender. Statutory interests (Medicare, Medicaid, WC, hospital, child-support-with-notice, CVCF) are NEVER eligible for this path — hard block.
2.4 Fund-location tracking (trust accounting linkage)

Per-lien field fund_status: segregated-in-IOLTA / registry / disbursed / dissipated-nontraceable / traceable-asset — with date stamps. Segregation is what makes funds reachable (Bombardier); dissipation is the Montanile shield; the field also drives the interest/60-day Medicare clock ("funds received" date) and laches/delay evidence (Verizon v. Rogers — plan's delay fatal).

3. SETTLEMENT-WORKFLOW ADDITIONS

Insert four stations into the existing flow (settlement reached → check received → settlement statement → liens resolved → disbursement → closed). Two of them fire before release signing, which the current flow doesn't model — add a "release drafting/execution" step between "settlement reached" and "check received."

3.1 Taxable-damages gate (BEFORE release signing)
Trigger screen (auto-flag at case level, re-checked at settlement): gross-negligence/exemplary claims (CPRC §41.001 — punitive damages taxable); WD against a subscribing employer under Labor Code §408.001 (recovery is exemplary-only → entire recovery taxable — the Ch. 19 worked example: $1M recovery, ~$350k tax, plus tax on the $400k fee = the "double tax"); defamation/reputational claims; sexual-abuse claims without demonstrable physical injury (§104(a)(2) narrow reading; federal fix pending); emotional distress not tied to physical injury; pre/post-judgment interest.
Gate requirements when flagged: (1) settlement-planner/tax-advisor consult task completed before mediation or release ("tax planning must occur before the agreement is signed — signing locks in tax exposure"); (2) allocation language in the settlement agreement per claim/damage type/claimant (vague general releases → IRS treats all as taxable; defendant's stated payment intent matters; resist tax-indemnity clauses — Vincent; expect 1099 matching); (3) options documented: QSF (preserves post-settlement planning time; situs state; §468B; direct defendant-to-QSF payment avoids trust-account receipt), structured settlement (tax-free growth for §104 cases; bracket-spreading for taxable), Plaintiff Recovery Trust (removes the fee from the plaintiff's taxable income — the $400k-fee exclusion in the worked example), family-member co-claimant allocations, future-medicals allocation (Rev. Rul. 75-232).
Attorney-fee deferral sub-checklist (Childs-compliant; the five GLAM bad facts as boolean checks): fee agreement amended BEFORE settlement; defendant promises the future payments in the settlement agreement (not a provider side-contract); no lump-sum promise; client's fee obligation not terminated on funding; no right to borrow against deferred fee.
Data: allocation schedule entity (claimant × damage element × amount × tax treatment); QSF/structure/PRT booleans + instrument uploads; 1099-expected field; general-release-only warning flag.
3.2 Medicare futures / MSA decision point (at settlement negotiation)
No LMSA regime exists (proposed rules withdrawn Oct. 13, 2022; review thresholds are WC-only: current beneficiary + settlement ≥$25,000, or reasonably-expected-within-30-months + ≥$250,000 — and those are review, not compliance, thresholds). But CMS's position is that Medicare's interest must be considered whenever a beneficiary recovers for future medicals.
Decision-tree (document one exit): (a) not a beneficiary and not expected within 30 months → no MSA, record basis; (b) treating-physician certification that injury-related care was complete as of settlement (CMS Benson memo, Sept. 30, 2011 — keep the certification, nothing filed; new certification per additional settlement) → no MSA; (c) compromise settlement documents that limits/liability/causation disputes precluded funding future medicals (list the damage elements the money actually represents) → documented no-MSA; (d) court allocation after contested hearing (Big R Towing) → binding; (e) on-the-job injury with WC benefits plus third-party/first-party settlement → MSA required; (f) fund and self-administer (or SNT-embed) an MSA — track exhaustion before Medicare is billed. Exclude non-covered items from any MSA (grab bars, vans, custodial care).
Fields: beneficiary status; 30-month lookahead; physician-certification doc type; MSA amount/administrator/exhaustion ledger; decision-basis memo.
3.3 Minor-settlement sub-workflow
Structure: friendly suit/court approval; TRCP 173 guardian ad litem (parents remain natural guardians — next friend unnecessary, In re Bridgestone); minor-prove-up hearing with notice to lienholders of date/time to bind them (Roland); disposition options: county-registry deposit, Prop. Code §142.001/Est. Code Ch. 1301 management trust, structured annuity, SNT (Medicaid-eligible minors); ad litem fee taxed as costs.
Allocation logic (the parental/child split the Medical tab should mirror): parents own medicals to 18 (Fam. Code §151.001(a)(3); Sax v. Votteler); child owns pain, impairment, disfigurement, LEC, post-18 medicals (PJC 15.5 split). Torres v. Giacona (Houston [1st], June 10, 2025) [CONFIRM pet. status]: parents may NOT reimburse themselves for medicals out of funds allocated to the child — "and neither should a subrogated plan." Manning (2024): children lack standing to sue for medicals during minority. Parents cannot waive the child's causes of action via plan terms (Lowery v. Berry; Munoz).
Montanile-shield order template (from the paper, p.138): findings that all sums are the minor's personal property, represent undivided general assets for her own damage elements, and that none represents pre-18 medical reimbursement — template the proposed-order language into the forms library.
Fee note: probate/guardianship approval contexts cap contingency at 1/3 absent advance court approval (§1155.053) — link to §4.2.
3.4 Wrongful-death vs. survival ALLOCATION logic
Model WD and survival as separate claims with separate settlement agreements, separate releases, and separate fee agreements (Price Johnson: "perhaps the biggest pitfall"). WD agreement scrubbed of estate/decedent/heir/survival language; survival agreement is the only one submitted for probate approval. An unallocated global settlement lets the probate court seize jurisdiction over the entire settlement, impose its own allocation, push money to estate creditors, and cap the fee at 1/3.
Lien-engine rule: liens and estate creditors attach to the survival share only (Est. Code §101.051); the WD share is shielded (CPRC §71.011; Harris Cty. v. White — $100k+ county medicals could not offset WD recovery; hospital liens don't attach to WD, Jones). Medicare: no claim on WD-only recoveries (MSP Manual §10.9 — Texas WD excludes decedent's medicals); MA plans same via Parra; ERISA plans can't reach WD (Rhodia; Caterpillar v. Wilhem).
Sigmundik pleading strategy flag: if the plan is to avoid the survival claim's medical-expense burden, plead ONLY wrongful death from the start — never plead survival then amend it away with the subrogee participating (Tex. Health Ins. Risk Pool v. Sigmundik, Tex. 2010: a participating party who requests allocation cannot be cut out). WD-only documentation set: fee agreement limited to WD; no survival pleadings/discovery; release preserves survival claims.
Allocation-defensibility rule: allocations bind subrogees only via contested hearings/judgments (Ledbetter; Zinman/Denekas for Medicare) with notice to (or joinder of) the lienholder; agreed allocations won't survive challenge; segregate each family member's damages; never broad-form damages. Bradley v. Sebelius (probate allocation cut a $38,875 Medicare claim to $787.50 on policy-limits facts) as the model, with the don't-overread caveat.
Allocation-record entity: claimant × claim (WD/survival) × damage element × amount, feeding both the lien engine (which shares are reachable) and the taxable-damages gate (§408.001 WD cases).

Per-lien columns become: asserted amount → validated amount (post-checklist) → calculator output (Ch. 140 / §411.37 / Ahlborn / §417.002) → negotiated payoff → status → fund location. Plus: allocation schedule; two-check flags; registry/holdback amounts; probate-approval reference on estate cases; UM/UIM-consent and Mendez-sequencing confirmations; "no-lien clerk letter" doc link.

4. ESTATE/PROBATE GATING WORKFLOW (death and incapacity cases)

New case-level module, triggered by marking any party deceased or incapacitated. Applies to deceased defendants too (temporary-administration service route).

4.1 PR-appointment gate (blocking for survival claims)
No survival-claim engagement, filing, or settlement until: probate opened → PR appointed → letters issued → fee agreement signed by the PR in representative capacity. An estate cannot sue, be sued, or release (Henson v. Estate of Crow); releases signed by "heirs" for estate claims are void; only the PR receives survival proceeds.
§351.152(b) VOID-FEE TRAP: contingent fee >1/3 on estate (survival) claims is VOID without advance express court approval — even in independent administrations; guardianship engagements need approval of the entire agreement (§1155.053). Dual fee-agreement template pair: WD signed individually by beneficiaries now; survival agreement prepared but unsigned pending PR appointment (+ approval if >1/3 or dependent/guardianship). Flag any firm-standard fee above 1/3.
Relation-back rescue (Austin Nursing Ctr. v. Lovato): if the 2-year SOL is imminent, file naming the prospective PR "as personal representative," then secure appointment within a reasonable time — DANGER: if someone else is appointed, no relation back (Covington). Rule 151 heirs-substitution: model as strongly disfavored (necessity of administration is trivially shown — two debts, §306.002(c); a defendant can manufacture it by counterclaim).
Administration-type decision support: independent (cheap, no 90-day discharge rule, §403.058) vs. dependent (court oversight + bond, but unlocks the creditor-discharge machinery — deliberately choose it when medical-debt discharge value exceeds cost) vs. temporary (rescue). Venue intelligence: statutory-probate-court counties (24 courts/12 counties), reach-out-and-grab exposure (§34.001(a); PI carve-out §34.001(b) requires independent CPRC Ch. 15 venue, Gonzalez v. Reliant), race-to-file priority (§33.052), Dallas-court practice of refusing WD-beneficiary administrators; daily probate-docket watch on deceased-defendant counties (automation candidate).
4.2 Deadline engine (all from date of death unless noted)
3-month rule (CPRC §71.004(c)): if no statutory WD beneficiary (spouse/child/parent) has filed within 3 calendar months of death, the executor/administrator SHALL prosecute unless all beneficiaries request otherwise. Calendar it on every death case.
§16.062 tolling: death tolls the estate's (survival) limitations for up to 12 months — but tolling ends early when a PR qualifies (§16.062(b)); it does NOT extend WD claims (Sowell), and there is no revival if limitations ran pre-death (Russell v. Ingersoll-Rand). Engine inputs: injury SOL date, death date, PR-qualification date, claim type. Rule of thumb encoded: WD within 2 years of death; survival possibly +1 year absent earlier PR qualification.
4-year letters deadline (Est. Code §301.002(a)) with the §301.002(b)(1) exception — administration to pursue the estate's claim against the negligent party may be opened past 4 years [CONFIRM — the (b)(1) exception's wording is not established by the book text]; surface the exception rather than hard-blocking.
Temporary administration (Ch. 452): verified application; term up to 180 days; letters within 3 days; 15-day opposition window (temp administrator keeps acting); powers must be enumerated in the order (plaintiff-side: investigate, obtain records, engage counsel, file/settle; defendant-side: accept service, notify carrier, tender for defense & indemnity). This is the limitations-rescue and the deceased-defendant service tool.
Heirship (intestate deaths): Ch. 202 proceeding; attorney ad litem for unknown heirs is mandatory (§202.009); two disinterested witnesses (§202.151(b)); judgment is in rem. Affidavits of heirship in the settlement context = "blatant malpractice" — warn if one is uploaded as the basis for distribution. Heir-inventory prompts: common-law spouses, stepchildren/equitable adoptees (estate share but NO WD standing, Amos), adult-adoption limits, terminated-rights parents.
Probate-side deadlines (reference set): will contest 2 years; statutory bill of review 2 years (§55.251); restricted appeal 6 months; MNT 30/75 days; guardian inventory 30 days.
4.3 Creditor-discharge strategy (dependent administrations)

Sequence to encode as tasks with clocks: published general notice (§308.051) → secured-creditor notice (§308.053) → permissive notice to unsecured creditors (§308.054) starting the 120-day presentment clock (§355.060) — non-presenting creditors lose the claim entirely → PR rejects presented claims without stating reasons (§355.051) → 90-day suit clock (§355.064) — claimant who doesn't sue is discharged. Caveats as flags: doesn't reach the surviving spouse's necessaries liability (Fam. Code §2.501 — but that claim does NOT attach to the WD recovery); Ch. 140 subrogation likely survives the discharge (§140.003 supremacy — you can kill the debt but not the subrogation against the PI settlement); ERISA/Medicare dischargeability is an open federal-preemption question (probate exception; Boisseau; AEP v. Fitch) — mark [OPEN LAW] in the UI.

4.4 Guardianship/incapacity track
Triggers: TBI/stroke/dementia client; suspected diminished capacity at intake (capacity to contract is destroyed by incapacity — Mandell & Wright; fee agreement invalid; meet the client alone; CME form (rev. Sept. 2023) to confirm/rule out).
Alternatives-first checklist (§1002.0015): existing statutory durable POA (read it — litigation powers §752.101; springing trigger; execution date vs. incapacity onset), medical POA, Ch. 1301 management trust (defendant/carrier can initiate; often faster than guardianship), §1355.001 resident-creditor deposit (settlement ≤$250,000, no guardian of estate → deposit with county clerk binds the incapacitated plaintiff — the carrier-friendly fast path), representative payee, supported decision-making, SNT.
Guardianship path: application (§1101.001, any interested person); attorney ad litem for proposed ward; clear-and-convincing standard + least-restrictive findings (§1101.101); temporary guardianship hearing within 10 days on imminent-danger showing (running limitations qualifies); all guardianships are dependent — settlement approval required (§1151.102(b)(4)); suits by guardian of the estate, not next friend, for incapacitated adults.
Rule 1.17 documentation (eff. Jan. 31, 2022): permissive protective action for diminished-capacity clients (ad litem / information letter); record the assessment and action taken; note CPRC §16.001 tolls the incapacitated client's malpractice claim indefinitely.
4.5 Cross-links into the Liens module

Death case → survival-only lien attachment (§3.4); MERP screen; Estates Code notice-to-creditors workflow as an ERISA/hospital-debt lever; deceased tortfeasor → creditor-induced administration + "friendly administrator" Stowers path; bond-as-recovery-source note (§403.060 heirs' bond mandatory on creditor request).

3.1 Pre-suit investigation phase (upgrade from Slack ch. 25)

TPIA § 552 crash-report/records request tracker. All case types — intake: recorded intake calls (announced consent) auto-populate the intake-history lists, coverage inventory, and Medicare/Medicaid screen. The intake transcript rides the PNC funnel: into the case file on conversion, or as declination documentation.
