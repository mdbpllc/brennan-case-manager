THE CORE CONCEPT: the playbook engine

You already capture, per charge, a plain-English name + statute cite + offense level. The engine hangs off that. When a charge is entered, the system opens the matching offense playbook, which surfaces four buckets:

Challenge / watch-out — the issue-spotting checklist for this offense (the suppression angles, the proof gaps, the junk-science flags).
Discovery to demand — the specific, offense-tailored items to request on day one (beyond the standard 39.14 demand).
Deadlines to calendar — the clocks this offense triggers, fed straight into the deadline engine.
Defenses to consider — the affirmative defenses and negating theories worth evaluating early.

Two cross-cutting issue-spotters — search-and-seizure and investigative-detention — sit underneath almost every playbook, because nearly all of your offense types begin with a stop, a search, or a warrant. They're written once and referenced by each playbook rather than repeated.

Scope: deep playbooks are built for the offenses you actually handle. Per your direction, that's the ten below. Sexual-assault / child-sex (Art. 38.37) material is deliberately out of scope and no playbook is built for it.

THE TEN OFFENSE PLAYBOOKS
1. DWI / Intoxication (with blood or breath evidence) — the deepest one

The book embeds an entire DWI-manual chapter organized as a blood-test attack framework. That framework is the playbook.

Challenge / watch-out — three attack buckets:

Consent / warrant: Was consent actual and voluntary, or coerced? Was implied consent improperly relied on (esp. unconscious client — Mitchell v. Wisconsin, State v. Ruiz)? Is there a warrant, and is the affidavit actually sworn to? (The oath — not the signature — is the real issue; State v. Hodges.) Is the warrant stale? Four-corners defects?
Blood-draw method: Drawn by a qualified person (physician, qualified tech, RN, LVN per Transp. Code 724.017)? Accepted medical practice? Sanitary location? Any 4th-Amendment problem in the manner?
Analysis: Chain-of-custody gaps; analyst training/qualifications; batch/run errors; "crappy chromatography"; whether the sample was enzymatic serum "hospital blood" (which reads higher and needs conversion).
Don't lose the forest for the trees: it's still a DWI — preserve the ordinary attacks on the stop, the driving facts, the video, and the SFSTs.

Discovery to demand:

The search warrant and the supporting affidavit (public record once executed — CCP 18.01(b)).
The blood-draw records, the phlebotomist/nurse qualifications, and the kit lot/expiration.
Full lab package: analyst credentials, the chromatograms (not just the number), calibration/QC records, the batch run, and the lab's accreditation status (ANAB).
Breath-test maintenance/calibration records if breath.

Deadlines to calendar:

ALR (Administrative License Revocation) hearing request — short window after the notice of suspension; this is a separate civil track from the criminal case and is easy to lose. Flag it the instant a DWI is opened.
Standard pretrial-motion clock (below).

Defenses to consider: no probable cause to arrest; rising-BAC / absorption; contamination or fermentation; medical/GERD and mouth-alcohol (breath); alternative cause of driving facts.

2. Smuggling of Persons

Frequently charged alongside drug and organized-crime counts; often begins with a highway stop.

Challenge / watch-out: the stop and detention (see cross-cutting detention spotter) — reasonable suspicion for the initial stop, and whether the detention's scope/duration outgrew its justification; knowledge element (did the client know the passengers were being unlawfully transported / did it involve concealment or a shielding-from-detection purpose?); enhancement triggers (numbers of people, minors, bodily injury/death, held-against-will) that jump the offense level.

Discovery to demand: dashcam/bodycam of the stop; dispatch/CAD logs and the stated basis for the stop; any tip/BOLO and its source; passenger statements and their immigration-status handling; GPS/toll/ALPR data; the interpreter records for any roadside questioning.

Deadlines to calendar: pretrial-motion clock; if a co-defendant case, severance considerations; federal parallel-prosecution watch (smuggling frequently gets adopted federally — see federal module).

Defenses to consider: lack of knowledge; no shielding/concealment; mere-presence; duress; challenge to the stop that suppresses everything downstream.

3. Assaultive Offenses (assault, aggravated assault, deadly conduct, terroristic threat)

The Family Violence charging article gives a full offense-by-degree tree — elements, lesser-included offenses, and whether a family-violence finding attaches — which is a ready-made dataset for this playbook.

Challenge / watch-out:

Family-violence finding flag — whether the State can seek a 42.013 FV finding for the specific offense charged (it can for most assaultive FV offenses; it cannot for 25.07 bond-condition-violation offenses). This flag drives the firearms and immigration cascades.
Firearms consequence — a FV finding or an FV-relationship element can make the client ineligible to possess a firearm under state law (Penal Code 46.04(b)), federal law (18 U.S.C. 922(g)(9)), or both. Surface this before any plea, and remember a deferred adjudication can still be used to enhance a future FV case (Penal Code 22.01(f)(1)).
Lesser-includeds — surface the ladder for the charged degree so you can see the fallback verdicts.
Enhancement/degree triggers: impede-breath-or-circulation, deadly weapon, serious bodily injury, prior conviction, pregnant complainant, discharge of firearm.

Discovery to demand: 911 call and CAD; bodycam of the scene and of every witness contact; the complainant's medical/photo evidence; prior inconsistent statements / recantations; any protective-order file; the complainant's criminal history where admissible.

Deadlines to calendar: pretrial-motion clock; if self-defense is in play, notice/expert considerations; protective-order hearing dates (parallel civil track).

Defenses to consider: self-defense / defense of third person / defense of property (the book has dedicated self-defense articles — build the justification checklist here); consent for mutual combat where available; lack of the required bodily-injury or threat element; complainant credibility/recantation.

4. Theft

Challenge / watch-out: the value ladder (value determines the offense level, so scrutinize valuation and aggregation across transactions); the intent-to-deprive element; the chain of possession; whether "theft by aggregation" is being used to bump the grade; 7-year limitations for Chapter 32 (fraud) offenses if it's charged in that family (2025 change, SB 2798) — but theft under Ch. 31 has its own limitations, so confirm which chapter.

Discovery to demand: surveillance/POS records; the loss-prevention report and the LP officer's file; valuation/appraisal documents; ownership and lack-of-consent proof; any restitution ledger.

Deadlines to calendar: limitations check at intake (versioned rule table); pretrial-motion clock.

Defenses to consider: claim of right / mistaken ownership; lack of intent to deprive; valuation attack that lowers the grade; consent.

5. Unauthorized Use of a Vehicle (UUMV)

Challenge / watch-out: the knowledge/consent element — did the client know operation was without the owner's effective consent? (This is the whole ballgame in most UUMV cases.) Recent-possession inferences and how the State is stitching intent together; the stop that produced the vehicle (detention spotter).

Discovery to demand: the owner's statement on consent and its limits; any prior permissive-use history between owner and client; the recovery report; keys/ignition condition; ALPR hits.

Deadlines to calendar: pretrial-motion clock.

Defenses to consider: mistaken belief in consent; effective consent that was later revoked; mere passenger vs. operator; identity.

6. Forgery of an Instrument

Challenge / watch-out: intent to defraud or harm (the core mental state); whether the instrument qualifies and how the grade is set (money/securities/government instruments raise the level); 7-year limitations for Chapter 32 offenses (2025, SB 2798) — forgery lives in Ch. 32, so the extended period applies; possession-vs.-passing distinctions.

Discovery to demand: the original instrument and any forensic document examination; handwriting/exemplar analysis and the examiner's file (build the expert challenge history here); surveillance of the passing; the account/victim records.

Deadlines to calendar: limitations = 7 years (confirm) — surface at intake; pretrial-motion clock; expert-disclosure request (≥30 days pre-trial; State discloses ≤20 days) if a document examiner is involved.

Defenses to consider: lack of intent to defraud; lack of knowledge the instrument was forged; authority to sign; misidentification of the passer.

7. Drug Possession (Health & Safety Code)

Search-and-seizure is the whole case for most of these — the cross-cutting spotters do heavy lifting.

Challenge / watch-out: the stop/search/warrant (both cross-cutting spotters); affirmative links to possession (was the client in care/custody/control, or just present?); the lab — substance identity, weight including or excluding adulterants/dilutants, and which penalty group; chain of custody; social-media delivery enhancement (SB 1883, 2025 — bumps the offense one category if a social-media platform was used in furtherance).

Discovery to demand: the warrant + affidavit (four-corners review); bodycam of the search; the lab report and the analyst's bench notes/chromatograms; the field-test vs. lab-test reconciliation; the property/evidence log.

Deadlines to calendar: pretrial-motion clock; suppression-motion strategy tied to the warrant defects.

Defenses to consider: no affirmative link / mere presence; unlawful search suppresses the substance; lab weight attack that lowers the penalty group; entrapment where a CI/UC was involved.

8. Manufacture / Delivery of a Controlled Substance

Shares the drug-possession spotters and adds delivery-specific angles.

Challenge / watch-out: the delivery element (actual/constructive transfer or offer to sell) and whether it rests on a CI or UC buy; penalty group + weight grading; the social-media enhancement (SB 1883); confidential-informant reliability (feeds the search-warrant PC analysis — named vs. confidential vs. anonymous informant, statements against penal interest); any wiretap/CS-buy audio; enhancement to organized-crime (below).

Discovery to demand: CI file and payment/benefit records (Brady/Giglio); buy-money and recorded-buy evidence; surveillance logs; lab package (identity, weight, chromatograms); the warrant + affidavit and the informant's basis of knowledge.

Deadlines to calendar: pretrial-motion clock; expert-disclosure clock for the chemist; federal-adoption watch.

Defenses to consider: entrapment; lack of transfer/offer; agency (procuring agent) theory where available; CI-reliability attack collapsing the PC; suppression of the search.

9. Evading Arrest or Detention

Challenge / watch-out: the lawfulness of the underlying attempted stop/arrest — if the detention wasn't supported by reasonable suspicion, the evading charge is undermined (detention spotter is central here); the knowledge element (did the client know a peace officer was attempting to lawfully detain/arrest?); the vehicle enhancement (using a vehicle raises the grade) and any prior that enhances; identity in pursuit cases.

Discovery to demand: dash/bodycam of the pursuit; dispatch/CAD showing the basis for the attempted stop; the officer's articulation of reasonable suspicion; any pursuit-policy compliance records.

Deadlines to calendar: pretrial-motion clock.

Defenses to consider: unlawful attempted detention (no reasonable suspicion); lack of knowledge it was an officer / that a detention was being attempted; necessity; misidentification.

10. Engaging in Organized Criminal Activity (EOCA)

Usually an overlay on the drug/smuggling/theft counts — so it inherits those playbooks and adds the enterprise angle.

Challenge / watch-out: the "combination" / criminal-street-gang element and the intent — did the client act with intent to establish, maintain, or participate in a combination or its profits, or merely commit the underlying offense? (This is the pressure point.) Overt-act and agreement proof; how the predicate offense's grade drives the EOCA grade (one category higher, generally); gang-evidence admissibility (Rule 403 — the book flags gang/RICO-style testimony being excluded as unhelpful/prejudicial); severance from co-defendants.

Discovery to demand: all co-defendant statements; the alleged combination's structure/roster evidence; gang-database designations and their basis; the overt-act inventory; any wiretap/CS material; the State's link-analysis charts.

Deadlines to calendar: pretrial-motion clock; severance / joinder motions; extraneous-offense notice request; federal-adoption watch (EOCA fact patterns migrate to federal RICO/conspiracy).

Defenses to consider: no combination / no intent to participate in an enterprise (just the standalone offense); insufficient overt act; severance to avoid spillover; attack the predicate (if the predicate falls, EOCA falls); gang-evidence exclusion under 403.

THE TWO CROSS-CUTTING ISSUE-SPOTTERS (referenced by the playbooks above)
A. Search-and-Seizure / Warrant spotter

Fires whenever a case involves a warrant or search. Checklist drawn from the Search & Seizure article:

Four-corners rule — is PC established within the affidavit's own text?
Source of PC — named citizen informant vs. confidential informant vs. anonymous tip vs. statement against penal interest vs. crime victim; each has a different reliability analysis.
Staleness — is the PC too old?
Conclusory statements — bare conclusions without underlying facts.
Was the PC lawfully obtained (fruit of a prior illegality)?
Franks / truthfulness — false statements or reckless omissions in the affidavit.
Nexus — link between the item sought and the place searched and the crime.
Good-faith exception and the narrower Texas statutory view.
Scope / knock-and-announce / searching persons present at the location.
B. Investigative-Detention spotter

Fires whenever a case begins with a stop or field detention. Checklist drawn from the Investigative Detentions article:

Reasonable suspicion at the moment of the stop — articulable facts, not a hunch.
Third-party tips — did a tip carry enough indicia of reliability to support RS?
Duration/scope — did the detention last longer or go broader than its justification (turning it into an unlawful seizure)?
Exceptions in play: border-search, community-caretaking, good-faith — each with its own limits.

Both spotters end the same way: if the stop or the warrant falls, what evidence gets suppressed, and does the case survive without it? That's the question the tool should push you to answer early.

SHARED DEADLINE CLOCKS (into the deadline engine, offense-agnostic)

Surfaced by the pretrial and detention articles — these apply across the playbooks:

Pretrial written pleadings — 10 days after arrest (CCP 27.11, where no indictment required) or 10 days after service of indictment (CCP 27.12). A re-indictment restarts the 10 days.
Article 17.151 speedy-release — the State must be ready or the client is released on PR bond / reduced bail within 90 days (felony) or 30 days (misdemeanor) of the commencement of detention. This is a powerful custody clock — auto-calculate it from the detention date and alert as it approaches.
Speedy trial — now constitutional-only (the Texas Speedy Trial Act was repealed in 2005); evaluate under the Barker v. Wingo four factors. Not a fixed clock, but a tracked issue.
Expert disclosure — request ≥30 days pre-trial; State discloses ≤20 days pre-trial (39.14(b)).
Extraneous-offense notice — request early so the State's 404(b)/enhancement notice obligations are triggered.
ALR hearing request (DWI only) — separate civil license track, short fuse.
OPTIONAL FEDERAL MODULE (only if/when you take the federal adoptions)

Several of your offense types — smuggling, manufacture/delivery, EOCA — get adopted federally. If federal work is in scope, one module covers it, keyed off a "federal case" flag:

Detention hearing: never waive. The Bail Reform Act (18 U.S.C. 3141 et seq.) nominally favors release, but detention is the practical default; use live witnesses, not a proffer; a de novo detention hearing motion is available. (From the Detention Hearings article, which even supplies sample motions.)
Federal pretrial-motion practice and the 2025 Sentencing Guidelines amendments have their own tracks; wire the guidelines calc in only if you're doing enough federal volume to justify it.
Flag the parallel-prosecution / adoption risk on the state playbooks above so a state case that's about to go federal is spotted early.

TRANSCRIPT HOOKS (added after the transcript-integration layer was designed; full spec in docs/specs/transcript-workflows.md)

Michael now records and transcribes audio of conversations. On the criminal side, the transcript layer plugs into this engine at these points — with harder guardrails than the civil side, because the discovery and privilege stakes cut differently:

Guardrails first (encode as hard rules):

Jail/facility calls are NOT privileged. Facility-recorded calls are monitored; the system banner-flags any transcript sourced from a facility call, and the client-communication playbook keeps substantive conversations in person or on unmonitored attorney lines. This is the single most important transcript rule in the criminal practice.
Defense witness-interview recordings carry work-product and reciprocal-discovery awareness flags — what gets recorded may have to be produced, and Art. 39.14 reciprocal obligations are narrower than civil discovery but not zero [CONFIRM scope before relying].
Consent status + privilege tier per transcript, and the no-unsecured-AI-tools rule, apply exactly as in the civil design.

Hooks into the criminal workflows:

Client meetings (unmonitored settings): recorded with client consent where Michael chooses — documenting advice given on plea offers, punishment ranges, immigration/firearms/collateral consequences (the playbooks' collateral-consequence prompts get a contemporaneous record that the warnings were actually delivered — the Padilla-adjacent malpractice shield).
Plea negotiations and plea-hearing prep: the plea-discussion transcript documents what the client was told about future record-clearing relief — feeding the plea-hearing eligibility reminder and, once the barratry consult clears, the future-contact consent engine (the client's consent conversation is itself recorded and linked to the written consent).
Pretrial compliance layer: recorded client check-in calls feed the pretrial compliance log directly — the "good, bad, and ugly" record builds itself.
State's audio evidence in-bound: the same transcript pipeline processes the OTHER side's recordings — bodycam audio, 911 calls, CI/UC buy recordings, and the State's jail-call productions get transcribed, speaker-linked, and fed to the Statement Bank and contradiction finder (a complainant's 911 statements vs. their later testimony; an officer's bodycam narration vs. the offense report vs. suppression-hearing testimony). This is the criminal side's highest-leverage transcript use: the discovery-audit skill gains a searchable index of everything said in the State's own audio.
Suppression-hearing prep: officer testimony from prior hearings/trials accumulates in the per-officer Statement Bank (law-enforcement-officer party records already have cross-case history) — an officer's articulated reasonable-suspicion patterns across cases become searchable, which feeds both cross-cutting spotters.
Court-check calls (pre-indictment limbo): the recurring court-check reminder's calls are logged with transcripts, so the indictment-status trail is documented without note-taking.
