## 0. THE HEADLINE

1. **THE ENTRY-31 BRIDGE EXISTS, IT IS HELD, AND IT IS NOT WHERE T-27 LOOKED.** ***Hurlburt v.
   State*, 506 S.W.3d 199 (Tex. App.—Waco 2016)** — **published, full "OPINION," Tom Gray, C.J.** —
   squarely holds that the Court of Criminal Appeals' construction of *"in a single criminal
   action"* governs **art. 102.073(a)**. It says the gap out loud before closing it: *"The phrase,
   'in a single criminal action' is not defined in this provision, and **no court has otherwise
   defined this phrase in the context of court costs.**"*
2. **AND IT NARROWS THE TEST FOR COSTS — a refinement that matters to entries 4a/4b.** The
   "same criminal episode" limb **drops out** under art. 102.073, because the statute has no such
   limitation. §3.2.
3. **THE ROUTE IS *PHARR*, NOT *LaPorte* — which is why T-27's bridge test came back empty.** The
   intermediate courts route through ***Ex parte Pharr*, 897 S.W.2d 795 (Tex. Crim. App. 1995)**,
   which carries the same formulation. **T-27 asked "does any of the nine apply *LaPorte* to
   102.073?" and the answer was correctly no — but the question was aimed one case to the side.**
4. **THE CCA HAS SPOKEN ON 102.073's SCOPE, AND EXPRESSLY RESERVED THE REST.** ***Anastassov***
   (Tex. Crim. App. 2022) holds art. 102.073 *"applies only to court costs and fees"* — **a fine is
   not a court cost** — while stating in terms that it does **not** consider the costs holding
   below. §3.5.
5. ***XL Specialty* does NOT undercut *Fontenot*. It runs the other direction, and it confirms the
   test.** §3.1.
6. ***In re Arden* is the *Fontenot* pattern, not a third-party one** — and my withdrawn adjuster
   sentence was wrong for a second reason nobody had yet named. §3.3.

**⚠ A METHOD FINDING ABOUT T-27 ITSELF.** T-27 read **the nine PDFs in a folder**. Four of the five

## 3. THE READS

### 3.1 *In re XL Specialty Ins. Co.*, 373 S.W.3d 46 (Tex. 2012) — the element, and it runs the OTHER WAY

**Holding, from the opening paragraph:** *"We must decide whether, in a bad faith action brought by
an injured employee against a workers' compensation insurer, the attorney-client privilege protects
communications between the insurer's lawyer and the employer during the underlying administrative
proceedings. **We hold that the privilege does not apply.**"*

**On TRE 503(a)(2), the element *Fontenot* turns on:** *"Cintas could not have been a 'representative
of the client,' as it did not have the authority to obtain legal services for **its insurer, XL**.
For the same reason, Cintas could not act, on XL's behalf, on any advice 'thereby rendered'…"*

**⚠ THE AUDIT IMPLIED THIS MIGHT CUT AGAINST *Fontenot*. READ IN FULL, IT DOES NOT — AND THE
DIRECTION IS THE WHOLE POINT.**

- In ***XL Specialty***, the party claiming privilege was the **INSURER**, and the question was
  whether the **INSURED** was a representative of the insurer's client-side. **No** — the insured
  had no authority to obtain legal services *for the insurer*.
- In ***Fontenot***, the direction is **reversed**: the **INSURED** was the client, and the
  **CARRIER** was held to be a representative of the client, because the policy *"expressly confers
  upon TMLT the right and contractual duty to obtain and facilitate legal representation for Dr.
  Fontenot."*

**So *XL Specialty* applies the same 503(a)(2) test and confirms what it turns on: WHO has authority
to obtain legal services FOR WHOM.** It is also a **bad-faith action** — insurer and insured
**adverse**. The privilege does not bridge adverse parties; it does bridge a carrier obtaining a
defense for its insured. **And once again the disposition is failure of proof:** *"But XL has
neither pleaded nor proved that this is the case."*

**Candidate `C-XL-1` (unadopted):** TRE 503(a)(2) "representative of the client" requires authority
to obtain legal services **on behalf of that client**; an insurer and insured are not automatically
representatives of each other, and in a posture where their interests are adverse the privilege does
not reach between them. **This SHARPENS the WS-3 entry rather than undermining it.**

### 3.2 *Hurlburt v. State*, 506 S.W.3d 199 (Waco 2016) — THE BRIDGE, HELD

**The gap, stated by the court in 2016:** *"The phrase, 'in a single criminal action' is not defined
in this provision, and **no court has otherwise defined this phrase in the context of court
costs.**"*

**The holding that closes it:** *"the fact that 'in a single criminal action' has been interpreted by
the Court of Criminal Appeals and that interpretation has been accepted indicates to us that **we
must presume the Legislature meant for the same interpretation to apply to the same phrase when the
Legislature used it in article 102.073(a).** Thus, we presume that the Legislature, in using the
phrase, 'in a single criminal action' in article 102.073(a), meant the phrase to be interpreted as
'allegations and evidence of more than one offense… [which] are presented in a single trial or plea
proceeding' as stated in *Pharr*."*

**AND THE NARROWING — this is the part that changes entries 4a and 4b:** *"He reasons that because
article 102.073 does not include the phrase, 'arising out of the same criminal episode,' we need not
use that part of *Pharr*'s definition. **We agree with Hurlburt.**"* … *"Here, the statute does not
limit its reach to convictions arising out of the same criminal episode. Rather, it applies to
convictions of 'two or more offenses or of multiple counts of the same offense.' Thus, **there is no
need to use the phrase, 'convictions that arise out of the same criminal episode,' when applying the
*Pharr* definition of 'in a single criminal action' to article 102.073(a).**"*

**So under art. 102.073 the test is narrower than under § 3.03: were allegations and evidence of
more than one offense presented in a single trial or plea proceeding — full stop. No criminal-episode
requirement.**

**Application, and it is directly on the firm's facts:** four **separate indictments**, heard
together at the defendant's request, **punishment tried over four different days spanning six
months** — still a single criminal action. *"It is clear that all of Hurlburt's offenses were heard
at one time."* Distinguished from *Pharr*, *"where the trial court concluded each proceeding before
the next one began."*

**⚠ AND THAT DISTINCTION IS THE AUTHORITY ENTRY 31 HAS BEEN MISSING.** Entry `4a` (=31) is the
**separate-actions** proposition, resting on art. 102.073's negative implication. ***Pharr* is the
case where sequentially concluded proceedings were NOT a single criminal action** — the negative
side, held. **Named as a candidate; *Pharr* was not read and is not selected.**

### 3.3 *In re Arden* (El Paso 2004) — the *Fontenot* pattern, and my withdrawn sentence was wrong twice over

**Facts, which are not what the audit's snippet suggested:** **Arden was the DEFENDANT** — he
rear-ended Bryan. **Farmers insured Arden.** The adjuster took **Arden's own** recorded statement.
The adjuster's affidavit stated he secured it *"while acting as **his** representative, for the
purpose of obtaining and facilitating **his** legal representation because he believed that the
carrier would owe him a legal defense."*

**So this is the *Fontenot* pattern exactly: the insured's own statement, to his own carrier's
adjuster, where the carrier owed him a defense. It is NOT a third-party witness statement**, and it
does not stand for the broad proposition that adjuster recordings are privileged.

**Two further points worth keeping:**
- **The proof was made.** *"The adjuster's affidavit was the only evidence presented."* That is the
  difference from *W&G Trucking*, where the record established nothing — **the burden point again.**
- **Arden expressly conceded the work-product limb**, moving for protection *"asserting that
  **although witness statements are not work product**, such statements can still be protected by
  the attorney-client communication privilege."* **A party conceding in 2003 exactly what TRCP
  192.5(c)(1) says — independent corroboration of `#103`'s correction to the WS-3 memo's §4.**

**Disposition:** *"we conditionally grant the Relator's petition for writ of mandamus."*

**Candidate `C-ARD-1` (unadopted):** an insured's own recorded statement, taken by his carrier's
adjuster who is acting to obtain and facilitate the legal defense the carrier owes him, may be
protected by the attorney-client privilege **where the record establishes that role**. **Civil,
memorandum opinion, post-2003.**

### 3.4 *Whaley v. State* (Dallas 2020) — applies both, and independently validates two `#103` corrections

**Its own summary makes the link:** *"We conclude that the offenses were part of the same criminal
episode **prosecuted in a single criminal action** and therefore the trial court erred by stacking
the sentences. **And, because the offenses were adjudicated in a single proceeding, costs should not
have been assessed for both offenses.**"*

**On costs:** *"Here, because the cases were tried in the same proceeding, the court should only have
assessed costs for the higher category offense…"*

**⚠ INDEPENDENT VALIDATION OF `#103`.** *Whaley*'s footnote 1 quotes LaPorte's definition and
pin-cites it to **415** — **not 414** — with the parenthetical ***"overruled on other grounds by Ex
parte Carter, 521 S.W.3d 344 (Tex. Crim. App. 2017)."*** **That is precisely the page and precisely
the parenthetical form `#103` proposed at `Q-RL6-3`, arrived at by a Texas court of appeals
independently.**

**But be precise about what *Whaley* is worth:** the costs issue was **unopposed** — *"Appellant and
the State agree that the court erroneously assessed duplicative costs"* — so the court reasoned
little; it **applies** the two provisions on shared facts without expressly holding that LaPorte's
definition governs 102.073; and **its publication designation is unresolved** (§2). ***Hurlburt* is
the stronger authority on every axis.**

### 3.5 *Anastassov* (Tex. Crim. App. 2022) — the CCA on 102.073's scope, and an express reservation

**Found by search, not in any folder.** The CCA held the court below *"incorrectly relied in part
upon Code of Criminal Procedure Article 102.073, **which applies only to court costs and fees**…
**A fine is not a court cost or fee; it is part of the punishment.**… To the extent the court of
appeals suggested that Article 102.073 was applicable to fines and meant that the imposition of
multiple fines here was unlawful, **it was incorrect.**"*

**And it reserved the rest, in terms:** *"We understand the State's petition for discretionary
review to challenge only the court of appeals' deletion of the $10,000 fine… and not its deletion of
the $599 in court costs. **Therefore, we do not consider the propriety of the court of appeals'
holding with respect to court costs in this opinion.**"*

**Two consequences.** **(a)** There is a **CCA holding on 102.073's scope** — costs and fees only,
**not fines** — and CR-10's cost-total check could easily conflate the two. **(b)** There is still
**no CCA holding** on whether the "single criminal action" construction governs 102.073; *Hurlburt*
remains the reasoned intermediate authority, and **the CCA declined the chance to review it.**

**Candidate `C-ANA-1` (unadopted):** art. 102.073 applies **only to court costs and fees**; a fine is
part of the punishment and outside the article's reach. **Authorship not checked — flagged, not
staged for verification.**
