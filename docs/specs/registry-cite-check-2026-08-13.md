## §2 — Candidate cites for the two cite-less criminal entries (registry: `legal-rule-registry-criminal-plea-and-costs.md`)

### Entry 3 — "degree of offense and punishment assessed must correspond" (no cite carried)
Candidates, with what was actually read:
- **Tex. Penal Code § 12.34** (Third Degree Felony Punishment) — verbatim text confirmed (Texas
  statutes site): third-degree punishment is "imprisonment in the Texas Department of Criminal
  Justice" for 2–10 years. **§ 12.35** (State Jail Felony Punishment): state-jail punishment is
  "confinement in a state jail" for 180 days–2 years. Together these supply the specific mismatch
  in the capture's live exhibit (a third-degree felony punished by state-jail confinement is
  outside the authorized scheme).
- **Mizell v. State, 119 S.W.3d 804, 806 (Tex. Crim. App. 2003)** — READ IN FULL from FLP,
  majority opinion (Cochran, J., seven judges joining). Exact passage at 806: **"A sentence that
  is outside the maximum or minimum range of punishment is unauthorized by law and therefore
  illegal."** Also at 806: any court with jurisdiction "may always notice and correct an illegal
  sentence." Directly powers CR-10's check as a warning-grade rule.

### Entry 4 — "court costs assessed per cause, non-concurrent" (no cite carried) — **FLAG: the statute's text cuts BOTH ways**
- **Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 102.073** — verbatim text confirmed (texas.public.law):
  (a) "In a single criminal action in which a defendant is convicted of two or more offenses or of
  multiple counts of the same offense, the court may assess each court cost or fee only once
  against the defendant." (b) costs determined by offense category are assessed "using the highest
  category of offense." (c) fine-only actions excluded. Added 2015 (S.B. 740), eff. 9/1/2015.
- **The flag, stated plainly:** the proposition as carried ("per cause, non-concurrent") is
  accurate as against *concurrent sentencing* — costs do not merge because sentences run
  concurrently — but for causes disposed in a **single criminal action** (e.g., one consolidated
  plea hearing), art. 102.073(a) points the OPPOSITE way: each cost is assessed **only once**, not
  per cause. The capture's live exhibit was "a multi-cause consolidated plea." If those causes
  were a single criminal action, a CR-10 cost check built on the unqualified proposition would
  systematically produce totals the statute forbids. **The entry likely needs a wording split**
  (separate-actions rule vs. single-action rule), which is Michael's verification-and-wording act.
- Case candidates for "single criminal action" — **existence confirmed in FLP, passages NOT read;
  conventional descriptions only, UNVERIFIED**: *LaPorte v. State*, 840 S.W.2d 412 (Tex. Crim.
  App. 1992) (commonly cited as defining "single criminal action"); *Hurlburt v. State*, 506
  S.W.3d 199 (Tex. App.—Waco 2016) (commonly cited applying art. 102.073 to cases tried together).
