## §2 — *EX PARTE PHARR*, READ IN FULL — the negative side

**Posture.** Post-conviction habeas under art. 11.07. Applicant convicted of **two capital
murders**; under a plea agreement, punishment assessed at **life in each cause, to be served
consecutively.** No appeal taken.

**Contention.** The trial court improperly cumulated because the causes *"arose out of the same
criminal episode and were prosecuted in the same criminal action"* — Penal Code § 3.03; *LaPorte v.
State*, 840 S.W.2d 412 (Tex. Crim. App. 1992).

**The definition, quoted from the copy:**

> "The Texas Legislature intended a 'single criminal action' to refer to a **single trial or plea
> proceeding**." — *LaPorte*, 840 S.W.2d at 414.

> "A defendant is prosecuted in a 'single criminal action' when allegations and evidence of more
> than one offense arising out of the same criminal episode are presented in a **single trial or
> plea proceedings**." — *LaPorte*, 840 S.W.2d at 415.

**The facts, and they are the whole value of this case:**

> "Here, the statement of facts shows that in cause number 7760 applicant pleaded guilty, was
> admonished, was found guilty, and was sentenced. **Immediately thereafter**, in cause number 7761
> applicant pleaded guilty, was admonished, was found guilty, and was sentenced. The record supports
> the trial court's finding that applicant was **not tried in a single criminal action**. The
> cumulation order did not violate Section 3.03."

**Disposition:** *"The relief sought by applicant is denied."*

**WHAT THIS ESTABLISHES, AND IT IS NARROWER AND HARDER THAN A SUMMARY WOULD SUGGEST.** Two plea
proceedings, **same day, in immediate succession, same court, same defendant, same plea
agreement** — and still **two criminal actions.** **Physical contiguity does not merge proceedings.
The operative fact is that each proceeding ran to completion before the next began.** *Hurlburt*
later restates exactly that as the distinguishing fact (§3).

**THE LAPORTE SUPERSESSION IS CHECKED RATHER THAN ASSUMED, because this project already caught it
once.** `criminal-opinions-read-2026-08.md` records that **LaPorte is overruled IN PART** — the
**void-sentence** holding by five votes — while **its DEFINITIONAL holding survives.** **Everything
*Pharr* takes from *LaPorte* is the definitional half**, at 414 and 415. **So *Pharr*'s footing is
on the surviving half, not the overruled one.** *(This is a reading of the record against the text,
not a citator check — see §5.)*

## §3 — *HURLBURT v. STATE*, READ IN FULL — the bridge to art. 102.073

**Posture.** Four separate indictments, four offenses of aggravated sexual assault of a child;
20 years on each, **concurrent**; **$354 in costs assessed in each of the four judgments.**

**The statute, as the opinion quotes it:**

> "(a) In a single criminal action in which a defendant is convicted of two or more offenses or of
> multiple counts of the same offense, the court may assess each court cost or fee **only once**
> against the defendant." — art. 102.073(a).

**THE GAP THE OPINION OPENS WITH, and it is why the bridge was needed at all:**

> "The phrase, 'in a single criminal action' **is not defined in this provision, and no court has
> otherwise defined this phrase in the context of court costs.**"

**THE BRIDGE, in the court's own words:** the phrase *"has, however, been defined by the Court of
Criminal Appeals for a different provision, section 3.03 of the Texas Penal Code. See Ex parte
Pharr."* The court then presumes the Legislature used the settled construction:

> "the fact that 'in a single criminal action' has been interpreted by the Court of Criminal Appeals
> and that interpretation has been accepted indicates to us that **we must presume the Legislature
> meant for the same interpretation to apply to the same phrase** when the Legislature used it in
> article 102.073(a)."

**THE CRIMINAL-EPISODE LIMB DROPS OUT — expressly, and by agreement rather than against
opposition:**

> "Hurlburt wants us to use **only part** of the *Pharr* definition… He reasons that because article
> 102.073 does not include the phrase, 'arising out of the same criminal episode,' we need not use
> that part of *Pharr*'s definition. **We agree with Hurlburt.**"

The reasoning: § 3.03 concerns convictions arising out of the same criminal episode, so *Pharr*
repeated that phrase; **art. 102.073 does not limit its reach that way** — it applies to *"two or
more offenses or of multiple counts of the same offense."*

**THE STATE'S CONTRARY READING WAS PUT AND REJECTED** — that the phrase means *"the individual case
or filing instrument."* Rejected because (i) that interpretation for § 3.02 was rejected in
*LaPorte*, *"the predecessor to Pharr"*; (ii) it would conflict with the statute's own coverage of
*"two or more offenses or multiple counts"*; and (iii) the court held the phrase **not ambiguous**
and declined the State's extra-textual sources.

**APPLICATION — and the contrast with *Pharr* is drawn by the court itself:**

> "This is **unlike the facts in Pharr where the trial court concluded each proceeding before the
> next one began**. It is clear that all of Hurlburt's offenses were **heard at one time**. Thus,
> because allegations and evidence of more than one offense were presented in a single trial or plea
> proceeding, **the trial court erred in assessing costs in each conviction.**"

Facts supporting it: all four cases heard together **at Hurlburt's request, the State joining**;
open pleas to each, accepted after each; **punishment tried together to the court on four different
days spanning six months.** *(That last fact is worth keeping: a proceeding spread over six months
was still "one time." **The test is not calendar compactness — it is whether the proceedings were
conducted as one.** It is the mirror image of *Pharr*, where same-day succession was still two.)*

**Disposition on costs:** three of the four judgments modified to **delete the assessed court
costs**. Two further issues sustained on the State's concession: **no statutory authorization for a
$2.00 extradition fee**, and the **jury reimbursement fee is limited to $4.00** (art. 102.0045(a)).

**FOOTNOTE 1 IS PART OF THE FINDING AND IS NOT BURIED HERE.** The court acknowledges *Williams v.
State*, 495 S.W.3d 583 (Tex. App.—Houston [1st Dist.] 2016, pet. granted) (op. on reh'g), where the
State conceded error and **"without any analysis"** the court held it was error to assess costs in
each judgment. **Discretionary review had been granted in PD–0947–16 — but "no issue was presented
challenging the appellate court's decision regarding error in assessing costs in each judgment."**

## §4 — THE PAIR, AND WHAT IT DOES FOR ENTRY 31

**They interlock, and *Hurlburt* is the one that does the interlocking.**

| | *Pharr* (CCA, 1995) | *Hurlburt* (Waco COA, 2016) |
|---|---|---|
| Statute | Penal Code **§ 3.03** (cumulation) | **Art. 102.073** (costs) |
| Facts | two plea proceedings, **same day, one concluded before the next began** | four cases **heard at one time**, punishment over six months |
| Result | **NOT** a single criminal action | **IS** a single criminal action |
| Role | **the NEGATIVE side** | **the POSITIVE side, and the bridge** |

**FOR ENTRY 31's NEGATIVE IMPLICATION — that separate criminal actions mean per-cause costs — the
pair supplies the reasoning but note precisely what each contributes.** *Hurlburt* holds that a
single criminal action means costs **once**. **The converse — that separate actions mean costs
**each** — is the negative implication, and neither opinion states it as a holding.** What *Pharr*
supplies is the **definitional boundary**: it shows what falls outside "single criminal action," and
it does so on facts that are close rather than easy. **The inference from "outside" to "costs per
cause" is the step the entry would be making, and it is Michael's to make or decline.**

## §6 — CANDIDATE PROPOSITIONS (all UNVERIFIED; sources named per item)

**None of these is entered anywhere. They are drafted so that a ruling can be one act.**

**`C-PH-1` — "single criminal action" means a single trial or plea proceeding.**
*Proposition:* A defendant is prosecuted in a "single criminal action" when allegations and evidence
of more than one offense are presented in a single trial or plea proceeding.
*Cite:* *Ex parte Pharr*, 897 S.W.2d 795, 796 (Tex. Crim. App. 1995) (per curiam), quoting *LaPorte
v. State*, 840 S.W.2d 412, 414–15 (Tex. Crim. App. 1992).
*Status:* **UNVERIFIED.** *Source:* vLex Fastcase copy `VLEX-887450014`, read 2026-08-19; not
reporter-verified; no currency check run.

**`C-PH-2` — successive proceedings are not a single criminal action, even same-day.**
*Proposition:* Where the record shows one plea proceeding was concluded before the next was begun,
the defendant was not tried in a single criminal action — **even where the second followed
immediately after the first.**
*Cite:* *Ex parte Pharr*, 897 S.W.2d at 796.
*Status:* **UNVERIFIED.** *Source:* as above.

**`C-HU-1` — *Pharr*'s definition governs art. 102.073(a).**
*Proposition:* The phrase "in a single criminal action" in art. 102.073(a) carries the construction
the Court of Criminal Appeals gave it under Penal Code § 3.03.
*Cite:* *Hurlburt v. State*, 506 S.W.3d 199, 201–03 (Tex. App.—Waco 2016).
*Status:* **UNVERIFIED.** *Source:* vLex Fastcase copy `VLEX-890991412`, read 2026-08-19; not
reporter-verified; no currency check run. **Intermediate appellate authority from the Tenth Court;
persuasive only in the Fourth.**

**`C-HU-2` — the criminal-episode limb does not carry over to costs.**
*Proposition:* Because art. 102.073(a) is not limited to offenses arising out of the same criminal
episode, that limb of the *Pharr* definition does not apply when the definition is applied to
art. 102.073(a).
*Cite:* *Hurlburt*, 506 S.W.3d at 202.
*Status:* **UNVERIFIED.** *Source:* as above. **Note on its face: reached on the appellant's
argument, with the court stating "We agree with Hurlburt" — not decided against opposition on this
limb.**

**`C-HU-3` — costs once, where offenses are heard at one time.**
*Proposition:* Where allegations and evidence of more than one offense are presented in a single
trial or plea proceeding, it is error to assess court costs in each conviction.
*Cite:* *Hurlburt*, 506 S.W.3d at 203–04.
*Status:* **UNVERIFIED.** *Source:* as above.

**NOT DRAFTED, DELIBERATELY:** the negative implication itself — *separate criminal actions mean
costs may be assessed per cause.* **Neither opinion holds it.** It is the inference entry 31 would
rest on, and **drafting it as a proposition would disguise an inference as a holding.** `Q-PH-2`.
