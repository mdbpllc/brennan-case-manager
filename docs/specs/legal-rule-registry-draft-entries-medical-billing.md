## ENTRY 1 — CPRC §41.0105 / Haygood v. De Escabedo: paid or incurred

**Cite:** Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §41.0105; Haygood v. De Escabedo, 356 S.W.3d 390 (Tex. 2011).

**(a) Core rule.** [READ — Haygood] Recovery of past medical expenses is limited to amounts "actually paid or incurred by or on behalf of the claimant" — expenses that have been or will be paid — excluding the difference between that amount and charges the provider bills but has no legal right to be paid. Haygood, 356 S.W.3d at 396–97. The statute operates "in addition to any other limitation under law." The rule prevents a windfall to the claimant (following Daughters of Charity, 226 S.W.3d 409 (Tex. 2007)).

**(b) Evidentiary bar.** [READ — Haygood; Henderson] The limitation governs the evidence at trial, not merely recovery: "only evidence of recoverable medical expenses is admissible at trial." Haygood at 399. Mechanics: charges the provider cannot collect are irrelevant to damages (TRE 402), and any residual relevance to noneconomic damages is substantially outweighed by likely confusion (TRE 403, Haygood at 398 & n.57). Post-verdict adjustment does not cure erroneous admission of unadjusted bills; the resulting verdict rests on legally insufficient evidence and requires new trial. Henderson v. Spann, 367 S.W.3d 301, 304–05 (Tex. App.—Amarillo 2012, pet. denied). Collateral source rule survives for the recoverable amounts: jury is not told about insurance coverage or that adjustments were insurance-driven. Haygood at 399–400.

**(c) Uninsured / LOP / factoring branch — full liability means full amount incurred.** [READ — Katy Springs; Primoris; and see Gallegos as cited therein] Where the claimant remains legally liable for the full billed amount — no adjustment has occurred and no contract prohibits the provider (or its assignee/factor) from collecting in full — the full billed amount is "incurred" and admissible; the discounted price a factoring company paid for the receivable is not the measure. Katy Springs & Mfg. v. Favalora, 476 S.W.3d 579, 601–03 (Tex. App.—Houston [14th Dist.] 2015, pet. denied); Primoris Energy Servs. Corp. v. Myers, 569 S.W.3d 745, 762–63 (Tex. App.—Houston [1st Dist.] 2018) (op. on reh'g; note: parties settled, original opinion withdrawn and this opinion issued in its stead — flag posture). An assignee stands in the assignor-provider's shoes.

**(c-1) TIMING.** [READ — Ahmed] "Incurred" is measured by the provider's legal right to be paid as of trial. A post-verdict, prejudgment lien reduction is not a debt reduction (debt merely undersecured) and does not reduce the judgment; applying it post-verdict would be the procedure Haygood rejected. Ahmed v. Sosa, 514 S.W.3d 894, 896 (Tex. App.—Fort Worth 2017).

**(c-2) STALE/UNPAID BILLS.** [READ — Sheppard] A hypothetical, waivable defense the patient could assert against the provider's collection claim (e.g., limitations on 4-year-old unpaid bills) does not convert into a §41.0105 evidentiary bar against the patient's tort recovery. Forth's limitations sentence is dictum. Court expressly protective of LOP-based treatment. Sheppard v. Martinez, No. 14-23-00473-CV, 2025 Tex. App. LEXIS 1266, at *17–22 (Tex. App.—Houston [14th Dist.] Feb. 27, 2025, mem. op.) [CONFIRM subsequent history — none shown in copy on file].

**(c-3) QUALIFIED-LOP CAVEAT — ATTORNEY RULING NEEDED.** [READ — K&L; JUDGMENT] In re K&L Auto Crushers: where the LOPs promised payment "only for any reasonable and necessary medical charges," the Court found nothing legally obligating the claimant to pay unreasonable amounts — and held that even if he were so obligated, tortfeasor liability is independently capped at a reasonable amount. 627 S.W.3d 239, 250–51 (Tex. 2021). Implication for our Kostura qualified-LOP template (which contains the same reasonableness condition): the client is arguably not "obligated" for amounts above reasonable, which (i) protects the client and the lien-clearance analysis, but (ii) gives the defense a K&L-grounded argument that billed amounts above reasonable were never "incurred" at all. **Michael to rule on how the registry states the incurred amount under a qualified LOP.** The billing module must not hard-code either answer meanwhile.

**(d) Scope limit.** [READ — Huntress] The Haygood framework does not govern a plaintiff's no-evidence summary-judgment burden when suing the provider itself for the provider's own tort; incurring any expense caused by the wrongful conduct raises a fact issue without proof of reasonableness/necessity or paid-vs-incurred amounts. Huntress v. Hickory Trail Hosp., L.P., No. 05-19-00892-CV, 2020 Tex. App. LEXIS 4112, at *19–21 (Tex. App.—Dallas May 29, 2020, pet. denied) (mem. op.).

**(e) Currency.** [NEG] No post-2011 Texas Supreme Court decision or 2025–2026 legislation altering the core holding or the statute's text. Consistent with everything in the collection through Jan. 2026, but requires citator pass on Haygood + current statute pull.

**Dropped from external draft:** McMillan v. Hearne, 584 S.W.3d 505 (Tex. App.—Texarkana 2019) was cited by the external response for (a) and (c). [READ] It is a nonsubscriber-employer offset case under Labor Code §406.033 (post-verdict equitable offset for plan payments allowed; no offset for bill-negotiation vendor fees; Haygood distinguished as inapposite). It discusses Haygood accurately but does not apply the paid-or-incurred measure and involves no LOP. Keep only, if at all, as: "discussing Haygood in the nonsubscriber offset context."

## ENTRY 2 — CPRC §18.001: affidavits and counter-affidavits

**Cite:** Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §18.001; In re Allstate Indem. Co., 622 S.W.3d 870 (Tex. 2021).

**Confirmed by collection:**
- [READ — Haygood; Gunn; K&L] §18.001 is "purely procedural," streamlining proof of reasonableness and necessity; it does not establish that billed charges are reasonable and expressly contemplates controversion. Haygood at 397–98; Gunn v. McCoy, 554 S.W.3d 645, 672–75 (Tex. 2018); K&L, 627 S.W.3d at 249–50 (§18.001's text confirms the common-law reasonableness requirement survives §41.0105).
- [READ — Gunn] Proper affiants are not limited to providers and their custodians; subrogation agents of health insurers who paid the expenses qualify under §18.001(c)(2)(B), and affidavits reflecting amounts actually paid are legally sufficient evidence of reasonableness and necessity. Gunn at 672–75. Amounts stated must be paid-or-incurred amounts, not billed amounts. Id. at 672.
- [READ — Katy Springs] "Person in charge of records" is disjunctive; a factoring company made records custodian by contract qualifies. 476 S.W.3d at 604–05.

**NOT verified by anything in hand:** [EXT]
- The consequences of a defendant's failure to serve a proper counter-affidavit (In re Allstate's procedural-not-evidentiary holding; whether the defendant may still contest reasonableness/necessity at trial with other evidence). **In re Allstate is not in the project collection.** Pull it.
- The full deadline table (90/120/30-day structure; (e-1) post-answer services rule; supplementation windows; notice-of-service filing mechanics). Verify directly against current §18.001 text (same method as the Ch. 146 pull).
- [NEG] SB 30 (89th Leg.) death in conference; no enacted successor.

## ENTRY 3 — Negotiated-rate & cost discovery: North Cypress / K&L line

**Cite:** In re N. Cypress Med. Ctr. Operating Co., 559 S.W.3d 128 (Tex. 2018) (substituted opinion — project PDF is the original 2018 Tex. LEXIS 346 slip; use 559 S.W.3d cite); In re K&L Auto Crushers, LLC, 627 S.W.3d 239 (Tex. 2021) (reh'g denied Sept. 3, 2021).

**(a) Core discovery rule.** [READ — North Cypress; K&L] When reasonableness of charges is contested, the following are relevant (not dispositive) and discoverable: the provider's negotiated reimbursement rates with private insurers; Medicare/Medicaid reimbursement rates for the services at issue; the provider's Medicare cost reports; the provider's costs for the services/devices billed; and chargemaster methodology. North Cypress (hospital-lien reasonableness, uninsured patient); K&L at 249–51 (extended to personal-injury suits: "The reasonableness of the claimant's medical expenses is as germane in a personal-injury case as it is in a suit to challenge the validity of a medical lien").

**(b) Doctrinal basis.** [READ — K&L] §41.0105's "in addition to any other limitation" preserves the independent common-law requirement that recoverable medical expenses be reasonable (Dallas Ry. v. Gossett, 294 S.W.2d 377 (Tex. 1956), and 19th-century line). Charged amount alone is not evidence of reasonableness. K&L at 249–50.

**(c) Reaches LOP providers; reduced non-party protection.** [READ — K&L] The discovery reaches Letter-of-Protection providers on the plaintiff's side; LOP providers have a direct financial stake in the case outcome and "forfeit a degree" of the protection afforded disinterested non-parties. K&L at 253–54.

**(d) Confidentiality → protective order, not denial.** [READ — North Cypress; K&L] Trade-secret/confidentiality objections are addressed by protective order; on a TUTSA trade-secret assertion, Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §134A.006(a) creates a presumption in favor of a protective order. Complete denial without considering that alternative is abuse of discretion. K&L at 255–56.

**(e) Proportionality; tailoring.** [READ — K&L; Central Oregon] Requests must be tailored to the same/similar services and devices at issue as of treatment dates; K&L-style narrowed requests approved; but relevance does not entitle a party to all tangentially related documents, and trial courts must run the Rule 192.4 balancing. In re Cent. Or. Truck Co., 644 S.W.3d 668, 669–70 (Tex. 2022) (per curiam) (near-total denial of billing discovery — expressly including adjustments, payments, payor identity, insurer contracts, and the LOPs themselves — was abuse of discretion; remanded for tailoring under K&L; also approving discovery of pre-accident medical/employment/insurance records on causation and earning capacity).

**(f) LIMIT — no counterfactual insurer discovery (Fourth Court).** [READ — In re United Healthcare] K&L does not authorize deposing the plaintiff's health insurer about the rates it would have paid on claims never submitted. Discovery targets the treating providers' actual rates; hypothetical reimbursement on unsubmitted claims is irrelevant. In re United Healthcare Ins. Co., 652 S.W.3d 458, 461–62 (Tex. App.—San Antonio 2022, orig. proceeding). (Defense had pleaded failure-to-mitigate for not using health insurance; court did not reach that theory's merits.) **This is our home appellate district.**

**(g) Extension trend — UCR/surprise-billing litigation.** [READ — Christus] Federal court (E.D. Tex., Sherman Div. — note: not W.D.) extends the relevance logic to compel discovery of hospital costs/billing methodology in Ch. 1467 "usual and customary rate" litigation, while acknowledging "reasonable" and "usual and customary" are distinct questions. Christus Santa Rosa Health Care Corp. v. UnitedHealthcare Ins. Co., 2026 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 353 (E.D. Tex. Jan. 5, 2026). Persuasive only; shows direction of travel.

**(h) Admissibility side (pre-K&L).** [READ — Primoris] Excluding a hospital billing director's testimony of ~400% markups and a pre-negotiated 40% factoring rate was harmful error — relevant to reasonableness even though the full billed amount was "incurred" and admissible. Primoris at 763–64. The two threads (full amount in; reasonableness attackable) operate simultaneously.

**(i) Currency.** [NEG] No post-2021 SCOTX narrowing. Consistent with the collection (Central Oregon extends; United Healthcare is an intermediate-court boundary, not a narrowing of the core rule); requires citator pass.

## ENTRY 5 — CPRC Ch. 146 (timely billing): bar, lien interaction, H.B. 4145

**Cite:** Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code ch. 146 (NOT Insurance Code — spec correction); H.B. 4145, 89th Leg., R.S., ch. 1092 (eff. Sept. 1, 2025).

**(a) What the statute actually is.** [STATUTE] A timely-billing statute, not an EOB-based lien ceiling. §146.002 deadlines: bill the patient/responsible person by the first day of the 11th month after services (a); plan issuers and government payors per contract/regulation or the same 11-month default ((b)–(c)). §146.003 bar: a violating provider "may not recover from the patient" amounts the patient would have been entitled to receive as plan payment/reimbursement or would not otherwise have owed had the provider billed timely; bar extends to family members otherwise responsible. **Spec's prior framing ("EOB patient-responsibility amount is the maximum permissible lien") is wrong as a description of Ch. 146** — EOB-as-ceiling concepts live in the surprise-billing statutes (Ins. Code Ch. 1467 / SB 1264 world), per external draft [EXT]. Spec-feedback item filed.

**(b) H.B. 4145 amendment.** [STATUTE] Sole change: new §146.002(c-1) — for services related to a PI claim where the patient is represented, the provider *may* satisfy the (a) deadline by billing the patient's attorney within the same 11-month window, unless the patient/responsible person directs otherwise; conforming (d)(1) date-of-billing amendment (mailing to attorney's address of record). Effective Sept. 1, 2025. **Open [EXT]:** the session law's applicability clause (services before vs. after effective date) — pull the enrolled bill.

**(c) Interaction with Haygood/§41.0105 — ATTORNEY RULING NEEDED.** [READ — Sheppard; JUDGMENT] Sheppard rejected converting a *waivable* limitations defense into a §41.0105 exclusion. §146.003 is materially different: a self-executing statutory bar ("may not recover"), plus §55.004(d)(5) carve-out from hospital liens [EXT]. A statutory bar fits Haygood's "no legal right to be paid" test far better than the argument Sheppard rejected — but **no court in our collection has held §146.003 operates as a Haygood exclusion.** Treat as open. Registry status: unverified proposition, argument-grade only.

**(d) Practical exposure window.** [JUDGMENT, derived] For services billed only to plaintiff's counsel (not the patient) *before* Sept. 1, 2025, a §146.002(a) violation argument exists once the 11-month mark passes; (c-1) closes this prospectively. Billing module: per-bill flag = (service date + billing route + 11-month clock + pre/post-9/1/2025). Estimates-not-adjudication disclaimer applies; flag is an issue-spotter only.

**(e) Coverage.** [STATUTE] "Health benefit plan" defined broadly: insurance policies, HMO evidences of coverage, MEWAs, workers' comp policies, and MVA policies to the extent of PIP/med-pay. §146.001(1). "Health care service provider" = state-licensed provider whose services may be paid under such a plan. §146.001(2). No disciplinary action for violations (§146.004).

## ENTRY 10 — NEW: Compelled medical examinations (TRCP 204.1 / Sherwin-Williams line)

**Cite:** Tex. R. Civ. P. 204.1; In re Sherwin-Williams Co., 668 S.W.3d 368 (Tex. 2023) [EXT — as characterized within Club Car; pull it]; In re Auburn Creek Ltd. P'ship, 655 S.W.3d 837 (Tex. 2022) [EXT — same]; In re H.E.B. Grocery Co., 492 S.W.3d 300 (Tex. 2016) [EXT — same]; In re Club Car, LLC, No. 02-25-00532-CV, 2025 Tex. App. LEXIS 9102 (Tex. App.—Fort Worth Nov. 25, 2025, orig. proceeding) (mem. op.) [READ].

**(a) Good-cause test.** [READ — Club Car, quoting Sherwin-Williams] Movant must show: (1) examination relevant to a condition in controversy and likely to lead to relevant evidence; (2) reasonable nexus between examination and the condition; (3) desired information not obtainable by less intrusive means.

**(b) "Records review suffices" argument fails.** [READ — Club Car] Serving an expert report on substantial claimed injuries puts them in controversy; defense doctors are not required to rely on plaintiff's doctor's records; requiring the defense expert to testify without examining the plaintiff creates a credibility disadvantage the supreme court has rejected. Conclusory-affidavit attacks on the defense doctors' need-to-examine statements rejected on modest affidavit language.

**(c) Trend.** [READ — Club Car; JUDGMENT] Second Court granting mandamus to compel exams in quick succession (Club Car; In re Progressive Cnty. Mut., Nov. 21, 2025). Expect exams to be compelled statewide post-Sherwin-Williams where damages experts are served on TBI/CRPS/neuropsych-probeable injuries.

**(d) Practice hook (case manager, not registry-computed):** On serving a damages expert report in a PI case, fire an IME-anticipation task: client counseling, proposed protocol, terms-and-conditions positions (scope, duration, recording, attendance) — the mandate leaves "reasonable and appropriate terms and conditions" to the trial court, so that is where the fight is.
