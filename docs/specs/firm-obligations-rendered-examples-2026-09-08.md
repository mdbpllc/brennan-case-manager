## §1 — What it is, and how to use it at the sitting

An interactive rendering of the module the SPEC describes, on a **fictional fixture firm**, under the SPEC's nineteen `FOD-` defaults — with the SHEET's eleven decisions as switches. Open it beside the sheet. The left rail lists DECISION 0–10 in the sheet's order (plus the held items and the findings); choosing one shows, on the right, **the sheet's own question text verbatim** (extracted by program from the sheet at HEAD — sha256 `c13c7727d407c640964fe353f04696a04efa5d5645d1b3ce1f8a44c1079156c4`), the starting points as radio switches, and "Look at" buttons that jump to the surface where the answer shows. The centre is the product window: `/cases` (the dashboard card beside the legal-watch card), `/firm/obligations` (the twelve-month register), and an **Outlook projection** of what the app would push. The fixture clock is a date control at the top ("Today"); four quick dates are provided, including the January pile-up the spec's `FOD-14` reasoning names.

- It runs in any browser from the single file; nothing leaves the page (no network, no data); the switches he sets and the DECISION 9 worksheet are remembered in that browser only.
- **It is not the product.** The hands-on items the spec §11 holds for the product (the card's wording and position, the register's shape, Outlook keep-vs-delete, a firm-wide calendar page, the leads) are rendered as DEFAULTS so the eleven decisions can be seen in context, and the mock says so where they appear; they are not put here (`CC-1(b)`).
- **Nothing in it is a fact about the firm.** Every date, every activation, every "firm fact" a template's condition names (payroll, a lease, a notary commission, which counties) is invented, and every screen says so in a banner. The templates, their kinds, leads, weights and source strings are the SPEC §7 catalog's, copied; the source strings are the spec's own cite-and-status strings and characterize nothing new (§6).

**The session shape the sheet asks for is unchanged:** one decision at a time, in the sheet's order, his answer written into the running ledger in his words within the exchange; options as a way of asking, never a menu (`CC-1(a)`); dates never asked. The findings in §4 attach to decisions and are put right after the decision they belong to, each as its own adopt / reject / edit.

## §2 — The fixture

Two registers, chosen from the header. **Fixture A — with a backlog** (the default; clock 2026-10-02): twenty-three templates activated with invented dates, chosen so that every rule kind, both weights and the per-period lapse are on screen at once — two hard items overdue (the 2025 practice-time report never filed, 352 days; the September trust reconciliation, 27 days), two routine ones overdue (a database restore test never run, the October heartbeat review), three lit inside their lead windows (MCLE at 59 days of a 90-day lead; malpractice at 49 of 60; the domain at 44 of 60), one routine item lit (the documents restore test, 7 of 14), and the rest pending — plus the notary commission (2028) and the assumed-name certificate (2031) beyond twelve months, and five rows created inactive (the IOLTA certification, the two credential rotations, DMARC, the BOI note). **Fixture B — everything current** (clock 2026-09-20): nine templates, nothing overdue and no hard item within fourteen days, so the dashboard card renders NOTHING (the `WorklistCard` precedent) while the register still shows the lit rows.

What the clock does: "lit", "overdue" and "pending" are DERIVED from the clock, the effective due date and the lead (SPEC §3.3, §4.3) — nothing flips them, exactly as the spec says nothing runs on a timer. Moving the clock to **Jan 20, 2027** shows the January pile-up — the W-2, the 1099-NEC and the property-tax payment all due January 31 (a Sunday, so `FOD-1`'s note shows), the county bar membership due January 1 already in the Overdue pin, and behind them whatever the fixture's backlog still holds — the crowding against which `FOD-14`'s fourteen-day card horizon was written; **Dec 28, 2026** shows the lease-notice window; **Oct 20, 2026** brings the Q3 941 inside its fourteen-day lead.

What the buttons do: **Done** opens the close form (date, note, where the proof is); **Not applicable…** appears only on `conditionalPerPeriod` rows and requires a reason (`FOD-18`); **Undo** appears on a row closed in this browser and deletes the untouched next occurrence (`FOD-7`); **Set the real due date…** is the `dueOnOverride`, and refuses to move an overdue occurrence later (`FOD-4`); **Edit lead / weight…** changes the lead and the weight and re-evaluates the open occurrence (`FOD-4`; rule edits — the date, the kind — are not rendered, §5); **Retire** closes nothing — the open occurrence stays in its group marked *retired — stays until done*, no next materializes when it is done, and the obligation then rests in the Inactive section with its history (`FOD-8`; **Un-retire** reverses it); a **one-time** obligation retires itself when done (§3.2); **Add obligation** opens the seed catalog, and **Activate…** takes his date and never guesses one (`FOD-9`). Every act writes a `review_log` line the row's expander shows (`FOD-6`).

## §4 — What building the mock exposed — fifteen findings, all PROPOSED, each his to adopt, reject or edit

Rendering a spec forces every "and then what?" into the open, and an adversarial read of the rendering forces the rest (`FOM-12`–`FOM-15`, §6). Each item below names what the SPEC says, what the mock showed, and a proposed wording; the ones marked *rendered* have a switch in the mock so both readings can be seen. None changes a ruling (`FO-1`, `FO-2`, `FO-3` stand as ruled); every one is a DEFAULT-level change to PROPOSED text. **Nothing below is applied to the spec by this packet** — an adopted item becomes an edit act in the ruling sitting's packet, cited by the finding's label.

**`FOM-1` — the collapse rule re-materializes too soon, and — completed early — the SAME date.** *(rendered)* SPEC §4.1: under `collapse` the next occurrence is *"the first rule date AFTER the completion date."* Read literally: the September 5 reconciliation done October 2 produces the October 5 occurrence — three days away and lit at once under its five-day lead; done EARLY on September 3 (early completion is allowed, `FOD-15`) it produces **September 5 again** — and an annual or anniversary row put under `collapse` by DECISION 2 option 3 re-materializes its own date the same way (the October 15 report done September 20 → October 15 again). Pinned by the harness (§6). **Proposed, two limbs:** *a cadence completion CLOSES THE PERIOD it falls in — the next occurrence is the first rule date in a LATER period* (the September-3 and October-2 completions both close their month; the next is October 5 and November 5 respectively); *a dated kind under collapse never re-materializes its own date — the next is the first rule date after the later of its due date and the completion.* `interval-from-completion` is unaffected (its next is completion + days by construction). Changes `FOD-5`'s wording and the `materializeNext` rule.

**`FOM-2` — "Conditional on" mixes activation facts with per-period lapse.** *(rendered)* SPEC §7 marks `conditionalPerPeriod` on four rows (`FOT-8`, `FOT-13`, `FOT-14`, `FOT-17`), while its *Conditional on* column carries both a one-time activation fact (*he holds a notary commission*) and a per-period one (*the firm owns income-producing tangible personal property on January 1*). The rendition (`FOT-10`), the property-tax payment (`FOT-11`), the 940 (`FOT-15`) and the TWC report (`FOT-16`) can each lapse for a period and offer no "not applicable". **Proposed:** mark `conditionalPerPeriod` explicitly on every row where the duty can lapse for a period, and split the column — *Applies if* (his activation fact) from *Can lapse per period* (the flag). Changes the catalog's flags, nothing else. The mock renders the spec's four as written; the switch adds the other four.

**`FOM-3` — a twelve-month view has no home for an occurrence beyond twelve months.** *(rendered)* SPEC §5.2 groups *"every active obligation's next occurrence … by month"* in *"a twelve-month view"*; the notary commission (four years), the assumed-name certificate (ten) and the guardianship certificate (two) fall outside every group once their next occurrence lies past the twelfth month — at activation, or after the first Done. **Proposed:** a **Later** group at the foot, Done still allowed there (`FOD-15`), with the date shown in full.

**`FOM-4` — activation cannot know a period missed BEFORE activation.** *(rendered in Activate…)* SPEC §4.1 states the first occurrence for `anniversary` kinds — *"the first due date on or after today"* — and is silent for `fixed-annual`, `fixed-quarterly` and `fixed-monthly`; the mock applies the same rule to them as its own default. Either way, a report never filed for 2025 and activated in September 2026 materializes the 2026 occurrence and never learns of 2025 — so the sheet's DECISION 2 example (*"finishing the 2025 practice-time report in 2027"*) and §4.1's (*"a report completed two years late"*) can only arise for a period missed AFTER activation. **Proposed (a new default, and the fixed kinds' first-occurrence rule stated):** *a filing kind's first occurrence is the first rule date on or after today; at activation a `serial` obligation takes an OPTIONAL "last period completed" date, and if given, the first occurrence is the period after it, possibly already overdue.* `FOD-9` is unchanged — the date is his.

**`FOM-5` — where inactive obligations live is unstated.** *(rendered)* Rows the catalog seeds INACTIVE (the IOLTA certification, the LegiScan and model-credential rotations, DMARC, the BOI note) and rows he retires (`FOD-8`) need a place on the page. **Proposed:** an **Inactive** section at the foot of the register, collapsed by default, each row with **Activate…** (taking his date); templates never added live only in the Add-obligation catalog and are not "inactive".

**`FOM-6` — month precision and the lead window interact, and the spec picks the 1st regardless.** *(rendered)* `FOD-17`: a `precision: month` occurrence *"lights on the 1st"*. For bar dues with a 45-day lead that is LATER than the lead would light it (June 1 against May 16); for a 5-day lead it is earlier. **Proposed:** *the occurrence lights at the EARLIER of the 1st of its month and due − lead* — imprecision about the day never shortens a lead.

**`FOM-7` — `periodLabel` is undefined for two kinds.** SPEC §3.3 gives examples for annual, quarterly, monthly and membership-year labels and none for `interval-from-completion` or `one-time`. **Proposed:** *"by <due date>"* for interval kinds; the due year for one-time.

**`FOM-8` — build-time verification, not a design change: the reminder beyond two weeks.** `FOD-10` relies on `isReminderOn` with the reminder at the lead window (30–180 days before an all-day event). Outlook's own reminder presets for events stop at two weeks; whether Graph honours a `reminderMinutesBeforeStart` that large on an all-day event is not established anywhere on the record. **Proposed:** the slice's tests prove it against live Graph before the lean is relied on; if it fails, the lit moment needs another carrier (the To Do route the sheet already names, or a second "lead" event). Nothing changes on the sheet.

**`FOM-9` — the spec fixes no order INSIDE the Overdue pin; the mock chose most-overdue-first.** *(rendered)* SPEC §4.3 has overdue items *"rendered first, in the warning colour … with the day count"* and §4.4 has `hard` above `routine` *"at equal due proximity"* — nothing orders the pin itself. The mock renders most-overdue-first with weight breaking ties, which puts a routine item 31 days overdue above a hard item 27 days overdue (the database restore test above the trust reconciliation in Fixture A). Put for his eye with DECISION 6; the alternative — hard first within the pin, then by days — is a switch in the mock. **Proposed:** state the pin's order in §4.3, whichever he prefers.

**`FOM-10` — clarification: a backlog under `serial` is cleared one Done at a time.** Stated obliquely — the sheet's *"and so on until he is current"*, §4.1's *"A missed period is never skipped silently"* — and implied by the one-open invariant (`FOD-5`): each Done materializes the next already-overdue period, so two years behind is two clicks. **Proposed:** one plain sentence in §4.1, so the walk does not read it as a defect.

**`FOM-11` — undo "within the same period" (§4.2) against `FOD-7`'s "while the next occurrence is untouched".** The second is the operable test and the one rendered — Undo appears only on the close whose materialized next is still untouched, and a serial filing done two years late and undone the next day is not "within the same period" under any reading. **Proposed:** drop the period phrase from §4.2.

**`FOM-12` — `FOD-1` exempts only the weekend; Monday prints "overdue" though a roll may make Monday the due day.** *(found by the audit; rendered)* `FOD-1`: *"no 'overdue' label printed on the weekend after a Saturday date — the display says a next-business-day rule may apply and is not computed."* Rendered as written, the Sunday after a Saturday due date reads *past its date · weekend* with the note; on Monday *"2 days overdue"* prints — itself a conclusion the display was told not to draw, if a next-business-day rule applies. **Proposed:** either the exemption runs through the next business day, or a weekend-dated occurrence reads *"past its date — a next-business-day rule may apply; not computed"* until done, and never "overdue".

**`FOM-13` — the card's horizon: "due within 14 days", or "lit and due within 14 days"?** *(found by the audit; rendered)* `FOD-14` says the card carries `hard` items *"overdue + due within 14 days"*; §4.3 says *"Nothing lit or overdue → the card renders NOTHING."* They disagree for a short-lead row: the trust reconciliation (lead 5) is "due within 14 days" for nine days before it lights. The mock renders the conjunctive reading — the card never shows what the register has not lit — and the switch renders `FOD-14` literally. **Proposed:** the conjunctive reading, written into `FOD-14`.

**`FOM-14` — the register's horizon is stated two ways.** *(found by the audit)* §4.3: *"The lead window is the register's horizon"*; §5.2: *"A twelve-month view, every active obligation's next occurrence grouped by month."* The mock renders §5.2 — every open occurrence, the pending ones in grey. **Proposed:** reword §4.3 — the register shows every open occurrence; lit and overdue are what it emphasizes.

**`FOM-15` — the owner column at the solo stage is stated two ways.** *(found by the audit)* §5.2 lists *"owner (only when it is not the firm)"* on every register line; §10 says the field *"changes NOTHING on screen"* at the solo stage. The mock follows §10 — no owner badge at the solo stage under any reading; badges appear only in the multi-user preview. **Proposed:** §5.2's parenthetical becomes *"only once more than one licensed user exists"*.
