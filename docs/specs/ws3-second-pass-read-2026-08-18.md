## §0 — The question this read was authorized to test

From the queue row and the #108 ruling: read *City of Dickinson*, *CSX*, *Baytown Nissan*, *Pope*
and *Jimenez* — **chiefly to test whether the Texas Supreme Court has touched the comment-9 limit
on TRCP 192.3(h).**

The limit at issue is the one the VERIFIED `WS-3` entry states: comment 9 to Rule 192 provides that

### 1.2 — But two findings changed what is available, and both are candidates

**(a) *City of Dickinson* does not touch comment 9 — it does something more useful.** It is the

to subsection **(e)**, not (h). That is the *structure* of the argument the `WS-3` entry makes about

### 2.1 — *In re City of Dickinson*, 568 S.W.3d 642 (Tex. 2019) (orig. proceeding)

- **Opinion ID 4368726 / cluster 4591473. Docket 17-0020. Filed 2019-02-15. Published.**
- **V-9 — IDENTIFIED.** The document's own face: `<opinion type="majority">` with the author line
  **"Justice Devine delivered the opinion of the Court."** No sub-opinion appeared in the document.
- **What it decides.** *"The issue in this original proceeding is whether a client, who testifies as
  an expert witness in the client's own case, waives the attorney-client privilege with respect to
  the client's expert testimony."* Held: **no** — the emails and affidavit drafts between Texas
  Windstorm's lawyer and its testifying-expert employee **remain privileged.**
- **DISPOSITION, stated precisely because this posture inverts easily.** The relator was **the City
  of Dickinson — the party SEEKING discovery.** The court of appeals had vacated the trial court's
  production orders; the Supreme Court held that court "did not abuse its discretion in vacating the
  trial court's discovery orders, which failed to recognize the attorney-client privilege," and the
  opinion's last line is **"The petition for writ of mandamus is denied."** The privilege holding and
  the disposition therefore run in opposite grammatical directions: **relief DENIED, privilege
  UPHELD.**
- **Why it matters here, precisely.** At **646** the Court reads the two subsections together:

  > "Texas Rule of Civil Procedure 192.3 addresses the scope of discovery in Texas. … **Subsection
  > (a) of the rule generally addresses the applicability of privileges**, stating that 'a party may
  > obtain discovery regarding any matter that is not privileged and is relevant to the subject
  > matter of the pending action.' *Id.* 192.3(a)."

  and then, same page:

  > "While subsection (e) provides that a party 'may discover' testifying-expert materials, **nothing
  > in its language permits such discovery when the materials are attorney-client privileged.** …
  > **To the contrary, subsection (a) confirms that, absent some specific provision otherwise, Rule
  > 192.3 does not require the disclosure of information that is attorney-client privileged.**
  > Because the rule does not specifically prohibit the use of the attorney-client privilege for
  > testifying-expert materials, if material is privileged it may be withheld."

  And at **648**, the asymmetry between the two privileges:

  > "**Unlike work product, which is expressly discoverable under Rule 192.5(c)(1), the rules do not
  > mention the discovery of attorney-client communications.** *See* TEX. R. CIV. P. 192.5(c)(1);
  > 192.3(e)(6)."

- **What it does NOT do, stated so the analogy is not overread.** It never mentions 192.3(h), a
  witness statement, or comment 9. **It is not a holding about witness statements.** Subsection (e)
  and subsection (h) are different subsections and (h) has its own comment; a court could
  distinguish them. The value here is the Supreme Court's stated *method* for reading 192.3(a)
  against a sibling subsection — **not a decided case on the entry's own question.**

### 2.4 — *In re Jimenez*, 4 S.W.3d 894 (Tex. App.—Houston [1st Dist.] 1999, orig. proceeding)

- **Opinion ID / cluster 2330887. Docket 01-99-00706-CV. Filed 1999-10-21. Published.**
- **V-9 — IDENTIFIED.** The document's own face: `<opinion type="majority">` with the author line
  **"TIM TAFT, Justice."**
- **The facts are the firm's own posture.** A car collision; the plaintiffs sued the driver, Srour;
  Srour **withheld his own recorded statement given to his insurance carrier**, asserting **work
  product** under Rule 193.3(a). The plaintiffs moved to compel under 194.2(f), 192.3(h), 192.5(c)(1)
  and 194.5. The trial court denied the motion.
- **The comment-9 passage — the reason this lead was worth reading.** At **896**, Srour argued
  comment 9 as a shield, quoting it in the same words the `WS-3` entry quotes, and adding that
  192.5(a)(2) defines work product to include a communication between a party and the party's
  representatives **"including the party's … insurers."** The court's response:

  > "Srour states that comment 9 is in contravention of rule 192.5(c) and implies that comment 9
  > trumps rule 192.5(c). **In circumstances like these, when the only privilege asserted is 'work
  > product'** and when rule 192.5(c) plainly states that a witness statement, even if made in
  > anticipation of litigation, is not work product protected from discovery, the Court rejects
  > Srour's argument."

  Holding, immediately following — **on 896 if that rendering's last marker holds, which it may not;
  see the pinpoint caveat above**:

  > "The Court concludes that Judge Chambers abused his discretion when he denied the Jimenezes'
  > motion to compel production of Srour's statement to his insurance carrier, despite the dictates
  > of rule 192.5(c)."

- **Why the opening clause matters, and how far it can honestly be pushed.** *Jimenez* is the
  closest thing in the set to authority **against** a comment-9 privilege limit. Its rejection is
  introduced by *"In circumstances like these, when the only privilege asserted is 'work product'
  and when…"* — **and the court nowhere says in terms that it is confining its holding.** Read at
  full width the case would be contrary authority; read as tied to those clauses it is a
  work-product-only holding that **leaves the attorney-client question open**, which is the space
  the `WS-3` entry occupies. **The narrower reading is the better one and it remains a reading, not
  a declaration** — which is why it is put to Michael rather than applied. **The `WS-3` entry
  currently records *Jimenez* only as applying 192.5(c)(1) and ordering production — true, and
  incomplete in the one direction that matters.**
- **And a caution that runs the other way, stated because it should be:** *Jimenez* is an
  **insured-statement-to-carrier** case in which production was **ordered**. The adopted `C-ARD-1`
  condition (*In re Arden*) protects an insured's statement to his carrier's adjuster **where the
  record establishes** the legal-defense-facilitation role. **The two are reconcilable, and the
  reconciling fact is which privilege was asserted and proved — not which way the case came out.**
  A build or a letter that treats "statement to carrier" as categorically protected would be wrong
  under *Jimenez*, and this read does not support that reading.
- **Two limits of this opinion, recorded rather than smoothed.** The witness statement itself **was
  never presented to the trial court and was never provided to the court of appeals** (its
  footnotes 4–5); and the opinion expressly says it is **unknown whether the statement was under
  192.3(h)(1) or (h)(2)** and that "any such distinction is unimportant" — so *Jimenez* decides
  nothing about the (h)(1)/(h)(2) split that the `Q-COM-12` capture-vs-transcription question turns
  on.

**`Q-WS3P-1` — Do you adopt `C-DICK-1`?** *In re City of Dickinson*, 568 S.W.3d 642, 646 (Tex.
2019) holds that Rule 192.3(a)'s "not privileged" limit governs Rule 192.3(e)(6) absent a specific
provision otherwise — the same structural move the `WS-3` entry makes about 192.3(h), from the
Texas Supreme Court, about a sibling subsection. Adding it would give the derivation note a Supreme
Court source for its method while leaving its honest core intact. **Cost, stated first: the `WS-3`
entry is VERIFIED as of #110, and adopting any wording change DETACHES that verification and
requires a later re-verification look.** Adopt, reject, or edit.

**`Q-WS3P-2` — Do you adopt `C-DICK-2`?** The same opinion at 648: *"Unlike work product, which is
expressly discoverable under Rule 192.5(c)(1), the rules do not mention the discovery of
attorney-client communications."* It supports the entry's SCOPE boundary — the same boundary the
entry already draws, and the same rule its cross-reference points to. **Same detachment cost.**
Put separately from `Q-WS3P-1` because you may want one and not the other.

**`Q-WS3P-3` — Do you adopt `C-JIM-1`, and does it change how the entry cites *Jimenez*?** The entry
currently records *Jimenez* as applying 192.5(c)(1) and ordering production. It also **considered and rejected the comment-9 shield argument, in
terms tied to its posture — "In circumstances like these, when the only privilege asserted is
'work product' and when…"**. **Whether those clauses limit the holding or merely describe the facts
is a reading, not the court's declaration, and the candidate says so on its face.** Adopting this narrows the risk that *Jimenez* is later read as contrary
authority **and** cuts against over-reading `C-ARD-1` as a categorical "statement to carrier is
protected" rule. **Same detachment cost.** And the cite consequence, corrected here rather than
understated: the entry names *In re Jimenez* **in prose with no citation whatsoever**, so this
candidate would supply a **full citation**, not a pinpoint — a cite act, its own act under ROUTE-C,
needing its own word from you.

**`Q-WS3P-4` — Does `Q-WS3-5` now close, or does the second pass reopen it?** The #108 ruling closed
`Q-WS3-5` as a question and left the read as a pending act. **The act has now run and the answer is

that ride as a new row, or as an amendment to the existing one? **A session should not pick this.**

**`Q-WS3P-5` — Do you want *Baytown Nissan* read for the `C-XL-1` "representative of the client"
