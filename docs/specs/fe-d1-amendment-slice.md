## 6. THE PARAGRAPH SCHEMA

### 6.1 The general form (RULED — `RC-1`, "Option 1", "The four slot order matches", AS-Q8b)

A designation paragraph is an ORDERED SEQUENCE OF PIECES, each **APP** (placed from the record or from the fixed-sentence table) or **WRITER** (a returned part, opaque plain text). For a treating-type facility:

| # | Piece | Who | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| L | **LEAD** | APP | bold `{provider_name}, {credential},` from the provider record — the individual, or the group's names for a collective paragraph (AS-Q8b) |
| 1 | opening | WRITER | who they are, what they did for the client; for a mixed facility, what EACH did (§15.6) |
| 2 | **BASIS** | APP | the provider type's basis sentence (§6.4), singular or plural (`RC-3`) |
| 3 | middle | WRITER | what they will testify about; the damages elements; **future care and its reasonable cost** (AS-Q8a); **the custodian sentence, all four limbs, anywhere in the writer's parts before the causation line** (§11.6) |
| 4 | **CAUSATION** | APP | the provider type's causation sentence (§6.4), singular or plural — **always last** |

The app joins the pieces with single spaces into one paragraph (§10 D-2), escapes the writer's parts at the XML boundary, and applies the paragraph shell per §10 D-19. **The writer returns NAMED parts — `opening` and `middle` — as plain text (`HD-20-c`); it never returns a paragraph; the app never reads inside a part.**
