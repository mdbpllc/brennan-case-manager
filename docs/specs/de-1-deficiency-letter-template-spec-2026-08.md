## §0 — What this specifies, and the one rule that governs the whole document

**It specifies the SHAPE of two instruments** — a deficiency letter and a motion to compel — as a
template driven by the DE-1 taxonomy-as-data, and **the authority each slot draws on.** It does not
specify storage, rendering technology, UI, or workflow, all of which belong to DE-1's build scope,
which is not authorized.

**THE GOVERNING RULE, stated once and applied throughout:**

> **No slot in this template may render an authority whose registry Status is UNVERIFIED without the
> instrument saying so at the point of use.**

That is not a stylistic preference. Registry discipline is that automation flags and only Michael
verifies; a template that renders a proposition into a filing has, at that moment, made the
proposition operative. **The mechanism this spec proposes is that every authority slot carries its
registry entry number and its status as data, and the renderer refuses to emit an UNVERIFIED
authority into a filing draft without a visible flag.** §7 shows why that mechanism is load-bearing
rather than decorative: **the single highest-value category in the taxonomy currently has no verified
authority at all.**

## §3 — The corrected thicket-waiver argument, which is the reason this spec exists

Entry 5's **ruled DE-1 note**, adopted with the #95 wording:

> "the corrected waiver limb argues that **the thicket itself waives the objections buried in it** — a
> different argument from the one the prior wording implied; **the letter template should say so.**"

**What changed, stated so the template does not quietly keep running the old argument.**

- **The old argument (implied by the prior wording):** *these particular objections are unfounded,
  therefore these particular objections are waived.* That is an objection-by-objection attack. Every
  objection has to be knocked down on its own merits, and the responding party can save any objection
  by defending it.
- **The corrected argument, which is what 193.2(e) actually says:** *"An objection ... that is
  **obscured by numerous unfounded objections**, is waived unless the court excuses the waiver for
  good cause shown."* **The waiver operates on the objection that is buried — including an objection
  that might have been perfectly good standing alone.** The thicket is the operative fact. The
  responding party cannot rescue a valid objection by defending it individually if it is obscured by
  the unfounded ones around it.

**Why this is a different argument and not a rewording of the same one.** Under the corrected limb the
movant's showing is about the **response as a whole** — how many objections, how many unfounded, how
uniformly repeated — rather than about the merits of each objection. **It is easier to make and it
reaches further**, and a template built on the old argument leaves the reach unused.

**The case that runs it.** *De Anda v. Webster* is the clean exhibit, and its structure is the
template's structure:

> "Webster lodged the **same global, prophylactic string of objections** ... to every interrogatory
> and request for production."
> "In sum, **many of Webster's objections were unfounded**. We therefore conclude that **Webster
> waived his objections** to De Anda's discovery requests. See Tex. R. Civ. P. 193.2(e) ('An objection
> . . . that is obscured by numerous unfounded objections, is waived unless the court excuses the
> waiver for good cause shown.')."

Note the order of the court's own reasoning: **many unfounded → therefore waived**, as to the
requests generally. Not: *this objection fails, that objection fails*.

### 3.1 — The template section this produces

A **WAIVER** section that renders **once per instrument**, not once per item, and that is
data-driven rather than argued from scratch:

1. **The count**, from the item store: *N* requests served; the same objection string appears at *M*
   of them; *K* distinct objections per response.
2. **The uniformity showing**: the objection text is identical or near-identical across items — which
   the item store can demonstrate mechanically because objections are stored per item.
3. **The unfounded showing**: for a named subset, why each is unfounded — **which is where the
   per-category authority from §2 attaches**, and it is a subset, deliberately, because the argument
   does not require all of them.
4. **The conclusion**: under 193.2(e) the objections obscured by that thicket are waived, absent good
   cause the responding party has not shown.
5. **Good-cause anticipation**: 193.2(e)'s escape is *"unless the court excuses the waiver for good
   cause shown"* — the burden of which sits on the responding party. **The template should say so and
   not argue against a showing not yet made.**

**AND THE SECTION MUST NOT SWALLOW THE PER-ITEM GRID.** §4 explains why running the thicket argument
*instead of* the per-item analysis would be a defect, not an economy.

## §4 — Per-item structure, and the authority that makes it mandatory

**The movant has a duty too, and it is the one most easily forgotten in a document whose whole
premise is that the other side was sloppy.**

*In re Volt Power* (entry 15) — *"A party challenging discovery responses must explain each asserted
deficiency"* — with the *In re Park Cities Bank* pinpoint at **876–77**, corrected on Michael's supply
at E-3 (2026-08-17).

**So the instrument has two layers and needs both:**

| Layer | Renders | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| **Thicket / waiver** | Once per instrument, over the response as a whole | 193.2(e) (entry 5, **V**); *De Anda* (16, **U**) |
| **Per-item grid** | Once per deficient item | *Volt Power* (15, **U**) + *Park Cities Bank* 876–77 (14, **V**) |

**Running only the thicket argument would fail the movant's own duty.** Running only the per-item
grid throws away the corrected waiver limb. **They are complements, and the template renders both.**

### 4.1 — The per-item row

Each deficient item renders, in this order:

1. **Identification** — instrument type, request number, request text verbatim.
2. **What was served back** — objections verbatim, answer text verbatim. *(Verbatim matters: the
   thicket showing at §3.1 step 2 is only as good as the fidelity of the stored objection text.)*
3. **Category tag(s)** — one or more of the seven. Per-item, multi-valued.
4. **The deficiency, stated as a deficiency** — the sentence that satisfies *Volt Power*. **This is
   the slot the template cannot fully generate**: a category tag is not an explanation. The template
   supplies a category-specific skeleton; **the specific sentence is Michael's, and the renderer
   should mark an item whose explanation is still the skeleton.**
5. **Authority** — from §2, by category, **each carrying its registry entry number and status.**
6. **What is demanded** — the corrected response, and by when.
7. **CONTRA only — `evidence_refs[]`** resolving to produced-document identifiers. *(REQ-08, absorbed
   into DE-1. The IN-2 cross-link is named in that absorption and is not specified here.)*

**A note on ordering that is not cosmetic.** Requests should render in served order, not grouped by
category. A grid grouped by category reads as an argument; a grid in served order reads as a record,
and it is the form opposing counsel and the court can check against their own copy of the responses.
**Subject-matter clusters are a separate question — `Q3` of the REQ-02/DE-1 absorption, unruled.**

## §5 — The conference-certificate fuse

**TRCP 191.2**, entry 13, **as ADOPTED 2026-08-17 (ROUTE-C) and executed at the fifty-third
invocation**:

> "All discovery motions **or requests for hearings** relating to discovery must contain a certificate
> by the party filing the motion or request that a reasonable effort has been made to resolve the
> dispute without the necessity of court intervention **and the effort failed**."

**Two elements of the adopted wording drive the design, and both were adopted precisely because the
prior wording blurred them:**

1. **The certificate attaches to motions AND to requests for hearing.** A request for a hearing on a
   discovery dispute needs the certificate as much as the motion does. **The template must therefore
   attach the certificate to the request-for-hearing path, not only to the motion path.**
2. **"and the effort failed"** — the certificate asserts two facts, not one: that a reasonable effort
   was made, **and** that it did not work. **A certificate rendered before the conferral has actually
   failed is a false certificate.**

### 5.1 — What that means for the fuse

**The deficiency letter is the conferral.** The motion is what follows when it fails. So:

- The letter **must precede** the motion in the workflow, and the item store should carry the letter's
  service date and the response deadline it set.
- **The motion's certificate slot must not render until the letter's deadline has passed without a
  curing response** — that is the "and the effort failed" element becoming true. **A template that
  renders the certificate on demand is a template that will eventually render a false one.**
- Where the responding party cures **some** items, the certificate is still true as to the rest — but
  the motion should be re-scoped to the uncured items. **`Q6` of the DE-1 absorption (splitting one
  data set into multiple motions with a shared conferral certificate and exhibits) is unruled and
  bears directly here.**

**THE STANDING CONSTRAINT: escalation triggers are MANUAL per Q-6.** Nothing in this section proposes
an automatic clock, an automatic send, or an automatic motion. The fuse **gates** rendering; it does
not **fire** anything. DE-2 is the escalation timeline and is separately unbuilt.

**THE FLAG THAT SITS ON THIS WHOLE SECTION: entry 13 is UNVERIFIED.** Its wording was adopted and

## §6 — Instrument shape

### 6.1 — The deficiency letter

1. Caption / addressee / instruments and service dates at issue.
2. **Short doctrinal preamble** — TRCP 192.3(a) scope (entry 1, **V**); one sentence on 193.1's
   complete-response duty (entry 4, **V**). **Short. A letter that opens with three pages of law is a
   letter nobody reads.**
3. **The waiver section** (§3.1) where the response set supports it.
4. **The per-item grid** (§4.1).
5. **The demand and the deadline** — a date, not "promptly."
6. **The conferral offer** — the sentence that makes the letter a reasonable effort under 191.2, and
   the record of it.

### 6.2 — The motion to compel

1. Caption; the instruments; the conferral history, by date.
2. **Preamble**, including the framing authority: *Able Supply* (19b, **V**) — and *Castillo* (19a)
   **only once verified**, noting that as of this writing 19a carries text identical to 19b and its
   own ROUTE-C question (`RL-1`) is open.
3. **Waiver** (§3.1).
4. **Per-item grid** (§4.1), each item carrying its own authority.
5. **The evidence-burden section** — *Alford* at 181 (20a) and *Collins* (20b), **both UNVERIFIED**;
   the rule-side authority *Collins* itself cites, **TRCP 193.4(a) and 199.6**, is **in no registry
   entry** (`RL-4`).
6. **Anticipated response** — *Dillard* (22, **V**), **and it must render with its
   opposing-authority marking intact.** The entry says so in terms: *"Entered as opposing authority;
   nothing in the registry takes sides."* **A renderer that strips that marking turns the opponent's
   best case into ours.** This is the single most dangerous slot in the template.
7. **Prayer** — TRCP 215.1(d) (entry 11, **V**), which is quoted in full in the entry and includes the
   substantially-justified exception. **Where requests for admission are in issue**, TRCP 215.4(b)
   (entry 12, **U**) is a **separate** application on a **different** standard and belongs in its own
   paragraph, not folded into the 215.1(d) prayer.
8. **Certificate of conference** (§5), gated.

## §7 — Gaps in the authority floor — the part of this spec that matters most

**§2's status column is not decoration. Read down it.**

### 7.1 — **CONTRA — the highest-value category — has NO verified authority**

REQ-03 calls contradictions between an opponent's answers and its own production *"the highest-value
deficiencies."* Its authority in §2 is **entry 2 (U)** and **entry 12 (U)**, with entry 9 (V)
reaching only the admissions limb. **Both of the entries that carry the category are in Group A —
adopted wording, executed, awaiting one look each.**

**Consequence, stated plainly: a template shipped today would render its strongest category on
unverified authority.** Under §0's governing rule it would render it flagged — which is correct
behaviour and is also a poor look in a letter to opposing counsel. **The cheap fix is not a design
change. It is verifying entries 2 and 12**, which is one look each and needs nobody but Michael.

### 7.2 — The thicket-waiver argument's CASE half is unverified

The rule half — **193.2(e), entry 5 — is VERIFIED**, and it is the linchpin. The case that runs the
corrected argument, **De Anda (entry 16), is UNVERIFIED**, though T-21 confirmed its "pet. denied"
history, its memorandum designation and its holding. **The argument can be made on the rule alone.
The template should be built so it renders on the rule and adds the case when the case verifies** —
not so that it depends on the case.

### 7.3 — The per-item duty rests half on an unverified entry

*Volt Power* (15) is **U**; the *Park Cities Bank* pinpoint at **876–77** (14) is **V**. **Same
posture as §7.2: build on the verified half.**

### 7.4 — Two rules the argument uses are in no registry entry

**TRCP 193.4(a) and 199.6**, which *Collins* cites for the evidence-burden rule (`RL-4`), and — from
the T-22 pass — **TRCP 194.1(a), 194.2(9) and 194.5**, the disclosure machinery (`WS-2`). **Neither
set may be cited by a template that draws its authority from the registry, because neither is in it.**
Flagged; **no entry is created here.**

### 7.7 — **`DE1-SPEC-1` · The question this spec puts to Michael**

> **Should the template's authority slots be data — each carrying a registry entry number and its
> verification status, with the renderer refusing to emit an UNVERIFIED authority into a filing draft
> without a visible flag — or should the template hold authority as text, with verification enforced
> by practice rather than by the tool?**

The first is proposed at §0 and assumed throughout. It has a cost: **it couples the drafting tool to
the registry's status lines, so a template's output changes when a Status line changes.** That is the
point, and it is also a commitment. **The second is simpler and puts the whole burden on the drafter
at the moment of drafting — which is the moment the record shows things get missed.** *(Michael's
choice. Nothing is built either way.)*
