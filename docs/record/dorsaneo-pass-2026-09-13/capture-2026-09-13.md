### 4.2 Four corrections, each carried to the operative rule text
1. **The 50-day discovery-response branch is FAMILY-CODE-ONLY and says so inside the rule.** All four request instruments — **196.2(a), 196.7(c)(1), 197.2(a), 198.2(a)** — carry *"except that a defendant **in a suit governed by the Family Code**…"*. **Rule 194** is titled *"REQUIRED DISCLOSURES IN SUITS NOT GOVERNED BY THE FAMILY CODE"* and has no 50-day branch; **Rule 194a** is *"REQUESTS FOR DISCLOSURE IN SUITS GOVERNED BY THE FAMILY CODE"* and its 194a.3 carries the branch without needing the qualifier. **BUILD-STATE's deadline memo is right; `docs/specs/trcp-deadline-skeleton-2026-03-01.md` §5 is the defect**, and its footnote — *"(Same structure also appears at 194a for Family Code suits — out of scope here.)"* — is the load-bearing error. **The skeleton also omits Rule 196.7 entirely** (0 occurrences). Practical consequence: a PI or civil-lit defendant served before answer has a **flat 30 days**, and the instrument where that matters most is requests for admission, where the default is automatic.
2. **TRCP 21a(c) adds three days for MAIL ONLY.** `P-2` stands as written. The doubt arose because **21a(b)(1)** *does* pair mail with commercial delivery — but that is the completion rule, not the added-days rule. Rule 4 says the same from the other side. Since almost all service is now electronic through the filing manager, the extension rarely applies at all.
3. **TRCP 190.3(b)(3) reads "any other party" — PAIRWISE.** `Q-FE5-9` confirmed against a **VERIFIED** entry whose wording says *"each party to 25 interrogatories."* ROUTE-C conforming wording drafted; **adoption would detach that entry's verification** (#95 sequencing).
4. **The VERIFIED Rule 194 entry carries no timing**, while **194.2(a)** — *"within 30 days after the filing of the first answer or general appearance"* — is the anchor of the entire discovery calendar: → 190.2(b)(1)(A)/190.3(b)(1)(A) period start → period end → 195.2's 90/60-day designations. Four rules, one chain, on a case event the app already stores. The entry also enumerates five content categories against the rule's twelve, and its own prose twice calls that "the four-category working list."
