## 1. Paid-or-incurred — the core rule

| Case | Cite | Court / Year | Proposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Haygood v. De Escabedo** | 356 S.W.3d 390 | Tex. 2011 | **The foundational case.** CPRC §41.0105's "actually paid or incurred" means expenses that have been or will be paid, and excludes the difference between that amount and charges the provider bills but has no right to collect. Only evidence of *recoverable* medical expenses is admissible at trial; the jury is not to be told a provider adjusted charges because of insurance. Post-verdict judicial adjustment of the medicals figure is improper — the limitation operates at the admissibility stage. |
| **Henderson v. Spann** | 367 S.W.3d 301 | Tex. App.—Amarillo 2012, pet. denied | Applies Haygood retroactively to a pending appeal. Admitting unadjusted bills that omitted ~$54K in workers'-comp write-offs was harmful error; the trial court's post-verdict reduction did not cure it. Reversed and remanded for new trial. Use for: post-verdict correction is not a fix. |
| **Ahmed v. Sosa** | 514 S.W.3d 894 | Tex. App.—Fort Worth 2017, no pet. | A provider's **post-verdict, prejudgment** agreement to reduce its lien does **not** reduce the judgment for past medical expenses. §41.0105 is measured at the time of trial, not by later lien negotiation. Directly useful on the plaintiff side when a defendant moves for remittitur after a lien reduction. |

## 2. §18.001 affidavits and counter-affidavits

| Case | Cite | Court / Year | Proposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gunn v. McCoy** | 554 S.W.3d 645 | Tex. 2018 | §18.001 affidavits served by a **subrogation agent / payor** — not just the provider or its records custodian — are proper and constitute legally sufficient evidence of reasonableness and necessity of past medicals. Rejects a two-affidavit requirement as a "complicated trap" contrary to legislative intent. §18.001 remains "purely procedural" and controvertible by counter-affidavit. |
| **Katy Springs & Mfg. v. Favalora** | 476 S.W.3d 579 | Tex. App.—Houston [14th] 2015, pet. denied | §18.001(c) is **disjunctive**: the affiant may be "the person who provided the service" *or* "the person in charge of records." A third-party billing company that is contractually the records custodian (here, MedStar) qualifies. Also: duplicative affidavits covering the same charges were harmless where the verdict did not turn on them. Also reaches a struck counter-affidavit. |
| **McMillan v. Hearne** | 584 S.W.3d 505 | Tex. App.—Texarkana 2019, no pet. | Workers'-comp **nonsubscriber** case. Employer-funded benefit-plan payments are not a collateral source as to that employer; the employer is entitled to an equitable offset. Offset is a **legal** issue properly determined post-verdict by the court and is not waived by failing to submit a jury question. Distinguishes Haygood (different statute, different legislative intent). |

## 3. Discovery of provider rates and billing practices

| Case | Cite | Court / Year | Proposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **In re N. Cypress Med. Ctr. Operating Co.** | ⚠️ see note | Tex. 2018 | Negotiated rates a hospital accepts from private insurers and public-entity payors are **relevant and discoverable** on the reasonableness of the chargemaster rates billed to an uninsured patient and secured by a hospital lien. **⚠️ The PDF in this collection is the withdrawn 2018 Tex. LEXIS 346 opinion. The operative substituted opinion is 559 S.W.3d 128 (Tex. 2018). Cite the substituted version; re-pull the PDF.** |
| **In re K&L Auto Crushers, LLC** | 627 S.W.3d 239 | Tex. 2021 | Extends N. Cypress into the **personal-injury defense** context. Provider negotiated rates and costs are relevant to reasonableness and discoverable, subject to a proportionality standard requiring case-by-case balancing. Trial court abused its discretion by denying outright the defendants' *narrowed* requests. The controlling case for defense billing discovery. |
| **In re Cent. Or. Truck Co.** | 644 S.W.3d 668 | Tex. 2022 (per curiam) | Medical-billing discovery nearly identical to K&L's is relevant. Mandamus **denied without prejudice** so the parties could confer and the trial court reconsider in light of K&L (decided while the petition was pending). Use for: procedural posture / how to tee up reconsideration rather than a holding on the merits. |
| **In re United Healthcare Ins. Co.** | 652 S.W.3d 458 | Tex. App.—**San Antonio** 2022, orig. proceeding | Trial court abused its discretion in refusing to quash the deposition of a **nonparty health insurer's** corporate rep sought to establish maximum contractual reimbursement rates, where the plaintiff submitted only one claim and otherwise treated on letters of protection. **4th Court of Appeals — controlling in Bexar County.** High-value for this practice's venue. |

## 4. Letters of protection, factoring, and assignment

| Case | Cite | Court / Year | Proposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primoris Energy Servs. Corp. v. Myers** | 569 S.W.3d 745 | Tex. App.—Houston [1st] 2018, no pet. (op. on reh'g) | **The medical-factoring case.** Haygood distinguished where the bills were assigned to a factoring company (MedFin) and no contract prevented the provider — or the assignee — from collecting the full value of services. Full billed charges were admissible. Central authority for LOP/factoring billing practice. |

## 5. Payor-side and coverage

| Case | Cite | Court / Year | Proposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Farmers Tex. Cty. Mut. Ins. Co. v. Beasley** | 598 S.W.3d 237 | Tex. 2020 | An insured lacked **standing** to sue his PIP insurer that paid the negotiated rate his health insurer actually paid providers rather than provider list rates — no actual or threatened harm. Suit dismissed for want of jurisdiction. Use for: PIP list-rate-vs-negotiated-rate claims. |
| **Christus Santa Rosa Health Care Corp. v. UnitedHealthcare Ins. Co.** | 2026 U.S. Dist. LEXIS 353 | E.D. Tex. (Sherman) Jan. 5, 2026 | Discovery ruling in an **out-of-network emergency care** "usual and customary rate" dispute under 28 TAC §§3.3708, 133.44. **Federal district court — persuasive only, not binding.** Recent; watch for further proceedings. |

## 6. Damages sufficiency / other

| Case | Cite | Court / Year | Proposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Press Energy Servs., LLC v. Ruiz** | 650 S.W.3d 23 | Tex. App.—El Paso 2021, pet. denied | Where the plaintiff **abandons** the claim for medical expenses and seeks only noneconomic damages, his medical bills are not admissible as a "benchmark" for noneconomic damages. Defense waiver arguments under old Rule 194.2(d) rejected. |
| **Sheppard v. Martinez** | 2025 Tex. App. LEXIS 1266 | Tex. App.—Houston [14th] Feb. 27, 2025 (mem. op.) | Legal-sufficiency review of past and future medical expense awards; future medicals require evidence of reasonable probability of future need. Affirmed in part, remittitur suggested. **⚠️ Memorandum opinion — confirm citability and precedential weight before relying on it.** |
| **Huntress v. Hickory Trail Hosp., L.P.** | 2020 Tex. App. LEXIS 4112 | Tex. App.—Dallas May 29, 2020, pet. denied (mem. op.) | Reversal of no-evidence summary judgment on **false imprisonment, DTPA unconscionability, and negligence** claims arising from psychiatric detention. **⚠️ This is not a billing case.** It sits in a billing-authority collection without an obvious connection. **FOR MICHAEL: confirm why this is here** — if it belongs to the record-clearing / mental-health-hold line of work it should be filed under that topic instead. |
